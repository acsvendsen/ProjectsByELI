### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By establishing clear subtitle placement rules based on confidence states, we can ensure that subtitles are always legible and unobtrusive while providing critical assistance when needed. This will help maintain the wearer's focus on the conversation and reduce cognitive load.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement modes: one for high-confidence text (e.g., names and faces) and another for lower-confidence text (e.g., background noise or less critical information). Implement a fallback mode that hides subtitles when they are below a certain confidence threshold to avoid distraction.

3. **Likely payoff**

   Clear and strategic subtitle placement will enhance the wearer's trust in the system, as the subtitles will be more likely to appear at times of highest utility without becoming distracting. This improvement will contribute significantly to the core attractors of real-time clarity and low-friction assistance.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving confidence display ensures that the wearer has a clear understanding of the system's certainty in showing subtitles or memory recall. This clarity builds trust and reduces cognitive load by providing real-time feedback on the reliability of the information.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple, consistent format for confidence objects that includes a numeric score, label, and short reason. For example, "score 0.84 + 'High' + 'Seen recently'; score 0.42 + 'Low' + 'Weak match'; score 0.63 + 'Medium' + 'Name heard once in introduction'."

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing clear and concise confidence information, the system can reduce misunderstandings and increase the wearer's trust in the subtitles and memory support. This will enhance overall user satisfaction and perceived reliability of the device.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a clear local memory threshold for face/name recall, we ensure that the system can efficiently manage memory while maintaining high trust in its recall accuracy. This helps in reducing the risk of corrupted or outdated information being presented to the wearer, thus strengthening the core mission of providing trustworthy and reliable assistance.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit for face/name memories on the phone, specifically limiting the number of entries that are kept fresh without needing cloud synchronization. For example, set a threshold of 100 recent face/name interactions to be cached locally with periodic reinforcement checks over time.

3. **Likely payoff**

   Implementing this memory cache policy will allow the system to handle frequent and dynamic interactions more efficiently while maintaining strong trust through timely updates. It reduces the need for constant cloud queries, thus saving battery and improving latency. Additionally, it ensures that the most relevant and recent memories are always available locally.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one local cache size limit of 100 entries for face/name memories on the phone, with periodic reinforcement checks to ensure accuracy without excessive resource usage.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Staying within a phone-first architecture for live subtitle processing ensures that V1 remains lightweight and socially acceptable, aligning with the attractor of real-time subtitle clarity. By keeping heavy processing on the phone, we avoid battery drain issues and ensure that subtitles remain clear and stable enough to be trusted during live conversations.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific task-boundary for live subtitle processing: **LiveSubtitleProcessing**. This involves ensuring that all live subtitle generation happens locally on the phone without fallback to cloud, maintaining low latency and preserving battery.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping live subtitle processing local, we ensure high-fidelity, real-time subtitles that do not compromise on clarity or stability due to network delays or cloud service interruptions. This enhances user trust in the assistive behavior provided by the glasses.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that subtitles are clearly prioritized over one-line prompts helps maintain the wearer's focus on crucial information during live interactions. This prevents visual overload and ensures that the wearer can read subtitles without getting distracted by less critical text, thereby maintaining their trust in the system.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a clear visual hierarchy rule that routes subtitles to the primary display area while relegating one-line prompts to secondary or marginal areas of the frame.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By clearly separating and prioritizing subtitles over one-line prompts, the wearer can trust the system more during conversations, reducing cognitive load and increasing overall comprehension. This clarity also helps in maintaining social acceptability by ensuring that critical information is not obscured or overwhelmed by less important text.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define a visual hierarchy rule that prioritizes subtitles over one-line prompts by displaying subtitles prominently and relegating one-line prompts to secondary areas of the frame.
