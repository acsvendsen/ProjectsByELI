### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring stable subtitle placement rules helps maintain consistent readability and trust in live conversations. Stable placement reduces visual distractions, allowing the wearer to focus on the conversation rather than struggling with subtitles that flicker or disappear.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a fixed position for subtitles at the bottom center of the screen, adjusting only slightly based on head movement but not dynamically responding to speaker changes or gaze direction. This minimizes distractions while keeping the text visible and consistent.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By locking subtitle placement in one location, we reduce cognitive load on the wearer, who can rely on a predictable visual anchor. This enhances overall trust in the system as subtitles remain stable and unobtrusive during conversations.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define the fixed position for subtitles as the bottom center of the screen.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   Improving the confidence display format enhances the wearer's trust in the system by clearly indicating the reliability of the information provided. This is crucial for maintaining credibility, especially during live interactions where accuracy and quick responses are paramount.

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   Define a concise confidence label format that includes a numeric score and a brief reason for the confidence level. For example, "Score 0.85: Recent mention" or "Score 0.60: Likely match".

3. **Likely payoff**
   By providing clear and understandable confidence indicators, users will be more likely to trust the system during live interactions. This can reduce cognitive load by helping them quickly identify reliable information and focus on high-confidence content.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a local cache size limit for face/name memory on the phone, we ensure that the system can quickly recall likely new faces and names without overloading the device's limited resources. This improves trust by providing fast responses while keeping the battery usage low.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache threshold of 10 most recently seen people for face/name memory on the phone.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will allow the system to quickly recall names and faces with high confidence, enhancing the wearer's understanding and reducing the need for slow cloud lookups or heavy local processing. The improved response time will help maintain trust in the assistant’s reliability.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define a local cache threshold of 10 most recently seen people for face/name memory on the phone.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining clear cloud boundaries for specific tasks such as memory archive sync, we can ensure that the system respects privacy while still providing useful assistance. This helps in maintaining a balance between the system's usefulness and respecting user privacy.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple cache eviction rule where old or less relevant memories are evicted from the local phone cache after a certain period, ensuring that only recent and frequently accessed memories remain available locally.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach will help in maintaining a clean and focused memory cache on the phone, reducing unnecessary data storage while still providing timely access to important information. It also ensures that privacy concerns are handled effectively by not keeping old or irrelevant data on the device.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.
