### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   
   By refining subtitle placement rules based on confidence levels, we can ensure that subtitles remain clear and useful without becoming distracting. This improves real-time subtitle clarity (attractor) and supports low-friction assistance by reducing visual noise.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a new subtitle placement rule that places subtitles at the bottom of the frame for high-confidence matches but moves them to the top center for medium or low-confidence matches, ensuring they remain visible yet unobtrusive.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This adjustment will enhance the wearer's trust in the subtitles by maintaining their visibility during critical moments while minimizing distraction during less critical times. It also aligns with the core attractor of real-time subtitle clarity and supports the low-friction assistance goal.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining the confidence display to provide a clear numeric score alongside a label and short reason, the system can enhance trust through transparent and consistent feedback. This approach ensures that the wearer understands the certainty of any information presented, which is crucial for maintaining the product's reliability in live interactions.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one confidence format candidate for V1: score + label + short reason. For example, "score 0.84 + 'High' + 'Seen recently';" this format provides a balance of clarity and conciseness, making the information easily digestible.

3. **Likely payoff**

   Implementing this format will likely lead to improved user trust in the product's accuracy, as users can quickly grasp the level of certainty behind any displayed information. This transparency is key for building long-term user confidence and reliability in the system.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a more conservative local memory threshold, we ensure that the system does not unnecessarily store low-value information while still maintaining robust support for frequently encountered people and names. This helps in preserving battery life and reducing thermal load, which are critical constraints.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   The small change is to set a local cache size limit of 10 face/name entries that are reinforced over time with repeated encounters, but no longer than one week since the last interaction.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will help in reducing the memory footprint and processing load on the phone while ensuring that frequently encountered people and names are reliably remembered for up to a week. It will also improve battery efficiency by limiting background syncing and local storage, thus making the system more resilient to thermal constraints.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one local cache size limit of 10 face/name entries that are reinforced over time with repeated encounters but no longer than one week since the last interaction.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear boundary for what runs phone-local versus cloud for live subtitle processing, we ensure that the most critical real-time data and computation stay close to the user's glasses. This reduces latency, improves reliability, and maintains the trust of the wearer by providing near-instant responses.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one task-specific local-cloud boundary where live subtitle processing is handled phone-first before falling back to cloud support if needed. For example, prioritize real-time speech-to-text transcription on the phone until the transcript can be sent to the cloud for refinement and additional context lookup.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will reduce latency, ensuring that subtitles appear almost instantaneously as they are spoken or heard. It also minimizes bandwidth usage, preserving battery life while maintaining high-quality subtitle support in real-world noisy environments where speed is critical.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one local-cloud boundary for live subtitle processing where the phone handles initial transcription and the cloud provides post-processing and context lookup if available.

   By defining a clear visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support, we ensure that the most critical information (subtitles) remains prominent while still allowing for useful contextual prompts without overwhelming the wearer.

It enhances both subtitle clarity and low-friction assistance, aligning well with the core attractors of real-time subtitle clarity and low-friction assistance.
