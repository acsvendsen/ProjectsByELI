### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining specific subtitle placement rules based on confidence levels, we can ensure that subtitles remain stable and unobtrusive while still providing crucial support when needed.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Introduce a simple rule: place subtitles at the bottom center of the frame for all high-confidence matches but keep low-confidence matches hidden until they become more certain.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will help maintain a clear and uncluttered visual field while ensuring that the most critical information is always visible, thereby improving both subtitle clarity and user trust.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

Improving the confidence display format enhances the trustworthiness of subtitle and memory assistance, ensuring that the wearer can accurately gauge the reliability of the information presented.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

Define a confidence format using numeric scores combined with clear labels and short reasons for each piece of information displayed. This will provide a more nuanced understanding of the certainty behind the data.

3. **Likely payoff**

By implementing a consistent, reliable confidence format, the system can better manage user trust in both subtitles and memory recall. Clear signals about the reliability of the information will reduce confusion and enhance the overall assistive experience.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the local cache size and eviction policy for face/name memory will enhance trust by ensuring that frequently encountered people are quickly recalled, while less relevant information is gracefully discarded to prevent cluttering the wearer's mental load.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache threshold of 10 recent face or name interactions that are reinforced over time, with an eviction policy that prioritizes keeping high-confidence matches longer than low-confidence ones.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By limiting the memory to the most relevant and recently encountered faces/names, the system will provide faster and more accurate recall during conversations, reducing the chance of incorrect or outdated information being displayed. This improves trust in the system's ability to assist accurately without overwhelming the wearer with unnecessary data.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.

3. **Likely payoff**

4. **Immediate next probe**

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that subtitles remain clear, fast, and stable enough to be trusted in live conversation directly supports the core attractor of real-time subtitle clarity. By optimizing subtitle placement rules for different confidence levels, the glasses can provide more reliable and less distracting assistance, reducing cognitive load and enhancing the wearer's understanding.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define 2 to 3 subtitle placement rules for different confidence states: high-confidence speech (e.g., clear speaker identity), medium-confidence speech (e.g., background noise or low-quality audio), and low-confidence speech (e.g., ambiguous or overlapping voices).

3. **Likely payoff**

   Implementing these subtitle placement rules will help the wearer better distinguish between different levels of certainty, reducing distraction and improving trust in the subtitles during live conversations. This refined visual hierarchy can lead to a more seamless and natural user experience.

4. **Immediate next probe**
