## Recent Project Signals Consolidation

### Strengthening Signals
1. **Subtitle Clarity**:
   - The importance of subtitle clarity remains high, ensuring that subtitles are clear, fast, and stable enough to be trusted in live conversations.
   
2. **Memory Trust**:
   - Memory support should continue to focus on remembering names, faces, and prior context without creating the illusion of certainty or causing recall issues.

3. **Low-Friction Assistance**:
   - The need for low-friction assistance remains strong, with one-line prompts that help the wearer without adding command overhead or social awkwardness.

4. **Core Deepening Mode**:
   - ELI should continue to focus on core improvements such as subtitle quality, memory trust, confidence display, and visual UX rather than broad feature expansion.

5. **Constraint Pressure**:
   - Early surface of constraints like battery, latency, privacy, and usability will help guide decisions that prevent wasting time on impossible or low-value directions.

6. **Frame-Touch-Only V1 Interaction**:
   - The interaction model should remain frame-touch only in the initial release, with no assumptions about voice commands or spoken control.

### Weakening Signals
1. **Automatic Memory Support vs Confidence and Correction Safety**:
   - The tension between providing automatic memory support and maintaining safety through clear confidence signals will continue to require careful balancing.

2. **Privacy vs Usefulness**:
   - There is ongoing pressure to ensure that the product remains useful while respecting privacy concerns, avoiding invasive behavior.

### Contradictions That Must Stay Alive
1. **Always-On Sensing vs Battery and Thermals**:
   - The need for always-on sensing to provide timely assistance must be weighed against battery management challenges.
   
2. **Discreet UX vs Visual Clarity**:
   - The product should strive for a balance between being socially acceptable (discreet) and providing clear, readable subtitles.

### Dormant Ideas Worth Reactivating
1. **Subtitle Placement and Visual Hierarchy**:
   - While not currently active, the need to find an optimal subtitle placement that is both usable and unobtrusive remains.
   
2. **Face/Name Memory Caching Strategy**:
   - The decision on how much face/name memory should be cached locally (on phone) versus relying on cloud for context needs further consideration.

### Concrete Tensions Around Subtitle Quality, Confidence Trust, Automatic New-Person Memory, Memory Reinforcement, Fast Lookup, Privacy, Latency, Battery, and Visual UX
1. **Subtitle Quality**:
   - Continuous effort to improve subtitle clarity and stability.
   
2. **Confidence Display**:
   - Clear representation of confidence levels using labels, short reasons, or scores without overwhelming the wearer.
   
3. **Automatic New-Person Memory**:
   - Ensure new faces and names are remembered with confidence objects and reinforcement over time.

4. **Memory Reinforcement**:
   - Implement mechanisms to reinforce memory for better recall in future interactions.

5. **Fast Lookup Requirements**:
   - Prioritize speed in memory lookups without sacrificing accuracy or privacy.
   
6. **Privacy Constraints**:
   - Respect user privacy by not storing unnecessary data and ensuring transparency about what is collected and why.

7. **Latency Management**:
   - Ensure real-time responses to maintain trust, even when using cloud processing.

8. **Battery Usage**:
   - Optimize power consumption while providing necessary functionality.
   
9. **Visual UX**:
   - Balance visual clarity with social acceptability in subtitle placement and overall display behavior.

### No Fluff
- Focus on core mission of discreet personal assistance through live subtitles, face/name recall, conversation memory, and one-line support.
- Continue to surface real-world constraints early to avoid wasted effort on impractical directions.
