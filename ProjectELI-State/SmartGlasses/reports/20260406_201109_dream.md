### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   Improving subtitle placement rules helps ensure that subtitles are always readable, stable, and in a position that does not distract from the conversation. This improves real-time clarity and trust, which aligns with our primary attractor of "Real-time subtitle clarity."

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   Define two subtitle placement modes: one for high-confidence states (where the system is very sure about who is speaking) and another for low-confidence states (where there is uncertainty or multiple speakers). This can be unlocked by adding a simple conditional rule that checks the confidence level of the speaker and adjusts the subtitle position accordingly.

3. **Likely payoff**
   By providing clear, stable subtitles in high-confidence states, users will have more trust in the system's accuracy. In low-confidence states, placing subtitles slightly lower or offset can prevent them from overlapping with important visual cues, thus reducing distractions and maintaining the natural flow of conversation.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving confidence display ensures that the wearer can trust the system's output, which is critical for maintaining real-time subtitle clarity and low-friction assistance. Clear and reliable confidence indicators help the wearer understand when they should pay extra attention to a name or face recognition.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a consistent format for confidence labels and short reasons that are easily understandable and unambiguous. For example, using a score 0-1 with label "High", "Medium", or "Low" along with a reason like "Seen recently" or "Weak match".

3. **Likely payoff**

   A well-defined confidence display will enhance the wearer's trust in the system, leading to better engagement and use of the glasses during critical interactions such as meetings or social gatherings. This can significantly reduce the cognitive load on the wearer by providing clear feedback about the reliability of each piece of information.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining clear boundaries for what runs on the phone versus the cloud, we ensure that critical processing happens locally where it can be most efficient and responsive. This improves real-time subtitle clarity, memory support, and overall low-friction assistance without overloading the phone's resources or compromising privacy.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one task-specific phone/cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing. For example, determine if the initial transcript cleanup happens on the phone and falls back to the cloud only when necessary, such as for complex language detection or advanced speech-to-text refinement.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This decision will ensure that subtitles remain clear and stable in real-time by keeping critical processing local. It will also allow the system to handle more demanding tasks off-device without breaking trust or adding unnecessary latency. Additionally, it will help in maintaining privacy by reducing data sent to cloud services.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one phone/cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing, prioritizing real-time clarity and stability over complex background processing.
