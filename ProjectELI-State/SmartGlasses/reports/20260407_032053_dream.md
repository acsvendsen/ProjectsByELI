### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

Stable subtitle placement rules in V1 will enhance the wearer's trust in the subtitles by ensuring they remain consistent and predictable, even as confidence in spoken content varies. This stability reduces visual distractions and improves the overall understanding experience.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

Define two subtitle placement rules: one for high-confidence speech and another for lower-confidence speech. For high confidence (e.g., 0.8+), place subtitles at a fixed, slightly above center position; for lower confidence (e.g., 0.6-0.79), use the same position but with a subtle fade-in effect to avoid sudden visual shifts.

3. **Likely payoff**

By providing stable placement rules, the wearer will experience more consistent and predictable subtitles that do not jitter or move erratically. This consistency enhances trust in the system's reliability, making it easier for the wearer to follow along during conversations.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining the confidence display to use a clear, concise format that avoids overconfidence and ensures the wearer understands the certainty level of each piece of memory or information provided, the system can maintain trust and prevent the wearer from relying on false certainty.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a confidence format that uses a numeric score (e.g., 0-1) with a corresponding label ("Very Low", "Low", "Medium", "High", "Very High") and a short reason explaining the basis for the confidence level (e.g., "Seen recently" or "Weak match").

3. **Likely payoff**

   A well-defined confidence format will help the wearer understand the reliability of memory and information provided, reducing misunderstandings and ensuring they are not misled by high-confidence claims that might be based on weak evidence.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a clear threshold for local memory storage, we ensure that only relevant and frequently encountered names and faces are cached on the phone, reducing the risk of privacy breaches while maintaining useful recall capabilities.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one fast-lookup index policy for people, names, topics, or recent context that prioritizes recently seen entities over less frequent ones, ensuring quick access to commonly encountered individuals and contexts.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will enable faster memory recall for frequently mentioned individuals and topics during conversations, enhancing the wearer's understanding without overwhelming their cognitive load with too many entries. It also reduces privacy risks by limiting local storage to the most relevant information.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set the cache size to store up to 10 recently seen people for quick memory recall.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining clear phone-local vs cloud boundaries for live subtitle processing, we ensure that subtitles are delivered in near-real-time, maintaining trust and clarity during live conversations. This reduces latency-induced errors and keeps the glasses lightweight while leveraging the phone's computing power where needed.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple cache eviction policy that prioritizes keeping recent and frequently used subtitle processing logic local to the phone. Only fallback to the cloud for specific edge cases, such as rare language requests or complex speech recognition tasks.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By caching common subtitle processing locally, we can achieve faster response times, reduce battery drain from constant cloud requests, and maintain a low-friction user experience that prioritizes real-time clarity over minor accuracy improvements.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the subtitle-vs-one-line priority rule ensures that subtitles remain clear, fast, and stable enough to support real-time understanding while minimizing visual overload. This helps maintain trust in the subtitles without overwhelming the wearer with too much information.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a visual hierarchy where subtitles are prioritized over one-line prompts when both are relevant but space is limited. For example, if only one line can be shown at a time, subtitles should always take precedence unless explicitly requested by the user through a frame touch.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By giving subtitles priority in the visual hierarchy, we ensure that critical information (subtitles) remains prominent and easily readable. This reduces cognitive load and enhances the wearer's ability to follow the conversation, thereby strengthening the core attractor of real-time subtitle clarity.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support where subtitles always take precedence in limited space scenarios unless explicitly requested by a frame touch.
