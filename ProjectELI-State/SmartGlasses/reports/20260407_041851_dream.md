### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining specific subtitle placement rules based on confidence states, we can ensure that subtitles are always clear and readable without being overly intrusive. This will help maintain trust in the system's ability to provide accurate real-time assistance.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple three-tier subtitle placement rule: 
   - High-confidence subtitles placed at the bottom of the frame.
   - Medium-confidence subtitles placed just above the bottom edge, slightly overlapping with lower confidence subtitles.
   - Low-confidence subtitles placed higher up in the frame, but still visible.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach will reduce visual clutter while ensuring that high-priority information is always easily readable. It will also reinforce the system's reliability by consistently placing key information where it can be seen and trusted, without overwhelming the wearer with too much text at once.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining the confidence display format to use a score with a label and short reason, we can provide more nuanced feedback that helps the wearer understand the reliability of the information. This approach builds trust through clear, concise indicators rather than overly narrative or ambiguous signals.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a new confidence object format: `score + label + short reason`, where:
   - Score: A numeric value between 0 and 1.
   - Label: A simple adjective like "High", "Medium", or "Low".
   - Short Reason: A brief, neutral explanation for the score.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will help users better understand when they can rely on the information provided by the system. Clear confidence indicators reduce cognitive load and increase trust in the system’s outputs, making it easier for wearers to follow along during conversations or remember important details from prior interactions.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support helps maintain a balance that enhances both subtitle clarity and low-friction assistance. By defining clear rules for when to prioritize subtitles over one-line prompts, or vice versa, we can ensure that the most critical information is always readable and understandable, reducing cognitive load and social friction.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a visual hierarchy rule that prefers subtitles over one-line support under conditions of high noise or low confidence in the one-line content. This could involve setting a priority order where subtitles are always shown first, followed by one-line prompts only when they are highly confident and relevant.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing this rule, we can ensure that critical information (subtitles) remains clear and readable even in noisy environments or when the system is unsure about providing useful one-line support. This will enhance the wearer's trust in the system and improve overall usability in real-world scenarios.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
