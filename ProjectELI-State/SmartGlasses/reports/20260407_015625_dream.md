### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving subtitle placement rules helps ensure that subtitles are always readable and stable enough to maintain trust during live conversations. This directly supports the primary attractor of real-time subtitle clarity, which is crucial for the wearer's understanding and confidence in noisy environments.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a fixed subtitle placement rule that ensures subtitles remain within the peripheral vision but are always visible when speaking or listening. For example, place subtitles at the bottom center of the field of view during speech to avoid blocking critical visual information.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By setting a fixed subtitle placement rule, we ensure that subtitles do not distract from the main focus of conversation while still being easily readable. This improves overall user trust and confidence in the product, aligning with our core attractors for real-time subtitle clarity and low-friction assistance.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

This improvement ensures that the system remains reliable and transparent, reinforcing its value as a daily-use assistant.

3. **Likely payoff**

   Implementing this confidence format will reduce cognitive load by providing quick, intuitive feedback on the reliability of subtitles and memory support. It will also help build trust through transparency, making the glasses feel more reliable and useful in real-world interactions.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a local cache size limit for name recall, we ensure that frequently encountered individuals are remembered with high confidence while minimizing memory overhead and preventing unnecessary data retention.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit of 50 entries for names and faces, ensuring that only the most recently seen individuals are stored on-device to maintain clarity and efficiency.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This will enhance the trustworthiness of name recall by keeping the memory relevant and reducing the risk of forgetting important people while also conserving battery resources and maintaining a low-friction user experience.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one local cache size limit for names and faces, with a maximum of 50 entries to balance memory efficiency and trustworthiness.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear boundary for what stays phone-local versus what falls back to cloud for live subtitle processing, we ensure that the primary real-time functionality remains stable and responsive. This focus on local processing reduces latency and ensures that subtitles are delivered with minimal delay, which is crucial for maintaining trust and usability in noisy environments.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   To unlock this benefit, we can define a specific task boundary: live subtitle processing. We will explicitly state that all subtitle-related tasks should be processed locally on the phone to ensure real-time performance and reliability.

3. **Likely payoff**

   Defining local subtitle processing as a clear rule will reduce latency and improve the overall user experience by ensuring that subtitles are delivered quickly and reliably, without depending on potentially slower cloud connections. This will enhance the trust and usability of the system in critical real-world scenarios.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one task-specific phone/cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing.
