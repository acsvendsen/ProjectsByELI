### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining specific subtitle placement rules for different confidence states, we can ensure that subtitles remain readable while minimizing distraction. This helps in maintaining the wearer's focus on the conversation without overwhelming them with too much information.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define three subtitle placement modes: fixed position (top of the screen), dynamic position based on head movement, and hidden until needed for low-confidence matches.

3. **Likely payoff**

   Implementing these rules will result in more reliable and less distracting subtitles, enhancing user trust and comfort during conversations. This can lead to better overall usage and a stronger core loop around real-time subtitle clarity.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

Improving confidence display ensures that the wearer can trust the system's assistance. Clear and accurate confidence information helps in maintaining the balance between being helpful and not overstepping, thus enhancing the overall user experience.

3. **Likely payoff**

4. **Immediate next probe**

Define one confidence format candidate for V1.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear local cache threshold for face/name memory, the system can ensure that common or recently encountered individuals are reliably remembered without excessive cloud usage, thereby balancing privacy and utility.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Introduce a fixed-size cache policy where each person-memory object is stored with a limited recency window, after which it expires locally unless reinforced by repeated interactions.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach will reduce the risk of storing unnecessary or outdated information while ensuring that key individuals are always remembered and available for fast lookup when needed.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear phone-cloud fallback boundary for memory archive sync, we ensure that critical personal memory is always available to the wearer in real-time, while non-critical data can be processed off-glasses to conserve battery and maintain privacy.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one specific task boundary where cloud processing will fall back to phone-local caching: memory archive sync for names, faces, and prior conversations.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach ensures that the wearer’s most critical personal memories are always accessible in real-time, enhancing trust and reliability while managing battery consumption effectively. It also respects privacy by keeping less sensitive data on the cloud, reducing data storage costs and potential exposure risks.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear precedence rule between subtitles and one-line support in the visual hierarchy, we ensure that the primary mission—real-time subtitle clarity—is not compromised by excessive visual elements. This helps maintain trust and readability during critical conversations.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple visual priority rule: prioritize subtitles over one-line prompts when both are present. If one line is busy displaying a complex subtitle, hide or suppress the one-line prompt to avoid visual clutter.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will reduce visual distraction during critical conversations, ensuring that subtitles remain clear and readable. It will also reinforce trust in the system by maintaining focus on the core assistive function without overwhelming the wearer with too much information.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one subtitle-vs-one-line priority rule for V1, prioritizing subtitles over one-line prompts when both are present.
