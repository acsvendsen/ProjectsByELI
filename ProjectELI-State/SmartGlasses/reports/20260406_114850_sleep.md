# Core Field

## Mission
Create smart glasses that act as a discreet, context-aware personal assistant focused on live subtitles, face/name recall, conversation memory, and one-line support to help the wearer understand and respond better in real-world interactions.

## Non-negotiables
- The glasses help the wearer; they do not pretend knowledge the wearer does not have.
- Early hardware remains simple: display text, record audio/visual input; phone/cloud handles heavy processing.
- V1 interaction is frame-touch only; do not assume voice activation or spoken command control.
- Context must persist across sessions.
- New faces and names should be remembered with confidence objects and reinforcement over time.
- Real-world constraints must be surfaced early: latency, battery, privacy, social acceptability, on-device limits.

## Core Attractors
- Real-time subtitle assistance in noisy environments
- Personal memory support for names and prior conversations
- One-line contextual prompts rather than overwhelming paragraphs
- Low-friction human experience; assistive, not deceptive

## Core Tensions
1. **Privacy vs Usefulness**
   - Balancing the need to remember and recall names and context without compromising privacy.
2. **Always-on Sensing vs Battery and Thermals**
   - Minimizing power consumption while maintaining always-on functionality for real-time subtitles.
3. **Local Processing vs Cloud Intelligence**
   - Deciding on the extent of local processing versus leveraging cloud resources for heavy computations.
4. **Rich Memory vs Fast Lookup**
   - Weighing the importance of having a rich memory bank against the need for fast and accurate lookups.
5. **Discreet UX vs Enough Visual Clarity**
   - Ensuring subtitles are clear enough to be useful without becoming too intrusive.

## Strengthen Signals
1. **Subtitle Quality and Placement**
   - Subtitles should remain unobtrusive but clear enough for the wearer to read quickly.
2. **Memory Recall and Confidence Objects**
   - Implementing a robust system to remember names and faces, with confidence levels to indicate reliability.
3. **One-line Support Clarity**
   - Ensuring one-line prompts are concise yet informative, providing just enough context without overwhelming the user.

## Weaken Signals
1. **Complex Interaction Patterns**
   - Avoid introducing complex interactions that could distract from the core functionality until V2 or later.
2. **Unnecessary Features**
   - Steer clear of adding features like advanced AR visuals or heavy autonomous behavior for now, to keep the system focused and usable.

## Contradictions That Must Stay Alive
1. **Balancing Real-Time vs. Battery Consumption**
   - Prioritizing real-time subtitle delivery over constant battery drain.
2. **Confidence Objects vs. Privacy Concerns**
   - Ensuring confidence objects are used judiciously to enhance trust without compromising privacy.

## Dormant Ideas Worth Reactivating
- Investigating the impact of different subtitle placement strategies (e.g., above, below, or to one side) on user experience.
- Exploring various confidence display formats (label only vs. label + reason vs. numeric score).

## Concrete Tensions Around Core Domains
1. **Subtitle Reasoning**
   - Ensuring subtitles are accurate and helpful without overwhelming the wearer.
2. **Memory Lookup**
   - Balancing the need for fast, reliable memory recall with the privacy implications of storing personal data.
3. **Visual UX**
   - Designing a visual hierarchy that ensures subtitles and one-line prompts do not become too distracting.

## Open Questions
- What is the best V1 split between glasses, phone, and cloud?
- How should face/name memory be stored and reinforced for fast lookup and trust?
- How should confidence be represented: numeric score, label, and short reason?
- How should subtitles and one-line prompts coexist without overloading the wearer?
- What is the best subtitle placement and visual hierarchy for fast reading with low distraction?

## Why It Matters
The system should reduce social and cognitive friction for the wearer, especially in noisy environments, hearing-challenged situations, memory-challenged situations, and dense multi-person contexts. It should help the wearer stay present, informed, and confident without pretending understanding.

## Evolution Rule
The project is allowed to evolve beyond its original form if that evolution strengthens the core mission, improves real-world usefulness, or increases trust, clarity, and assistive value. Expansion is good when it deepens the core, not when it distracts from it.

## Reality-check Rule
The system should continuously surface real-world limitations early, including hardware, battery, thermal, latency, privacy, and usability constraints, so the project does not waste time optimizing impossible or low-value directions.
