### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring stable subtitle placement rules prevents distractibility and improves trust in real-time subtitles. Stable placement reduces cognitive load by avoiding frequent, unexpected changes that could break the wearer's flow during conversations.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define 3 to 5 fixed subtitle placement rules for different confidence states: high-confidence (definite), medium-confidence (likely but not certain), and low-confidence (uncertain).

3. **Likely payoff**

   By establishing clear, stable placement rules, the system will become more predictable and trustworthy, reducing distractions during conversations and enhancing the wearer's ability to focus on the interaction.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

Improving the confidence display format helps ensure that the wearer can trust the system's memory and assistive behavior without overloading them with unnecessary details or creating a false sense of certainty.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

Define a simple, neutral confidence format: `score 0.85 + "High" + "Seen recently"` for high-confidence memories, `score 0.43 + "Low" + "Weak match"` for uncertain memories, and `score 0.62 + "Medium" + "Name heard once in introduction"` for moderately certain but less recent memories.

3. **Likely payoff**

This small change will enhance the wearer's trust in the system by clearly indicating the certainty of memory and context. It simplifies the display while providing enough information to aid decision-making, reducing uncertainty and improving overall user confidence.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that live subtitle processing remains phone-local can enhance real-time performance and reduce latency, which is crucial for maintaining trust and usability in noisy environments. By keeping this critical task on the phone, we ensure subtitles are as clear and fast as possible, aligning with the attractor of "Real-time subtitle clarity."

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific boundary where transcript cleanup (e.g., removing redundant words or improving readability) is handled locally by the phone instead of falling back to the cloud.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping live subtitle processing on the phone, we can ensure that subtitles are rendered in near-real-time, reducing lag and maintaining high confidence levels. This will help users trust the system more, especially in noisy environments where prompt and clarity are critical.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that subtitles remain clear, fast, and stable enough to be trusted in live conversation directly supports the primary attractor of real-time subtitle clarity. By prioritizing these qualities, we enhance the wearer's ability to follow conversations accurately without visual distractions.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a set of subtitle placement rules that prioritize readability over distraction. For example:
   
   - Subtitles should appear below the mouth region with consistent spacing.
   - Subtitles should be limited in length and speed, avoiding breaking into multiple lines unless necessary.
   - Subtitles should maintain a uniform font size and color scheme to ensure they are always legible.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing these subtitle placement rules, we can significantly reduce visual distractions while ensuring that subtitles remain clear and readable. This will help the wearer focus on the conversation without being overwhelmed by text, thereby increasing overall trust in the assistive feature.

4. **Immediate next probe**
