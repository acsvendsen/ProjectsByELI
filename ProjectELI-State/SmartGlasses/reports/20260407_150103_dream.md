### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving subtitle placement rules based on confidence levels ensures that subtitles are more usable without becoming distracting. This helps maintain trust in the system, as subtitles remain clear and readable when needed most.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement modes: one for low-confidence interactions (e.g., occasional mentions) where subtitles should be placed lower on the display to avoid distraction, and another for high-confidence interactions (e.g., frequent or important names) where subtitles can be placed higher for quicker reading.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing a clearer visual hierarchy based on confidence, the system can improve subtitle readability without overwhelming the wearer. This enhances trust in the assistive nature of the glasses and reduces cognitive load during high-stress interactions.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the confidence display format will enhance the trustworthiness of the system by clearly communicating the certainty of the information to the wearer. This clarity helps in maintaining a balance between providing useful assistance and avoiding overreliance on potentially incorrect data, thereby reducing social and cognitive friction.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple confidence format that combines a numeric score with a label and short reason. For example: "score 0.84 + 'High' + 'Seen recently'; score 0.42 + 'Low' + 'Weak match'; score 0.63 + 'Medium' + 'Name heard once in introduction'."

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will provide the wearer with a clear and concise way to understand the reliability of the system's output, thereby increasing trust and confidence. It also sets a consistent baseline for handling uncertainty, which is crucial for maintaining the core mission of not pretending understanding.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving local memory thresholds helps ensure that likely new-person memories are stored with sufficient confidence, enhancing trust in name recall and conversation continuity without overloading the phone's cloud resources.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit for face/name memory objects, setting a threshold of 5 to 10 recent interactions before moving older entries to the cloud for backup.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By limiting local storage to frequent interactions and offloading less frequently accessed memories to the cloud, this policy reduces memory footprint while ensuring that key new-person information is always available on-device with high confidence.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining specific phone/cloud boundaries for live subtitle processing, we ensure that critical real-time functionality runs on the phone to maintain low latency and high trustworthiness. This focus on real-time performance directly supports the attractor of "real-time subtitle clarity," ensuring that subtitles are clear, fast, and stable enough for the wearer's use.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

3. **Likely payoff**

It will help reduce latency and ensure that subtitles are reliable in critical live interactions, enhancing the wearer's trust in the system.

4. **Immediate next probe**
