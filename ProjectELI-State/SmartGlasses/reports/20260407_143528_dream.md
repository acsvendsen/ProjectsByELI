### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Stable subtitle placement rules improve the wearer's trust in the system by ensuring that subtitles remain in a predictable, unobtrusive position. This reduces cognitive load and helps maintain the wearer's focus on the conversation without distractions.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement modes: one for high-confidence content (e.g., well-known names or frequent speakers) and another for lower-confidence content (e.g., new faces or less frequently heard names). This ensures that critical information is always visible while reducing the visual noise from less important data.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing clear, consistent placement rules, the system will be more reliable and easier to use. The wearer can trust the subtitles to appear in a predictable location, which enhances their overall experience and reduces cognitive friction during conversations.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a local memory threshold for new-person recall, the system can ensure that likely new faces and names are remembered automatically with high confidence while still maintaining privacy by not continuously refreshing or syncing these memories across devices.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a fast-lookup index policy where likely new people are cached locally on the phone for quick recall, but only if they have been encountered multiple times within a short timeframe (e.g., 10 touches in the last hour).

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach will enhance memory trust by quickly associating new faces and names with high confidence objects, improving the wearer's ability to recall important people without creating unnecessary privacy risks or excessive cloud syncs.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear boundary for what runs phone-local versus falling back to cloud processing, we ensure that the system remains low-friction and reliable. This helps maintain the wearer's trust in the assistant by keeping critical functionality on-device where it can be trusted without internet connectivity issues.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific task boundary for face/name recall lookup. For example, set a rule such as: "Face/name recall lookups fall back to cloud only after 5 seconds of local cache miss."

3. **Likely payoff**

   This decision will ensure that critical memory tasks are handled quickly and reliably on the phone, reducing latency and improving trust. It also helps in managing battery usage by offloading less critical tasks to the cloud.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring a clear visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line prompts helps maintain focus on the most relevant information, reducing cognitive load and social awkwardness. This improves overall user experience by ensuring that the wearer can easily follow the conversation without being overwhelmed by additional text.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one visual priority rule that prioritizes subtitles over one-line support when confidence in both is high but one has higher contextual importance.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By clearly distinguishing between subtitles and one-line prompts, the wearer can focus on the most critical information without being distracted by less relevant text. This enhances the clarity of the live conversation and reduces the chance of missing important details.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
