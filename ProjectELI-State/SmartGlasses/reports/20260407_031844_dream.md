### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   
   Ensuring stable subtitle placement rules helps maintain the wearer's trust in real-time subtitle clarity, which is a primary attractor. Stable placement reduces distractions and ensures subtitles remain readable and useful throughout conversations.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement modes: one for high-confidence scenarios (e.g., well-known speakers or clear contexts) where subtitles can be placed lower on the screen to reduce visual intrusion, and another for low-confidence scenarios (e.g., new faces or noisy environments) where subtitles should be prominently displayed.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will enhance the wearer's trust in subtitle clarity by making them easier to read without being overly distracting. It also aligns with the project’s core of providing discreet, useful assistance.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining how confidence is displayed, we can ensure that the wearer always has clear and trustworthy indicators of the reliability of the information presented. This will enhance the core mission of providing helpful, real-time subtitles and memory support without overloading the user or creating false certainty.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Introduce a new confidence format that includes both a numeric score and a short reason for the confidence level. For example, "score 0.84 + 'High' + 'Seen recently'" for well-received names or faces, and "score 0.42 + 'Low' + 'Weak match'" for less certain matches.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will provide a clearer and more nuanced understanding of the reliability of each piece of information. It will help reduce cognitive load by avoiding overly complex labels while still conveying important details. The wearer can quickly grasp whether to trust the displayed information, which is crucial for maintaining confidence in the system.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By optimizing local memory thresholds for face/name recall, we can ensure that critical names and faces are stored in a way that supports fast lookup and trust-building without overwhelming the system's limited resources.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a cache size limit of 10 entries per person for face/name recall on the phone, with an additional layer of recency to prioritize recently encountered individuals over older ones.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach will maintain high memory trust by ensuring that key individuals are easily accessible and their presence is remembered accurately, while also conserving battery and thermal resources by limiting the amount of data stored locally.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one entity-linking rule for attaching new evidence to an existing person-memory object, prioritizing recency over frequency of occurrence.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Staying phone-first for live subtitle processing ensures that critical real-time performance is managed on a powerful device with access to more resources, reducing latency and ensuring that subtitles remain clear, stable, and trustworthy in live conversation.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define the live subtitle processing task as running entirely on the phone, with no fallback to cloud unless absolutely necessary for heavy load balancing or offline support.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping live subtitle processing local, we ensure that subtitles are delivered faster and more reliably, reducing the risk of lag or delay in real-time scenarios. This improves trust and the overall assistive value of the product in noisy environments where clarity is crucial.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one phone-local vs cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing only.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that subtitles are both clear and stable in V1 helps maintain the wearer's trust during live conversations, making the product more reliable and valuable. By prioritizing subtitle placement rules that minimize distraction while maximizing readability, we can keep the wearer engaged without overwhelming them with too much information.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define 2 to 3 subtitle placement rules for different confidence states: high confidence (e.g., from direct face-to-face interactions), medium confidence (e.g., from contextual clues like voice tone and context), and low confidence (e.g., from remote or less clear audio).

3. **Likely payoff**

   By establishing clear placement rules, we can reduce visual clutter while ensuring that important information is always visible. This will help maintain the wearer's trust in the system, as subtitles are consistently placed where they need to be without causing distraction.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
