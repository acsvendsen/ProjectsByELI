# Consolidation of Core Signals for SmartGlasses V1

## Strengthening Signals
- **Subtitle Quality**: The system must remain near-real-time to maintain trust, especially in noisy environments or hearing-challenged situations.
- **Memory Trust**: Confidence objects and explicit remember-touch behavior should be emphasized to ensure that the wearer understands the reliability of memory assistance.

## Weakening Signals
- **Complex Multimodal UI**: V1 interaction remains frame-touch only. Avoid introducing complex multimodal interactions like voice commands, gestures, or eye tracking until future iterations.
- **Overbuilding Hardware**: The current design direction focuses on a lightweight glasses model with minimal local compute and reliance on the phone for processing.

## Contradictions That Must Stay Alive
- **Real-time vs. Battery Trade-off**: While subtitles need to be near-real-time, this must not come at the cost of excessive battery drain or overheating.
- **Privacy vs. Utility**: The system should provide enough utility without compromising privacy or social acceptability.

## Dormant Ideas Worth Reactivating
- **Subtitle Placement and Visual Hierarchy**: Consider reactivating discussions on subtitle placement to ensure they are both readable and non-distracting. Explore visual hierarchy options that balance clarity with non-intrusiveness.

## Concrete Tensions Around Core Domains

### Subtitle Quality
- **Fast Latency vs. Battery Life**: Balancing the need for fast, high-quality subtitles with the constraints of battery life.
- **Visual Clarity vs. Distraction**: Ensuring subtitles are clear and readable without overwhelming the wearer or becoming distracting.

### Confidence Trust
- **Label + Reason vs. Numeric Score**: Deciding on a confidence format that builds trust without overwhelming the user. The current preference is for `Label + Reason` to provide clarity while remaining simple.
- **Explicit Touch Behavior**: Using explicit remember-touch behavior to reinforce memory trust and ensure the wearer understands when new information is being stored.

### Memory Policy
- **Recency First vs. Weighted Relevance**: Deciding on a caching policy that balances recency with relevance, ensuring recent interactions are prioritized but not at the expense of less frequent but important memories.
- **Memory Reinforcement**: Ensuring memory reinforcement through explicit remember-touch behavior and confidence objects to build trust in the system's accuracy.

### Fast Lookup
- **Local vs. Cloud Processing**: Deciding when cloud processing is necessary versus relying on local cache. The preference remains for phone-first architecture with cloud optional where justified.
- **Offline Functionality**: Ensuring key features work offline, particularly subtitle and memory recall functionalities, to maintain utility even in poor network conditions.

### Privacy
- **Data Storage and Sharing**: Balancing the need to store and share data for improving accuracy while respecting user privacy concerns. The system should minimize data collection and ensure that any data is handled securely.
- **User Control Over Data**: Providing users with control over their data, allowing them to opt-in or out of certain features and manage their privacy settings.

### Battery
- **Thermal Management**: Ensuring the glasses remain cool enough to prevent overheating while operating for extended periods. This involves optimizing power consumption and heat dissipation.
- **Low Power Subtitles**: Developing subtitles that are fast but do not drain the battery too quickly, maintaining a balance between utility and efficiency.

### Visual UX
- **Subtitle Placement**: Determining the optimal placement of subtitles to ensure they are visible without causing distraction. Current preference is for `More Prominent` subtitle placement.
- **Visual Hierarchy**: Ensuring that subtitles and one-line prompts have a clear visual hierarchy, with subtitles dominating to maintain readability but not overwhelm.

### Interaction
- **Frame Touch Interaction**: Maintaining frame-touch interaction as the primary method of user control in V1. Avoiding assumptions about voice commands or spoken interactions until future iterations.
- **Interaction Complexity**: Keeping interactions simple and low-friction to ensure usability without adding unnecessary complexity.
