### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring stable subtitle placement helps maintain the wearer's trust in the system by keeping subtitles clear and unobtrusive, which is crucial for real-time understanding during conversations. Stable placement also reduces cognitive load by minimizing distractions.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a fixed subtitle position that remains consistent across different confidence states, ensuring that subtitles are always visible but not overly distracting. For example, place subtitles slightly above the center of the field of view and adjust their size to be large enough for quick reading but small enough to avoid visual clutter.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By maintaining a fixed subtitle position, the system can ensure that subtitles remain stable and easily readable, thereby enhancing the wearer's ability to understand conversations in noisy environments. This consistency builds trust by making the experience more predictable and reliable.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining the confidence display format to provide a clear, concise, and unambiguous representation of certainty, the system can enhance trust in both subtitles and memory recall. A well-designed confidence format ensures that the wearer understands the reliability of the displayed information without feeling overwhelmed by too much detail.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Introduce a simple numeric score (e.g., 0-1) alongside a label and short reason for each confidence object. For example, "score 0.84 + 'High' + 'Seen recently';" this format provides immediate clarity on the reliability of the information.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will make it easier for the wearer to quickly assess the trustworthiness of subtitles and memory recall, reducing cognitive load and enhancing overall user confidence in the system. It also simplifies the interaction design by providing a standardized way to convey certainty levels without requiring complex visual elements or animations.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that memory support for names and faces is reinforced over time without continuous background syncing helps maintain a low-friction experience while keeping the system reliable. This approach balances privacy concerns with the need for consistent, trustworthy recall.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache eviction policy where new evidence (e.g., repeated face or name encounters) triggers reinforcement of existing memory objects without continuous background syncing.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By limiting background syncing to explicit user interactions or detected repeated context, the system can maintain strong memory recall while respecting privacy and battery constraints. This approach will help in creating a more stable and trusted memory system that relies on explicit user input for reinforcement.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory where new evidence triggers reinforcement of existing objects but continuous background syncing is minimized.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that live subtitle processing remains phone-local optimizes battery life and thermal management while maintaining real-time clarity and trust in noisy environments. This balance is crucial for the wearer's daily-use value, as lagging or less clear subtitles can break their trust.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local subtitle placement rule that prioritizes subtitle stability over dynamic adjustments based on confidence levels. For example, always place subtitles at the bottom of the screen, ensuring they do not overlap with other UI elements and remain visible in motion without requiring constant repositioning.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping subtitle placement stable, we can ensure that subtitles are always readable and not disrupted by gaze or head movements, which enhances the wearer's ability to follow conversations consistently. This stability will also reduce battery drain associated with frequent repositioning algorithms, thus extending wear time and improving user satisfaction.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring a clear visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support helps maintain the wearer's focus on the most critical information while reducing cognitive load. This balance enhances overall comprehension and reduces distraction, aligning with the core attractors of real-time subtitle clarity and low-friction assistance.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a rule that prioritizes subtitles over one-line prompts when both are active simultaneously. Specifically, this could be a visual hierarchy where subtitles have higher precedence in the display stack, ensuring they remain visible and readable even if one-line support text is shorter or less relevant at any given moment.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By giving subtitles priority, we ensure that the wearer's primary focus remains on understanding spoken words clearly. This reduces the risk of cognitive overload from too much visual information, thereby improving overall user experience and trust in the system.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one subtitle-vs-one-line priority rule where subtitles have higher precedence in the display stack when both are active simultaneously.
