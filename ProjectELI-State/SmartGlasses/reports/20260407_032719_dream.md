### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving subtitle placement rules can enhance the clarity and readability of subtitles, ensuring they remain stable and unobtrusive during live conversations. This improvement directly addresses the primary attractor of real-time subtitle clarity and supports the mission to provide discreet, reliable assistance.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two distinct subtitle placement modes: one for high-confidence scenarios where the speaker is known or easily identifiable (e.g., face matches are clear), and another for low-confidence scenarios where the speaker might be unknown or less recognizable (e.g., background noise or unclear faces).

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing these distinct placement rules, we can ensure that subtitles are more reliably visible in critical moments while reducing distraction during familiar interactions. This will enhance user trust and satisfaction with the system.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

   Improving the confidence display format ensures that the wearer can trust the subtitles and memory support more consistently, which is crucial for the core mission of reducing social and cognitive friction. A well-designed confidence system helps in making the assistant's outputs more reliable and less intrusive.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a consistent numeric score + label + short reason format for confidence objects. This small change will ensure that the wearer can quickly understand the reliability of subtitles and memory support without needing to interpret complex or vague labels.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing a clear confidence format, the system can reduce ambiguity and increase trust in its outputs. This will help in maintaining the core mission of making the wearer feel more informed and less anxious during conversations, thereby deepening the overall user experience.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring a robust memory cache policy that prioritizes local storage of frequently encountered names and faces helps maintain trust by reducing the risk of reliance on cloud connections, which can be unreliable or slow in real-world noisy environments.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple cache eviction rule for face/name memory that keeps the most recently seen people within a fixed size cache on the phone, ensuring fast lookup and reinforcement over time.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping frequently encountered names and faces locally cached, the system can quickly recall important individuals during conversations without the need for cloud connectivity, thus enhancing trust through reliable and timely memory support.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By optimizing the phone-cloud boundary for live subtitle processing, we ensure that the most critical real-time information is handled locally on the phone, reducing latency and maintaining the stability of subtitles crucial for trust in live conversations.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific task boundary where live subtitle processing remains local to the phone, with transcript cleanup and other less time-sensitive tasks falling back to cloud processing when necessary.

3. **Likely payoff**

   Reducing latency and ensuring real-time subtitles are clear and stable will significantly enhance the user's trust in the system during critical interactions, thereby improving daily-use value and core subtitle clarity attractor.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring a clear distinction between subtitles and one-line prompts in terms of visual hierarchy helps maintain the wearer's focus on essential information without overwhelming them with too much text. This balance is crucial for trust and usability, as overloading the wearer can break their concentration and reduce the effectiveness of real-time assistance.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific rule that prioritizes subtitles over one-line prompts when both are active in the display area simultaneously. For example, always show the most recent subtitle first, followed by any one-line prompts.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By clearly delineating what is essential (subtitles) from supplementary information (one-line prompts), this rule will help the wearer focus on key conversation elements without distraction. This improves overall clarity and trust in the assistive system's reliability.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support, prioritizing subtitles over one-line prompts when both are active.
