### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring consistent subtitle placement rules that are stable and unreactive to minor environmental changes will help maintain a clear, predictable reading experience for the wearer. This stability is crucial in noisy environments where quick shifts in subtitle position can disrupt understanding.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define fixed subtitle placement modes based on confidence levels: high-confidence text placed centrally with minimal movement, medium-confidence text slightly offset, and low-confidence text positioned at the bottom or periphery of the frame. This simple rule set ensures that critical information is always visible while less certain data remains unobtrusive.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By maintaining stable subtitle placement for high-confidence text, we reduce visual distractions during conversations, allowing the wearer to focus more on the primary speech without being disrupted by frequent adjustments in subtitle position. This will enhance trust and reliability of the system as a real-time assistant.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining the confidence display format to use a score + label + short reason, we can enhance the wearer's trust in the system by providing clear and concise certainty information. This will help manage expectations and reduce confusion, ensuring that the wearer understands the level of confidence behind each piece of displayed memory or support.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Update the confidence object to include a numeric score (e.g., 0.85), a label indicating the overall confidence level (e.g., "High"), and a short reason for that confidence (e.g., "Seen recently"). For example, the confidence display could show: **Score 0.85 + High + Seen recently**.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will provide the wearer with more granular and understandable certainty information, which can help in making better judgments about the accuracy of the displayed memory or support. It also aligns with the project's focus on honesty and trust, ensuring that the system does not overcommit to certainty when it is uncertain.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving local memory thresholds ensures that the glasses can quickly recall previously seen names and faces without overloading the phone's cloud resources or consuming too much battery on the glasses. This balance is crucial for maintaining a low-friction, always-ready experience that enhances real-time subtitle clarity and memory support.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local memory threshold for face and name recognition that limits the amount of recent context stored on the phone to only what has been seen in the last 10 minutes or so, with a maximum of 5-7 entities per person.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will reduce the need for constant cloud communication while ensuring that recently encountered people and names are quickly accessible when needed. It will help maintain a balance between memory richness and low latency, improving overall trust in the system's ability to recall relevant context accurately and efficiently.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one local cache size limit for face and name recognition, with a maximum of 5-7 entities per person and a refresh interval of no more than 10 minutes.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining clear rules for what stays phone-local versus falling back to cloud processing, we can ensure that V1 interactions remain fast and reliable. This will help maintain the real-time subtitle clarity attractor by reducing latency and ensuring that critical assistive behaviors are always available in a timely manner.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific rule for when live subtitle processing should fall back to cloud processing, focusing on scenarios where the local processing might struggle due to battery or thermal constraints. For example, if the local processor is below 70% battery and the environment is noisy, fall back to cloud.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By carefully balancing local and cloud processing, we can ensure that critical real-time subtitle assistance remains reliable even under resource-constrained conditions. This will help maintain trust in the system and improve the overall user experience by ensuring subtitles are always readable and timely.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one phone/cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing when local battery is below 70% and noise level is above 85 dB.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By optimizing the visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support, we can ensure that the most critical information is always easily visible without overwhelming the wearer's focus or causing distraction. This helps maintain a balance between providing enough context for understanding and not cluttering the display with too much information.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple subtitle-vs-one-line priority rule where subtitles are given higher precedence in the visual hierarchy, ensuring they remain prominent even when one-line support is displayed. For instance, if the wearer needs to read a subtitle, the system should temporarily suppress or visually de-emphasize any one-line prompts that might interfere with reading comprehension.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will enhance the wearer's ability to focus on critical information during live conversations, reducing cognitive load and improving overall understanding. It will also help maintain the low-friction design by minimizing distractions and ensuring that essential information remains clear and accessible.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
