### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring stable subtitle placement enhances the wearer's understanding by providing a consistent visual reference. This stability reduces cognitive load and ensures that subtitles remain within the wearer’s field of view, regardless of head movements or contextual changes.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement rules: one for high-confidence states (e.g., clear speaker, repeated words) and another for low-confidence states (e.g., background noise, unclear speech).

3. **Likely payoff**

   By having predefined rules for different confidence levels, the system can adapt its placement strategy without constant re-evaluation, leading to a more reliable and consistent user experience.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

4. **Immediate next probe**

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   - By setting a clear threshold for local caching of face/name memories, we can ensure that the system retains critical information while minimizing storage overhead and maintaining privacy.

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   - Define a recency-based eviction policy where recently seen faces and names are kept in local cache for faster lookup, but less recent entries are eventually evicted to make space.

3. **Likely payoff**
   - This approach will balance the need for fast memory recall with the constraints of limited local storage, ensuring that the most relevant information is always readily available while keeping the system lightweight and privacy-conscious.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.

1. **Why it benefits the core**

2. **What small change unlocks it**

3. **Likely payoff**
   - This boundary will ensure that subtitles are delivered in near-real-time, maintaining user trust and reducing battery usage. Simultaneously, it allows for more robust memory reinforcement without overloading the glasses' limited resources.

4. **Immediate next probe**
