### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Subtitle placement rules that prioritize stable, consistent display can enhance user trust in real-time subtitle clarity while reducing visual distractions. Stable subtitle placement ensures that subtitles remain on-screen without frequent jumping or disappearing, which is crucial for maintaining the wearer's focus during conversations.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a fixed subtitle placement mode where subtitles are always displayed at the bottom center of the screen, regardless of head movement or gaze direction. This simple rule ensures that subtitles do not move unpredictably and remain in a fixed location, making them easier to read.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By ensuring consistent subtitle placement, this change will reduce visual distractions for the wearer during conversations. Stable placement will allow the user to rely on the subtitles without worrying about their position changing unexpectedly, thereby increasing trust and usability.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define the fixed subtitle placement mode where subtitles are always displayed at the bottom center of the screen.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining the confidence display format to use a numeric score with a label and short reason, we can provide more nuanced and trustworthy feedback to the wearer. This improves trust by showing the degree of certainty in memory recall or subtitle accuracy, aligning closely with the core attractors of real-time subtitle clarity and memory support.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   The small change is to define a confidence format that uses a numeric score (e.g., 0.85), a label (e.g., "High"), and a short reason (e.g., "Seen recently"). This ensures that the confidence display is both informative and concise.

3. **Likely payoff**

   Implementing this confidence format will help build trust in the system’s memory and subtitle accuracy. Users can better understand when they should rely on the system's suggestions, leading to more confident interactions and a stronger overall experience.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a clear local memory threshold for name recall, we can ensure that new names are automatically remembered with confidence objects while maintaining a focus on subtitle quality and stable behavior. This helps in reinforcing trust through consistent and reliable memory support without overwhelming the local cache.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit of 10 face/name entries for automatic recall, with periodic reinforcement checks.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach will help in maintaining a balance between providing useful name recall and avoiding excessive memory load on the phone, thus preserving battery life and ensuring that new people are reliably remembered without cluttering the cache.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one local-cache size limit of 10 face/name entries for automatic recall with periodic reinforcement checks.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear boundary for what stays on the phone versus what falls back to the cloud for live subtitle processing, we ensure that the real-time subtitles remain stable and responsive. This helps maintain the wearer's trust in the system’s ability to provide accurate and timely information during critical moments of conversation.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Introduce a specific task boundary by setting a clear rule: all live subtitle processing must run locally on the phone, with no fallbacks to the cloud unless absolutely necessary for heavy processing tasks like complex speech-to-text conversion.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach ensures that subtitles are always available and stable in real-time, reducing latency and enhancing trust. It also allows for more aggressive optimizations on the phone, leading to a smoother user experience without sacrificing critical functionality.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support ensures that the wearer can focus on the most critical information without feeling overwhelmed by too many prompts. This helps maintain clarity, enhances trust in the system's ability to assist effectively, and reduces cognitive load.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a clear precedence rule where subtitles are prioritized over one-line support during high-confidence interactions. When the confidence is medium or low, one-line support can be suppressed or shown minimally to avoid distraction.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By ensuring that critical information (subtitles) remains at the forefront, the system will maintain a higher level of trust and usability. This clarity in design reduces cognitive overload, making it easier for the wearer to follow conversations without feeling bombarded by too much visual information.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
