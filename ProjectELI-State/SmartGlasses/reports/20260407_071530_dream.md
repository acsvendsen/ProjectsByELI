### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring stable subtitle placement enhances the wearer's trust in real-time subtitles, making them more reliable and less distracting. Stable placement rules reduce the cognitive load on the wearer by minimizing visual interruptions and ensuring that important information remains visible without sudden movements.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a default fixed position for subtitles near the bottom center of the field of view, which minimizes distractions while still being within clear sight during natural head movement.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By setting a consistent subtitle placement rule, V1 will deliver more predictable and reliable subtitle delivery, reducing the chance of missing important information and enhancing overall user trust. This stability also allows for faster visual scanning of subtitles without frequent repositioning, improving the wearer's comprehension during live interactions.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define the fixed position for subtitles as near the bottom center of the field of view.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

3. **Likely payoff**

4. **Immediate next probe**

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

Improving the memory cache policy helps ensure that frequently encountered faces and names are stored locally, reducing latency and improving the wearer's trust in face/name recall functionality. This directly supports the core attractor of "Trustworthy memory support" by ensuring that important context is readily available without relying on cloud access.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

Define a local cache threshold for frequently encountered faces and names to be stored on the phone, with an eviction policy based on recent encounters and user interaction. This simple rule will ensure that common entities are always quickly accessible.

3. **Likely payoff**

By setting a clear local cache threshold, we can significantly reduce the time required for face/name recall, improving the overall trustworthiness of memory support in noisy environments. This will enhance the wearer's confidence in the system, aligning with the core attractor "Trustworthy memory support."

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set the cache size to store up to 10 most recently encountered faces and names locally.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that subtitle processing stays local on the phone can reduce latency, maintain real-time quality, and preserve battery life while offloading heavy computation to a more powerful device. This helps in delivering reliable and timely subtitles, which are crucial for the wearer's understanding during live interactions.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific task boundary where subtitle processing should remain local: live subtitle rendering. This means that any subtitle-related processing, from initial transcription to final placement on the display, happens on the phone rather than being sent to or processed by the cloud.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping live subtitle processing local, we can ensure real-time clarity and stability of subtitles, which are essential for the wearer's trust and understanding during conversations. This local processing also helps in reducing battery consumption and thermal load on both the glasses and the phone, allowing more continuous use without interruptions.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear visual hierarchy rule between subtitles and one-line prompts, the system ensures that critical information is prioritized without overwhelming the wearer. This improves overall clarity and reduces cognitive load, enhancing the wearer's ability to focus on essential details during interactions.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a subtitle-vs-one-line priority rule where subtitles are given higher visual prominence when confidence in their accuracy is high, while one-line prompts remain subtle and non-intrusive.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This rule will ensure that the wearer can trust subtitles more easily during critical moments, leading to better understanding and reduced cognitive strain. Simultaneously, it maintains a low-friction experience for one-line prompts, ensuring they are helpful without being distracting.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
