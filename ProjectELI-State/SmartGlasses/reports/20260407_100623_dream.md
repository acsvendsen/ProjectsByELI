### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining specific subtitle placement rules that prioritize readability over distraction, we ensure the subtitles remain clear and stable enough for the wearer to trust them in live conversations. This improves real-time subtitle clarity, a primary attractor, by reducing visual clutter and maintaining focus on critical information.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two fixed subtitle placement modes: one for high-confidence situations (e.g., known speakers or familiar contexts) where subtitles can be placed centrally, and another for low-confidence situations (e.g., new faces or noisy environments) where subtitles should appear slightly above the bottom center to avoid distraction but remain readable.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This small change will enhance the wearer's trust in the system by ensuring that critical information is always visible and unobtrusive, thereby improving the overall user experience and reinforcing the core attractor of real-time subtitle clarity.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining the confidence display format, we can ensure that the wearer receives clear and reliable feedback about the certainty of the information provided by the system. This improves trust in real-time subtitles and memory support, aligning with the attractor "Trustworthy memory support" and ensuring that the wearer understands when the system is uncertain or has weak evidence.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a confidence format for V1 that uses both a numeric score and a label, accompanied by a short reason to explain why the system is certain about the information. For example: "score 0.84 + 'High' + 'Seen recently'; score 0.42 + 'Low' + 'Weak match'; score 0.63 + 'Medium' + 'Name heard once in introduction'. This simple format provides a clear indication of certainty and the basis for that certainty.

3. **Likely payoff**

   Implementing this confidence format will enhance user trust by providing transparent feedback on the system's reliability. It will help users distinguish between well-supported information and less certain data, reducing misunderstandings and ensuring they rely on the most relevant information available.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving memory cache retention strategies ensures that relevant names and faces are consistently available to the wearer, enhancing their trust in the system's reliability. By reinforcing recent encounters through a robust caching policy, we ensure that critical contextual information is readily accessible when needed, without overwhelming the user with unnecessary details.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache eviction rule based on recency and confidence scores. Specifically, prioritize retaining entities with high confidence scores and recent interactions over less relevant or older entries.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing a more intelligent caching strategy that prioritizes recently encountered and highly confident entities, the system will provide faster and more accurate memory recall. This improvement reduces latency in name and face recognition, thereby enhancing the overall trustworthiness of the memory support feature.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a specific phone-cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing, we ensure that subtitles remain reliable and real-time, enhancing trust and clarity during critical interactions. This balance helps to maintain the core mission of providing clear, stable, and useful subtitles without overloading the cloud, which can improve battery life and reduce latency.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a rule that keeps live subtitle processing local as long as there is no significant lag or performance degradation due to resource constraints. When such constraints are detected, fall back to the cloud for subtitle generation but prioritize the real-time experience by caching locally processed subtitles temporarily.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach ensures that subtitles remain clear and usable in critical moments, maintaining high trust and reducing the risk of battery drain or thermal issues caused by heavy cloud processing. It also helps in preserving the core attractor of "Real-time subtitle clarity" while balancing the constraints of local processing and cloud fallback.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.
