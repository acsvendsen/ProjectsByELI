### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining subtitle placement rules, we ensure that subtitles remain clear, stable, and unobtrusive during live conversations. This improvement enhances real-time subtitle clarity, which is a primary attractor for the project. Stable and well-placed subtitles contribute to the wearer's trust in the system’s ability to assist without distracting from the conversation.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a fixed subtitle placement rule that prioritizes readability over distraction. This rule will ensure that subtitles are positioned above or below speech bubbles, maintaining a clear visual hierarchy while minimizing interference with spoken dialogue.

3. **Likely payoff**

   The likely payoff is an improved user experience where the wearer can more easily follow conversations without being distracted by poorly placed subtitles. This enhancement will reinforce trust in the system’s ability to provide reliable real-time assistance, aligning closely with our core attractors of subtitle clarity and low-friction assistance.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining the confidence display format, we can enhance the trustworthiness of our system. A well-designed confidence label and short reason will help users understand when they should pay more attention to a piece of information or when it is safe to ignore minor details.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a clear, concise confidence format that includes both a numeric score and a qualitative label with a brief explanation. For example: `score 0.84 + "High" + "Seen recently"`.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This refined confidence display will allow users to better judge the reliability of incoming information, reducing cognitive load and increasing trust in the system. It will also help in distinguishing between important and less critical pieces of information.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving memory cache policies ensures that the system can quickly recall names and faces during conversations, enhancing the wearer's understanding and confidence without overburdening the phone or cloud with unnecessary data transfers.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit of 10 entries for recently seen people and entities, with an eviction policy that prioritizes more recent encounters to maintain trust in memory accuracy and response speed.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By limiting the cache size and using a recency-based eviction policy, the system will provide faster and more accurate name recall during conversations, reducing latency and ensuring that the wearer can rely on quick, correct responses without unnecessary data transfers or cloud delays.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that live subtitle processing remains phone-local until high-confidence matches are detected will maintain real-time clarity and stability, which are critical for trust and usability in noisy environments.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Modify the subtitle placement rules to route lower confidence subtitle text through the cloud only if a match is not found within a predefined threshold on the phone. For high-confidence matches, always display subtitles locally.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping low-confidence subtitle processing off the glasses and in the phone, we can ensure that subtitles remain clear and stable even when cloud processing introduces latency. This reduces the risk of breaking trust with the wearer during critical interactions.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line prompts, we ensure that the wearer can focus on essential information without feeling overwhelmed. This clarity supports the core attractor of low-friction assistance by making sure that critical real-time subtitles remain prominent while less important one-liners do not distract from understanding.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a priority rule where subtitles have higher visual precedence over one-line prompts, but both are clearly visible within the field of view.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By ensuring that subtitles remain the primary focus and that one-liners are secondary, we reduce cognitive load and social awkwardness. This improves the overall user experience by making sure critical information is always at the forefront without overwhelming the wearer with additional text.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support where subtitles have higher precedence but both remain visible within the field of view.
