### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring stable subtitle placement rules helps maintain a consistent, unobtrusive visual experience for the wearer. This stability reduces distractions and ensures that subtitles remain readable, which is crucial for real-time clarity and trust.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two different subtitle placement modes: one for high-confidence content (e.g., known names or frequently repeated phrases) and another for low-confidence content (e.g., new or rare occurrences). This can be unlocked by setting a confidence threshold that triggers the use of different placement rules.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing distinct placement rules, the system can avoid distracting the wearer with subtitles when they are less certain. This will enhance the overall user experience and maintain trust in the assistant's reliability.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   Enhancing the confidence display in V1 will strengthen the wearer's trust in the system by providing clear, consistent, and unambiguous feedback about the certainty of the memory and subtitles. This improves the overall reliability and user experience without overwhelming the wearer with complex or ambiguous information.

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   Define a simple but effective confidence format for V1 that uses a numeric score combined with a label and short reason. For example, "Score 0.84 + 'High' + 'Seen recently'" to indicate high certainty based on recent interaction history.

3. **Likely payoff**
   By implementing a clear and concise confidence display, the system will better manage user expectations and reduce cognitive load by providing just enough context without creating unnecessary complexity or uncertainty. This can lead to higher trust in the system's memory support and subtitle accuracy.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

This will help in maintaining an optimal user experience where common names and faces are quickly accessible without storing excessive information that could lead to privacy issues or memory clutter.

4. **Immediate next probe**

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By clearly defining what stays phone-local versus what falls back to cloud for live subtitle processing, we ensure that V1 focuses on delivering real-time, high-quality subtitles without overloading the battery or thermal capacity of the glasses. This improves trust and usability by ensuring that critical assistive behavior is not delayed or compromised.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific boundary rule stating that all live subtitle processing, including initial transcription and basic confidence assessment, should be done on the phone using TranscriptLab's transcript runtime. Only complex post-processing, such as advanced confidence refinement and memory updates, should fall back to cloud when necessary.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will ensure that V1 subtitles remain clear, fast, and stable, enhancing trust and user experience without draining battery or compromising thermal limits. It also sets a strong foundation for later optimizations by clearly separating what must be done on the phone versus in the cloud.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.
