### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining subtitle placement rules based on confidence states, we ensure that subtitles remain clear and unobtrusive while still providing essential support to the wearer. This approach helps maintain a balance between being helpful and not becoming distracting, thereby enhancing the overall user experience without compromising on clarity or stability.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define three specific subtitle placement rules for different confidence levels: low (hidden below one-line support), medium (placed above one-line but below the field of view's centerline), and high (always visible at the top of the field of view). These rules will ensure that subtitles are only displayed where they provide the most value without overwhelming the wearer.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This small change will lead to a more nuanced and effective subtitle display system, reducing visual clutter while ensuring critical information is always accessible. It will help maintain trust by keeping subtitles relevant and unobtrusive, which aligns with the core attractors of real-time clarity and low-friction assistance.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining the confidence display to use a clear label with a short reason for uncertainty, the system can better convey when subtitles or memory are less certain without overwhelming the wearer. This improves trust and understanding in noisy environments.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Introduce a new confidence format that includes a numeric score (e.g., 0.75), a label like "Medium" with a short reason such as "Seen once recently". For example, "score 0.75 + 'Medium' + 'Seen once recently'".

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will help the wearer better understand when to trust the subtitles and memory support more or less, leading to higher overall confidence in the system's accuracy.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear local memory threshold for face/name recall, the system can ensure that frequently encountered individuals are remembered with high confidence while minimizing unnecessary data storage on the phone. This balance helps in maintaining privacy and reducing battery usage by limiting continuous background syncs.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Introduce a local cache eviction policy that removes less frequently seen names from the memory index after a certain number of interactions, ensuring only recently encountered or important individuals remain cached for quick lookup.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will reduce the amount of data stored on the phone while maintaining high confidence in key interactions. It will help in managing battery usage by reducing continuous background processes and improve overall system performance without compromising on memory trust.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set a local cache eviction rule to remove names from the memory index after 5 consecutive frame touches or 30 minutes of inactivity.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear boundary for what runs locally on the phone versus when content falls back to the cloud, we ensure that critical real-time subtitle processing and face/name recall are handled efficiently on-device, reducing latency and preserving battery life. This balance improves trust and usability by providing timely and stable assistance.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific task boundary for live subtitle processing that stays phone-local and only falls back to the cloud under certain conditions, such as when the wearer is in a challenging environment or the system encounters an unusual situation.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This refined boundary will result in faster, more reliable subtitles during critical interactions, reducing delays and ensuring the wearer can trust the system’s real-time support. It also minimizes cloud usage for less critical tasks, extending battery life and maintaining a discreet UX.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that subtitles are placed in a way that is both stable and unobtrusive helps maintain the wearer's focus on the conversation, enhancing their understanding without creating visual distractions. A well-defined subtitle placement rule can improve trust by ensuring that critical information is always accessible and clear.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement rules: one for high-confidence text (likely to be accurate) and another for low-confidence text (potentially less reliable). This distinction allows the system to prioritize the most certain information while still providing additional context when needed.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By clearly separating high-confidence from low-confidence subtitles, the wearer can better trust the critical parts of the conversation without being overwhelmed by speculative or uncertain information. This clarity improves overall comprehension and reduces cognitive load.

4. **Immediate next probe**
