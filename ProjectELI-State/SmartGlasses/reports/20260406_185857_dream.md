### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring stable subtitle placement enhances the wearer's trust in the system by making the subtitles more predictable and less distracting. This stability allows the wearer to focus on the conversation without constantly adjusting their gaze, which can disrupt natural interactions.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a fixed subtitle placement rule that keeps subtitles at the bottom center of the field of view, regardless of head or frame orientation changes. This rule ensures that the subtitles remain easily readable and do not move unpredictably, providing a consistent reading experience.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By maintaining stable subtitle placement, users will have a more reliable and less distracting experience, leading to higher trust in the system's ability to assist effectively during conversations. This stability also reduces cognitive load for the wearer, allowing them to focus on the conversation rather than constantly adjusting their gaze.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that confidence display uses a clear, concise format helps build trust in the system's reliability and accuracy. By providing users with a simple numeric score, label, and short reason for each memory or subtitle, the wearer can better understand when to rely on the system's suggestions versus taking their own actions.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Introduce a standardized confidence format that includes a numeric score (e.g., 0.84), a label indicating certainty (e.g., "High"), and a short reason for the confidence level (e.g., "Seen recently").

3. **Likely payoff**

   This simple yet effective approach will enhance user trust by making it clear when the system is highly confident versus when it has lower levels of certainty. Users will be more likely to follow the system’s suggestions in high-confidence situations and double-check or disregard them in low-confidence scenarios, leading to a more reliable and useful assistive experience.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

Improving memory cache policies ensures that frequently referenced entities, such as people's names and recent contexts, are readily available to the wearer. This reduces the need for frequent lookups, minimizing latency and maintaining trust by ensuring that critical information is always at hand.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

Define a local cache eviction policy that prioritizes frequently encountered faces and names over less relevant entities. For example, maintain a fixed-size cache of up to 50 recent face names with the most recently seen entries given higher priority.

3. **Likely payoff**

This change will significantly reduce the latency associated with name recall during conversations, enhancing the wearer's trust in the system’s reliability. By keeping frequently encountered faces and names readily accessible, the glasses can provide quicker and more accurate memory support without overwhelming the local cache.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear phone-cloud boundary for live subtitle processing, we ensure that critical real-time operations remain on the phone to maintain low latency and high reliability. This focus on local processing supports the core mission of providing near-real-time subtitles that the wearer can trust in noisy environments.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one task-specific cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing, such as a rule where the glasses prioritize local processing but automatically fall back to cloud processing if the phone loses connection or if the processing load exceeds on-device capabilities.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This focused approach enhances the reliability and trustworthiness of subtitles by leveraging the phone's immediate processing power while maintaining flexibility for offloading in edge cases. It ensures that the wearer receives timely, high-quality subtitles without disruptions due to network issues or heavy computational demands.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one task-specific cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing, prioritizing local processing but allowing cloud support during brief disconnections or high-load scenarios.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear visual hierarchy that prioritizes subtitles over one-line prompts, we ensure that the wearer can focus on critical information without feeling overwhelmed. This improves understanding and reduces cognitive load in noisy or dense interactions.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple subtitle-vs-one-line priority rule: always show subtitles clearly first, then provide one-line support if space allows, but suppress one-line prompts if subtitles are present and the display is crowded.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This rule will enhance the wearer's ability to focus on essential information during live conversations, reducing distractions and improving overall comprehension. It will also align with the core mission of providing clear, uncluttered assistance that does not overwhelm the user.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define the visual hierarchy rule to prioritize subtitles over one-line prompts: always show subtitles clearly first, then provide one-line support if space allows, but suppress one-line prompts if subtitles are present and the display is crowded.
