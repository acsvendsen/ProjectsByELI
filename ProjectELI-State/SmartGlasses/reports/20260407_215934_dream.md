### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving subtitle placement rules ensures that subtitles remain clear, readable, and non-distracting during live conversations. This enhances the wearer's ability to follow the conversation without interruption, thereby reinforcing the core attractors of real-time subtitle clarity and low-friction assistance.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a fixed position for subtitles on the right side of the field of view, with a slight downward bias (e.g., 10-15 degrees), to minimize overlap with the wearer's primary visual focus while ensuring they remain within the wearer's peripheral vision and are easily readable.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By maintaining consistent subtitle placement, users will have a predictable and reliable way to read subtitles without having to frequently adjust their gaze or attention away from the conversation. This improves overall user experience and trust in the system’s ability to assist effectively without causing distraction.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define the fixed position for subtitles at 10 degrees to the right and 15 degrees downward from the center of the field of view.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the confidence display format helps ensure that the wearer can trust the system's assistance without overloading them with unnecessary information or creating false certainty. Clear and honest confidence indicators are crucial for maintaining the system's credibility and helping the wearer understand when to rely on the subtitles or seek additional context.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple numeric score + label + short reason format for confidence objects, such as: `score 0.84 + "High" + "Seen recently"`; `score 0.42 + "Low" + "Weak match"`; and `score 0.63 + "Medium" + "Name heard once in introduction"`.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing a clear confidence display format, the system will provide more reliable and understandable feedback to the wearer, reducing confusion and building trust over time. This can lead to better user acceptance and deeper integration of the glasses into daily use.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving memory cache policies ensures that frequently encountered faces and names are stored more robustly in local memory. This enhances the reliability of name recall during conversations, reducing the likelihood of missed identifications or errors.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit for person-memory objects to ensure that only the most recently and frequently seen individuals are kept in local storage, while less relevant entries are evicted.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By setting a specific cache size limit, we can balance memory usage against recall accuracy. This ensures that the device remains responsive and does not consume unnecessary resources on low-value memories, thereby improving overall performance and battery efficiency.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Set a local cache size limit of 10 person-memory objects to optimize memory use for high-frequency encounters.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear boundary for what runs on the phone versus the cloud, we can ensure that V1 stays focused on delivering high-quality, low-latency subtitle and memory assistance without overburdening the device or compromising privacy. This clarity will help in maintaining a discreet UX while providing essential support.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define explicit rules for which tasks should run phone-local (on-device) versus falling back to the cloud. For example, real-time subtitle processing and face/name recall lookup should be handled locally on the phone to ensure low latency and privacy. Transcript cleanup can optionally use cloud resources where the benefit justifies increased latency.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By clearly defining local and cloud tasks, we reduce battery drain from unnecessary cloud requests, maintain high trust through fast response times, and keep the system discreet by avoiding frequent interactions with the cloud. This will enhance user satisfaction and perceived value in V1.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that subtitles remain clear, fast, and stable enough to be trusted in live conversations directly supports the primary attractor of real-time subtitle clarity. This improves the wearer's ability to understand spoken interactions without cognitive overload or distraction.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement rules: one for high-confidence speakers (e.g., when someone is speaking clearly and with confidence) and another for low-confidence speakers (e.g., when someone is mumbling, speaking softly, or in a noisy environment).

3. **Likely payoff**

   By defining clear placement rules based on speaker confidence, the subtitles can remain unobtrusive yet highly visible during critical moments, thereby enhancing trust and usability.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
