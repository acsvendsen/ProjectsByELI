### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring stable subtitle placement helps maintain the wearer's trust in the system, particularly in noisy environments where clear and consistent subtitles are crucial for understanding live conversations.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define three fixed subtitle placement rules: one for high-confidence states (e.g., repeated names or well-known phrases), one for medium-confidence states (e.g., unfamiliar but likely correct words), and one for low-confidence states (e.g., new or uncertain inputs).

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing stable and predictable subtitle placement, the system will reduce cognitive load on the wearer, enhance trust in the assistant's reliability, and improve overall user satisfaction.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a recency-first cache eviction rule, we ensure that the most relevant and frequently encountered names and contexts are prioritized in memory lookup. This enhances the system's ability to provide timely and accurate support, maintaining trust and relevance.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple recency-first eviction policy where items are evicted based on last accessed timestamp, with a fixed maximum cache size to prevent excessive memory usage.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will improve the system's responsiveness by ensuring that recently encountered names and contexts remain readily available for quick recall. It will reduce the likelihood of outdated or irrelevant information being retrieved, thus enhancing overall trust and utility.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By clearly defining what tasks are handled phone-locally versus what falls back to the cloud, we can ensure that V1 remains focused on providing real-time and low-latency assistance without overloading the device's resources or sacrificing privacy.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a single task boundary for live subtitle processing: all live subtitle text generation should be handled phone-locally using the TranscriptLab test app, while transcript cleanup and memory archive sync fall back to the cloud.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This clear delineation will allow us to maximize V1's real-time performance and minimize battery drain by keeping heavy processing off the glasses hardware. It also ensures that live subtitles remain fast and reliable, which is critical for maintaining user trust and providing useful assistance in noisy environments.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring a clear visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support helps maintain focus during conversations, enhancing the wearer's ability to process information without feeling overwhelmed. By defining a priority rule that emphasizes subtitles over one-line prompts when necessary, the system can ensure that critical communication is not obscured.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a subtitle-vs-one-line priority rule where subtitles are given higher visual prominence and precedence in the display hierarchy, especially during high-confidence conversations or when multiple people are speaking rapidly.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By prioritizing subtitles over one-line support, the system can maintain clear visibility of crucial information, reducing cognitive load and enhancing real-time understanding during noisy environments or complex interactions. This ensures that the wearer's attention remains on the most important elements of the conversation.

4. **Immediate next probe**
