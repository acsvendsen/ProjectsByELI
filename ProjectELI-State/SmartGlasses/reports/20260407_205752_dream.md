### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining subtitle placement rules based on confidence levels, we ensure that subtitles remain clear and unobtrusive while still providing essential information to the wearer. This helps maintain trust in the system's reliability during live conversations.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement modes: one for high-confidence matches (e.g., known faces or names) placed above the conversation line, and another for low-confidence matches (e.g., likely new people) placed below the conversation line but still visible.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will improve the wearer's trust in the system by ensuring that critical information is always easily accessible while keeping less certain information from overwhelming the visual space. It also aligns with the core tension of balance between privacy and usefulness.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the confidence display format ensures that the wearer can trust the system's assistance while maintaining privacy. By clearly showing certainty levels, the system can prevent over-reliance or underestimation of its accuracy, thereby enhancing daily-use value and trust.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple yet informative confidence label with a short reason for low-confidence cases. For instance, instead of just "Low," provide a reason like "Weak match" to give context without overwhelming the wearer.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing clear and concise certainty information, the system can help the wearer make better decisions during interactions. This reduces cognitive load by aligning the user's trust with actual reliability, leading to more natural and less awkward conversations.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define the confidence score presentation format as "score 0.84 + 'High'; score 0.42 + 'Low'; score 0.63 + 'Medium'."

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving local memory thresholds ensures that relevant names and faces are cached on the phone for faster recall and more reliable support during conversations. This reduces reliance on cloud latency, enhances real-time subtitle quality by providing quicker context updates, and maintains privacy by keeping sensitive information closer to the device.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local memory threshold that caches names and faces seen in the last 10 interactions for at least one minute before evicting them. This simple rule ensures key people remain in memory while avoiding excessive storage overhead.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping recently seen names and faces in local cache, the system can quickly recall and display them during conversations without waiting for cloud updates or causing social awkwardness by asking the wearer to remember details from past interactions.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one memory eviction rule that caches names and faces seen in the last 10 interactions for at least one minute before evicting them.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear boundary for what runs phone-local versus what falls back to cloud processing, we ensure that V1 focuses on essential real-time subtitle and memory support while minimizing battery and thermal impact. This aligns with the project's goal of delivering high-quality, low-friction assistance without overburdening hardware.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a single task-specific phone-cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing that keeps critical logic on-device to maintain real-time performance and minimize latency.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This decision will ensure V1 delivers stable, fast subtitles in noisy environments while managing battery use effectively. It also sets a clear precedent for future cloud interactions, reducing the risk of over-reliance on slow network processing.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one phone-cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing that keeps critical logic phone-local to maintain real-time performance and minimize latency.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support ensures that the wearer can easily focus on critical information while maintaining a clear, uncluttered view of their surroundings. This enhances the overall user experience by ensuring that important subtitled content stands out without overwhelming the wearer with unnecessary text.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a priority rule that gives subtitles higher precedence over one-line support when both are active simultaneously. For example, use a policy where subtitles always appear on top of one-line prompts unless the one-line prompt is explicitly marked as high-priority for the current context.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By clearly prioritizing and differentiating between subtitle content and one-line support, the wearer can more easily follow along in conversations while still having access to brief contextual aids when needed. This improves understanding and engagement without distracting from the primary task of following spoken words.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define a rule where subtitles always appear on top of one-line prompts unless the one-line prompt is explicitly marked as high-priority for the current context.
