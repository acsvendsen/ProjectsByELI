### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving subtitle placement rules ensures that subtitles remain clear and unobtrusive while still providing useful information to the wearer. Stable, predictable placement reduces cognitive load by minimizing visual distractions and ensuring that important information is always in a consistent location.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define three specific subtitle placement rules: one for low confidence (e.g., 0.6 or lower), another for medium confidence (e.g., 0.7 to 0.89), and a third for high confidence (e.g., 0.9 or above). These rules can be based on the confidence score of the spoken word, ensuring that less certain words appear slightly below the baseline while highly confident ones stay at eye level.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing these placement rules, the system will become more reliable and intuitive for the wearer. High-confidence subtitles will always be in a fixed position, reducing visual clutter during critical moments when clarity is most needed. Low-confidence subtitles can be placed slightly below to avoid distraction but still provide useful information.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the confidence display format helps ensure that the wearer can trust the assistant's behavior without overloading them with unnecessary information. A well-designed confidence label and short reason will provide just enough context to build and maintain trust, while avoiding confusion or creating a sense of false certainty.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific confidence format for V1 that includes a numeric score, a label, and a short reason. For example: `score 0.85 + "High" + "Seen recently"`.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By clearly labeling the confidence level with both a numerical score and a human-readable label along with a short reason for the decision, the wearer can quickly understand when to trust the assistant's suggestions or rely on their own judgment. This reduces cognitive load and builds trust over time as the system consistently reinforces its reliability.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a local memory threshold for face/name recall, we can ensure that the system remembers critical names and faces without overwhelming the wearer's cognitive load. This helps in maintaining trust by providing accurate and timely support while keeping the interface uncluttered.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit of 10 entries for face/name recall on the phone, with automatic eviction based on recency.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This policy will allow the system to remember key names and faces effectively while managing memory usage efficiently, reducing the risk of information overload and maintaining high trust levels in memory support.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By clearly defining what stays phone-local versus falling back to cloud for live subtitle processing, we ensure real-time subtitle clarity remains stable and reliable. This helps maintain trust in the system during critical interactions, where delay or processing errors could lead to misunderstandings.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific task boundary: live subtitle processing should primarily occur on the phone unless cloud fallback is explicitly triggered by high latency or heavy load conditions.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By limiting live subtitle processing to the phone, we ensure real-time stability and fast response times, which are critical for maintaining trust in the system during live conversations. This improves the overall user experience and helps preserve the core mission of providing clear and reliable subtitle assistance.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear visual hierarchy that prioritizes subtitles while maintaining low-friction one-line support, we ensure that the wearer can trust the system in noisy environments and dense conversations without feeling overwhelmed or distracted. This focus on clarity and relevance directly supports the mission of reducing social and cognitive friction.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a subtitle-vs-one-line priority rule that gives subtitles clear precedence over one-line support when confidence levels are high, but allows for coexistence at lower confidence levels with clear visual cues to indicate the nature of each.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing this rule, we can ensure that the most critical information (subtitles) remains prominent and trustworthy, while still providing useful contextual prompts without overwhelming the wearer. This will enhance trust in the system and improve overall usability in real-world scenarios.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
