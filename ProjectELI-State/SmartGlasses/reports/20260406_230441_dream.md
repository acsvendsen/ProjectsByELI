### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring stable subtitle placement rules enhances the wearer's trust in the system, making subtitles more reliable and predictable. This stability helps the wearer focus on conversation without constantly adjusting their gaze or attention.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement modes: one for high-confidence states (e.g., well-known speakers) and another for lower-confidence states (e.g., new people or ambiguous contexts). This simple change allows the system to adapt its visual priority based on the reliability of the information, reducing unnecessary distractions.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing stable subtitle placement rules, the system will reduce gaze shifts during conversations, improving overall comprehension and trust. This stability can also help in creating a more discreet user experience that aligns with the core attractors of low-friction assistance and privacy awareness.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Memory caching policies that optimize for fast lookup while respecting privacy and battery constraints will ensure the system remains useful without overwhelming the wearer with too many false positives or outdated information. This improves the overall trust in memory recall, ensuring that the wearer can rely on the memory support when needed.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Defining a local cache eviction rule that removes old or less relevant face and name memories after a certain period of inactivity while maintaining a stable set of recently encountered entities will unlock faster lookup times without overloading the system with outdated data.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing this local cache eviction policy, the system can provide more timely and accurate memory support, reducing the cognitive load on the wearer by ensuring that only relevant memories are available at any given time. This will enhance the user experience and maintain a balance between utility and privacy.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one local cache eviction rule for face and name memories, removing old or less relevant entries after 24 hours of inactivity to optimize for fast lookup times while respecting battery constraints.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By carefully defining what stays phone-local versus what falls back to cloud for live subtitle processing, we ensure that subtitles remain clear, fast, and stable in real-time interactions. This reduces latency without sacrificing critical functionality, aligning with our attractor of "Real-time subtitle clarity" and addressing the tension between "Latency vs richness."

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a single task-specific phone/cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing where subtitles fall back to the cloud only when the local processing latency exceeds 100 milliseconds.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By limiting cloud fallbacks to high-latency situations, we can maintain near-real-time performance and stability in most interactions, enhancing trust and daily-use value without unduly draining battery or compromising privacy.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.
