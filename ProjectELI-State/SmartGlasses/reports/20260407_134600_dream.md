### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By ensuring stable subtitle placement, the system enhances the wearer's trust in real-time subtitles during live conversations. Stable positioning reduces distractions and ensures that important information is always easily accessible without shifting focus away from the conversation.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a fixed subtitle position rule for different confidence states, where high-confidence subtitles are placed at the bottom of the display and low-confidence ones slightly above or to the side, but still visible yet less intrusive.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This stable placement will reduce cognitive load by minimizing the need for the wearer to continually adjust their gaze between the conversation and the glasses. It also ensures that high-confidence subtitles are always easily readable, enhancing overall trust in the assistive system.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the confidence display format will enhance the trustworthiness of the system by clearly indicating the certainty of the information provided to the wearer. This clarity ensures that the wearer understands when they can rely on the subtitles or memory support and when they should seek additional confirmation.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a new confidence object format that includes both a numeric score, a label (High, Medium, Low), and a short reason (e.g., "Seen recently," "Weak match"). This format will provide the wearer with clear feedback on the reliability of the information.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing this improved confidence display, the system can better manage trust. Clear and consistent indications of certainty will help prevent overreliance on inaccurate or uncertain information, leading to more effective and user-friendly interactions.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a local cache threshold for face/name memory, we ensure that the glasses can quickly recall and display familiar names and faces in real-time, enhancing the wearer's confidence and understanding without overburdening the phone or cloud with continuous sync requests.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit for person-memory objects on the phone, ensuring that only frequently encountered names and faces are cached for quick recall.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will reduce latency in face/name recall, improve the reliability of the memory system, and allow for faster, more accurate subtitle placement based on recent interactions. It also aligns with the project's core tension of balancing privacy vs. usefulness by limiting data retention while maintaining real-time assistance.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define a local cache size limit of 10 frequently encountered names and faces for quick recall in V1.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By carefully defining which tasks stay phone-local versus those that fall back to cloud, we ensure that V1 remains focused on real-time subtitle quality and memory support without overloading the device or compromising privacy. This balance strengthens trust by providing timely, accurate assistance while managing resource constraints.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific task boundary for live subtitle processing: If speech recognition is within 500 milliseconds of audio capture, process it on the phone; otherwise, fall back to cloud processing. This ensures real-time clarity without overburdening local resources and maintains user trust.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping live subtitle processing phone-local, we can achieve faster, more stable subtitle delivery with lower latency. This improves the wearer's ability to understand in noisy environments and reduces battery drain from constant cloud queries. The confidence format will be simpler and more reliable, as it won't need to handle complex real-time processing.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one local vs cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing where speech recognition is within 500 milliseconds of audio capture.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring a clear visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line prompts helps maintain low-friction assistance by preventing visual overload while still providing necessary support. This balance enhances the wearer's ability to focus on conversation without feeling overwhelmed, thereby preserving trust in the system.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a subtitle-vs-one-line priority rule that suppresses or de-emphasizes one-line prompts when subtitles are active and visible, ensuring the wearer’s attention is directed primarily towards real-time subtitles.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By prioritizing subtitles over one-line prompts during live conversations, the system can maintain high clarity and reduce visual distractions, leading to a more seamless and effective assistance experience for the wearer.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define a visual hierarchy rule that suppresses one-line prompts when subtitles are active and visible.
