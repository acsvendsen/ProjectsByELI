### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring subtitle placement is stable and non-distracting in V1 helps maintain trust and clarity, allowing the wearer to focus on the conversation without visual interruptions. This stability supports real-time subtitle clarity, a primary attractor.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two fixed subtitle placement rules: one for high-confidence content (e.g., clear, well-known speakers) and another for lower-confidence content (e.g., less familiar voices or background noise).

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing stable subtitle placement based on confidence levels, the system can reduce visual distractions during critical moments of conversation, enhancing trust and the wearer's ability to focus.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that confidence labels and scores are clearly displayed helps build trust in the system’s accuracy. By providing a numeric score alongside a label, users can better understand the reliability of the information presented. This transparency is crucial for maintaining the wearer's confidence in the system's ability to assist accurately.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple confidence format that includes both a numeric score and a label with a short reason. For example, use "0.84 High (Seen recently)" or "0.42 Low (Weak match)".

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing this simple yet informative confidence display, the system will provide users with clear feedback on the reliability of the information presented. This transparency can significantly enhance trust and reduce cognitive load by making it easier for the wearer to understand when they should rely more heavily on the subtitles or look for additional context.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a clear local memory threshold for face/name recall, the system ensures that only recently encountered individuals are cached locally on the phone, reducing privacy concerns and improving trust through more precise confidence objects.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one local cache eviction rule based on recency of interaction to limit the number of stored faces/names per session.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will reduce memory storage overhead while maintaining useful recall for frequent contacts, enhancing privacy and trust by avoiding unnecessary data retention.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one local cache eviction rule that evicts face/name memories older than 24 hours.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that live subtitle processing stays on the phone until explicitly necessary for latency-critical tasks, such as face/name recall lookup or transcript cleanup, maintains real-time performance and trust in subtitle clarity.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a single task boundary where the cloud falls back to the phone: live subtitle processing should run locally on the phone first before potentially falling back to the cloud for rich analysis or heavy computation.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping subtitle processing local, the system can ensure real-time performance and clarity without introducing latency that could break trust in subtitles. This approach also reduces battery drain and thermal load from always-on cloud processing.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that subtitles are clear, fast, and stable enough in live conversations helps the wearer understand and respond better without feeling overwhelmed or distracted. This improves the core attractor of real-time subtitle clarity and aligns with the mission to reduce social and cognitive friction.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define 3 subtitle placement rules based on confidence levels: high (above 0.8), medium (0.5-0.79), and low (below 0.5). For each rule, prioritize placement in the top third of the wearer's field of view to ensure readability but avoid distraction.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By setting clear rules for subtitle placement based on confidence levels, we can reduce visual clutter during high-confidence interactions while still providing necessary support. This will enhance user trust and improve overall real-time subtitle clarity.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
