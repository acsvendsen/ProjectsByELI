### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   - Ensuring stable subtitle placement helps maintain the wearer's trust in the system by making subtitles readable and unobtrusive, which is crucial for real-time understanding.

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   - Define a fixed subtitle placement rule that prioritizes readability over dynamic adjustments based on confidence levels or gaze direction.

3. **Likely payoff**
   - By keeping subtitles in a consistent position, the wearer can rely on them more easily without worrying about where to look next, reducing cognitive load and enhancing overall understanding during conversations.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the confidence display format enhances trust in the system's reliability, ensuring that the wearer understands the level of certainty behind each piece of information provided. A well-designed confidence display will help manage expectations and reduce cognitive load by providing clear and concise indicators of confidence.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple yet informative confidence object format: use a numeric score with two labels ("High" or "Low") and a short reason (e.g., "Seen recently", "Weak match"). This format provides enough detail to build trust without overwhelming the wearer.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing a clear and concise confidence display, the system will appear more reliable and trustworthy, reducing user skepticism and increasing overall confidence in its accuracy. This will lead to better daily-use value as users become more comfortable with the assistant's assistance.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a local cache size limit for face/name memory, we ensure that only recently and relevantly encountered individuals are stored in memory. This reduces the risk of privacy concerns and overburdening the wearer with irrelevant information while maintaining a useful level of context for repeated interactions.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a maximum cache size for local face/name memory objects, limiting them to the top N most recently interacted individuals (e.g., 5-10).

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach balances the need for contextual support with privacy and memory management, ensuring that the system remains discreet and responsive without overwhelming the wearer with too much information.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define a cache size of 5 for local face/name memory objects to retain the topmost recent interactions.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the phone-cloud boundary for live subtitle processing ensures that subtitles remain real-time and reliable, which is critical for maintaining trust and providing continuous, useful assistance during conversations.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific threshold for when to fallback from local subtitle processing on the phone to cloud-based processing. For example, if the speech recognition latency exceeds 500ms, switch to cloud processing.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By ensuring that subtitles remain real-time, we reduce the risk of mistranslations and maintain a high level of trust in the system's accuracy, which is essential for its primary mission of aiding real-world interactions.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one phone/cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing where cloud fallback triggers at 500ms latency or higher.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support ensures that the wearer can prioritize critical information without being overwhelmed. This helps in maintaining clarity and reducing cognitive load, allowing the wearer to focus on the conversation more effectively.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a clear precedence rule where subtitles are always shown prominently but one-line prompts are displayed only when there is no active subtitle or when their content is less critical than ongoing speech.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By maintaining a clean visual hierarchy, the wearer can trust the subtitles more as they remain the primary focus. Simultaneously, useful contextual information will not be missed, leading to a better overall experience and increased user satisfaction.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support: prioritize subtitles over prompts when both are active, with prompts only displayed in the margin or as brief overlay notes during pauses in speech.
