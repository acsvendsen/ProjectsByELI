### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring stable subtitle placement rules in V1 helps maintain consistent user trust and comprehension. By defining a fixed position for subtitles, we can avoid distracting or fluctuating display behavior that could disrupt real-time conversation flow.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement modes: one for high-confidence speech (e.g., clear speaker) and another for low-confidence situations (e.g., background noise). This distinction allows the glasses to adapt minimally while maintaining stability.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing a stable yet context-aware subtitle display, we can enhance user trust in the system's ability to assist effectively without overwhelming them with constant adjustments. This consistency will be particularly beneficial in noisy environments where accurate and timely subtitles are crucial.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining the confidence display format to use a simple label with a short reason, we can ensure that the wearer understands the reliability of the information without overwhelming them with too much detail. This clarity helps maintain trust and ensures that the subtitles are seen as credible in real-time interactions.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Change the confidence object from `score 0.84 + "High"` to `score 0.84 + "High" + "Seen recently"` for high-confidence items, and keep the low-confidence items simple like `score 0.42 + "Low" + "Weak match"`. This small change provides a clear, concise reason for the confidence score.

3. **Likely payoff**

   Implementing this change will help users better understand when they can trust the subtitles in real-time interactions. It reduces cognitive load and enhances the overall trustworthiness of the system, making it more reliable during critical moments like live conversations or important meetings.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a clear local memory threshold for name recall, the system ensures that critical names are stored locally on the phone with a high degree of confidence and recency. This reduces the risk of privacy violations while ensuring that essential information is readily accessible to the wearer without overburdening the cloud.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local memory threshold for name recall where faces and names seen more than three times in recent interactions are stored locally with a confidence score above 0.75, while all other names are cached on the cloud or not retained at all.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This policy will enhance the wearer's trust by ensuring that critical names remain accessible without compromising privacy. It also optimizes resource usage by keeping only highly relevant and frequently encountered names locally, reducing cloud load and improving overall performance.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear boundary for what stays phone-local versus what falls back to cloud, we ensure that V1 can deliver real-time subtitle clarity and memory support without compromising battery life or privacy. This helps maintain the core mission of creating an assistive device that is both useful and discreet.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific task boundary for live subtitle processing where subtitles are generated on the phone first, then any fallback to cloud can be limited to low-latency tasks such as transcript cleanup or memory archive sync.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping live subtitle processing local and only sending necessary data to the cloud for cleanup or archiving, we reduce battery drain, maintain real-time performance, and ensure that sensitive information is not unnecessarily transmitted. This enhances trust and usability without overcomplicating the system.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one phone-cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing where the phone handles initial transcription and on-device cleanup tasks, with only critical data sent to the cloud for further refinement or archiving.

By defining a clear precedence rule, we enhance the low-friction assistance aspect of the system, making it easier for the wearer to focus on critical information during interactions.

The wearer can trust the subtitles more since they are always front-and-center, while still receiving necessary contextual support when needed but not obstructing their primary focus.
