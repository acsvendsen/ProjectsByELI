### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining specific subtitle placement rules based on confidence states, we can ensure that subtitles remain clear and stable enough to be trusted in live conversation while minimizing distractions. This helps maintain the wearer's focus on the interaction, enhancing their understanding without overwhelming them with too much text.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define three subtitle placement modes: one for high-confidence faces/names (e.g., >0.8 confidence), another for medium-confidence entities (e.g., 0.5-0.8 confidence), and a third for low-confidence or new entities (<0.5 confidence). Each mode should have distinct visual rules, such as font size, color, and screen position.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By having clear placement rules based on confidence, the system can maintain high subtitle clarity during critical interactions while reducing clutter in less certain situations. This improves overall usability and trust without overwhelming the wearer with too much text.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the confidence display format helps ensure that the wearer can trust the subtitle and memory support provided by the system. A clear, concise, and reliable confidence indicator reduces uncertainty and builds confidence in the assistive behavior, making the overall experience more useful and trustworthy.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple numeric score + label + short reason format for confidence objects. For example, use a scale of 0 to 1 where:
   - Score 0.95 or above: "High" + "Seen multiple times recently"
   - Score 0.7 to 0.94: "Medium" + "Name heard in recent context"
   - Below 0.7: "Low" + "Weak match, likely new"

3. **Likely payoff**

   By using a clear and consistent confidence format, the system will provide more reliable and trustworthy assistance. This will help the wearer understand when to trust the subtitles and memory support, leading to better overall user experience and trust in the product.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear boundary for what runs phone-local versus cloud, we ensure that the system remains lightweight and responsive, which is crucial for real-time subtitle clarity and memory support. This decision helps maintain the balance between useful assistive behavior and battery/thermal constraints.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific task-boundary rule: live subtitle processing should remain phone-local to avoid any cloud latency that could degrade the near-real-time requirement.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping live subtitle processing on the phone, we can ensure subtitles are delivered with minimal delay and high clarity, enhancing the wearer's trust in the system during critical conversations. This also helps manage battery usage more efficiently since heavy speech processing is handled locally without network overhead.

4. **Immediate next probe**
