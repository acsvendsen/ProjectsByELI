### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Keeping subtitles clear, fast, and stable in real-time conversation helps ensure that the wearer can trust them fully. This improves their ability to understand and respond appropriately, reducing social and cognitive friction.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a fixed subtitle placement rule for V1 that keeps subtitles at the bottom center of the frame. This ensures consistent visibility without distracting the wearer's gaze during conversation.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping subtitles in a fixed position, the system can avoid constant repositioning and potential disruptions. This will enhance user trust and reduce cognitive load, making the SmartGlasses more effective as an assistive tool.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that confidence labels are clear, concise, and provide a short reason for certainty can greatly enhance trust in the system's memory support. By representing confidence as a score (e.g., "score 0.84 + 'High' + 'Seen recently'"), the wearer can better understand the reliability of remembered information without feeling overwhelmed by too much detail.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one format for confidence labels that includes both a numeric score, a short label, and a brief reason for the confidence level. This could be structured as "score X + 'Y' + 'Z'" where X is the numerical value (0-1), Y is the label ('High', 'Low', or 'Medium'), and Z is a short phrase explaining the source of the confidence.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing clear, concise, and context-rich confidence labels, the system can build greater trust with the wearer. This clarity helps in situations where the wearer might need to quickly verify someone's name or recall recent interactions, ensuring that they do not rely on potentially inaccurate memory support.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

2. **What small change unlocks it**

3. **Likely payoff**
   - This change will ensure that frequently encountered people are quickly recalled without cloud assistance, enhancing real-time response and user trust in memory support.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear boundary for what stays phone-local versus falling back to cloud, we ensure that critical real-time subtitle processing happens on the phone without unnecessary latency or battery drain. This improves trust and reliability in noisy environments, which is essential for the core attractor of real-time subtitle clarity.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific task boundary where subtitles must remain entirely local. For V1, this could be: "Define live subtitle processing to always run on-device, with no cloud fallbacks."

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping live subtitle processing entirely phone-local, we reduce latency, ensure real-time responsiveness, and maintain low battery drain. This directly supports the core attractor of real-time subtitle clarity by ensuring that subtitles are displayed without delay or degradation.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring a clear visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line prompts enhances the wearer's ability to focus on critical information without becoming overwhelmed. By defining a priority rule, we can ensure that subtitles remain the primary focus while one-line prompts provide supplementary, non-distracting support.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a subtitle-vs-one-line priority rule that routes higher-priority content (subtitles) to more prominent display areas and lower-priority content (one-line prompts) to less intrusive areas or suppressed display modes when necessary.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This small change will help the wearer maintain their focus on essential information during conversations, reducing cognitive overload and increasing overall understanding without creating distractions. It also supports the core attractor of low-friction assistance by ensuring that one-line prompts do not compete with the primary task of reading subtitles.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
