### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining subtitle placement rules based on confidence levels, we ensure that subtitles remain readable and unobtrusive while still providing critical assistance. This helps maintain a balance between clarity and distraction, preserving the wearer's trust in the system without overwhelming them.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement rules: one for low-confidence scenarios where subtitles are displayed above the conversation stream to ensure they do not get lost or mixed with other text, and another for high-confidence scenarios where subtitles can be slightly more compact but still clearly visible.

3. **Likely payoff**

   Implementing these placement rules will reduce visual clutter during high-confidence interactions while ensuring that low-confidence contexts remain easily readable. This will help maintain a clear and unobtrusive interface, enhancing the wearer's trust in the system without sacrificing functionality.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the confidence display format can enhance the wearer's trust in the system by clearly indicating the reliability of the information provided. This ensures that the wearer is aware when they should rely more on their own judgment and when the system’s assistance is highly certain.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple numeric score + label + short reason format for confidence objects. For example, scores could range from 0 to 1 with corresponding labels like "Low", "Medium", and "High". The short reason should provide context without being overly narrative, such as "Seen recently" or "Weak match".

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing a clear and concise confidence format, the system will be more transparent and trustworthy. This can reduce cognitive load on the wearer by providing them with immediate feedback on the reliability of the information presented.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that new faces and names are remembered with a stable, confidence-aware memory policy helps maintain trust in the system's ability to assist effectively. By reinforcing memory through repeated encounters and caching recent entities locally, the glasses can provide fast and accurate support without overloading the cloud or battery.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache eviction rule that retains recently encountered faces and names for at least 30 minutes, with a priority on entities seen multiple times within an hour. This ensures that likely new persons are remembered automatically by default while allowing older entries to be cleared if necessary.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing this caching policy, the glasses will show improved memory support for first-time interactions, reducing the likelihood of forgetting important names and faces. This enhances user trust in the system's ability to assist effectively, especially in fast-paced or noisy environments where memory recall is crucial.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.
