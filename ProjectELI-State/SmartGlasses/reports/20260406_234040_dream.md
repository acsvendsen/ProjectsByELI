### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   - Improving subtitle placement stability enhances overall user trust in the system's ability to provide accurate and reliable real-time subtitles during live conversations, which is crucial for the wearer’s comprehension and understanding.

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   - Define a fixed subtitle placement rule that prioritizes a stable position on the lower right side of the field of view (FOV) with minimal movement, allowing users to focus without distraction.

3. **Likely payoff**
   - By ensuring subtitles remain in a consistent location, the system can reduce cognitive load and visual distractions for the wearer, leading to more seamless interaction and higher trust in the assistive capabilities.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the confidence display format ensures that the wearer can trust the information being shown. A clear and concise confidence label with a short reason will help maintain the wearer's confidence in the system, which is crucial for real-time subtitle clarity (subtitle_clarity attractor) and overall user trust.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a new confidence format that uses a numeric score, a label, and a short reason. For example: `score 0.95 + "High" + "Seen recently"` for well-established names or entities, and `score 0.30 + "Low" + "Weak match"` for uncertain or first-time encounters.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing a clear numeric score, label, and short reason in the confidence display, users will have a better understanding of how reliable the system's information is. This transparency helps in maintaining user trust, which is vital for the real-time subtitle clarity attractor and overall daily-use value.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a local cache size limit for memory objects, we ensure that critical information such as names and faces are stored efficiently on the phone without overwhelming the device's resources. This improves trust by preventing lag or crashes when looking up recent context.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit of 50 memory objects per person (names/faces), with a maximum total of 100 unique memory objects across all people and contexts.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change ensures that the phone can handle frequent lookups for common interactions without running out of space or performance, thereby maintaining consistent, fast responses during real-world use.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define a local cache size limit of 50 memory objects per person and a maximum total of 100 unique memory objects across all people and contexts.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining specific cloud fallback boundaries for different tasks, we can ensure that critical real-time functionality like live subtitle processing remains local to maintain low latency and high reliability. This focus on local processing aligns with the core mission of providing real-time, clear subtitles while respecting constraints around battery life and thermal management.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing that triggers when the glasses detect a significant drop in confidence or a new speaker appears unexpectedly.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This cloud fallback strategy ensures that critical real-time subtitles remain reliable by leveraging phone-side processing whenever necessary, thereby maintaining high trust and clarity in noisy environments without overloading the battery or overheating the hardware.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring a clear visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line prompts helps maintain focus on the most critical information. This clarity ensures that the wearer can quickly understand what is being said and avoid cognitive overload, thereby enhancing their overall understanding and engagement in conversations.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific subtitle-vs-one-line priority rule that routes attention to subtitles first, then one-liners when appropriate, but suppresses one-liners during high-confidence subtitle reads.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By prioritizing subtitles over one-liners during times of higher confidence and clarity, the system can reduce visual clutter and maintain a clear focus on critical information. This will improve the wearer's ability to follow conversations without feeling overwhelmed by additional prompts that are less necessary at those moments.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
