### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving subtitle placement rules can enhance the wearer's ability to trust and understand real-time subtitles in noisy environments. By ensuring that subtitles remain stable and unobtrusive, the system can maintain a high level of clarity without overwhelming the wearer. This stability is crucial for building confidence in the assistive behavior.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define 3 subtitle placement rules based on confidence levels: 
   - Low confidence: Place subtitles at the bottom center to avoid distraction but remain visible.
   - Medium confidence: Center subtitles slightly above eye level to be readable while not overwhelming the wearer.
   - High confidence: Place subtitles directly below speech to minimize visual clutter and maximize readability.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing these placement rules, the system can reduce subtitle distractions during conversations with low-confidence matches, balance visibility and clarity for medium-confidence matches, and maintain high-confidence matches in a minimally intrusive position. This will help the wearer trust the subtitles more and stay engaged in real-time interactions.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the confidence display format helps ensure that the wearer can trust the system's outputs. By providing clear and consistent confidence labels, short reasons, and numeric scores, the system enhances its reliability in noisy environments where accurate subtitles are crucial.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple but effective confidence label plus short-reason format for V1. For example: "High" + "Seen recently"; "Low" + "Weak match"; "Medium" + "Name heard once in introduction".

3. **Likely payoff**

   Clear and consistent confidence labels will help the wearer understand when to trust the subtitles and when they might need to pay more attention. This enhances the overall trustworthiness of the system, especially in critical listening environments.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

2. **What small change unlocks it**

3. **Likely payoff**

4. **Immediate next probe**

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that live subtitle processing remains local on the phone helps maintain real-time clarity and stability. This focus on local processing reduces latency, which is critical for trust in noisy environments where subtitles need to be clear and immediate.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a single task-specific cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing: only fall back to the cloud when the glasses' battery level drops below 30%, ensuring real-time performance remains consistent with low power usage.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping live subtitle processing local, we can ensure that subtitles remain clear and stable during critical conversations. This will strengthen trust in the system’s ability to provide reliable assistance without compromising on latency or battery life.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.
