### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   - By defining a fixed subtitle placement rule that balances readability with non-distracting stability, V1 can maintain consistent and reliable subtitle presentation. This ensures users trust their subtitles without the constant adjustment of where to look.

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   - Define a default subtitle position at the bottom center of the frame, visible but not obstructive during natural head movements. Adjustments can be made for varying confidence levels or specific usage contexts like noisy environments.

3. **Likely payoff**
   - A fixed and stable subtitle placement rule will enhance user trust by providing a consistent reference point. This reduces cognitive load on the wearer, allowing them to focus more on the conversation rather than where to find the subtitles.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one default subtitle placement rule for V1: bottom center of the frame.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

A clear and concise confidence display helps maintain a balance between rich assistance and privacy concerns.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By clearly displaying confidence, users can better understand when they should trust the information provided by the glasses, leading to more effective use in noisy environments and memory-challenged situations.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a local cache threshold for name recall on the phone, we can ensure that frequently encountered names are remembered more reliably and quickly retrieved during live interactions. This improves the trustworthiness of memory support, aligning with our "memory_trust" attractor.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit for frequently seen names on the phone to be 5-10 entries that are refreshed every hour. This ensures that common names remain fresh without overwhelming the phone's storage or battery.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This caching strategy will reduce latency in recalling familiar names, leading to more accurate and faster memory support during live interactions. It also aligns with our "memory_trust" attractor by ensuring that frequent encounters are handled efficiently and effectively.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define a local cache size limit for frequently seen names on the phone as 10 entries refreshed every hour.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear boundary for what stays phone-local versus what falls back to cloud for live subtitle processing, we ensure that subtitles remain stable and near-real-time. This stability is crucial for building trust in real-world interactions, where delay or lag can significantly impact understanding.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one cache eviction rule for face/name memory, ensuring that the most recently seen names are kept local to minimize latency during live subtitle processing while keeping older memories in cloud storage.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach will reduce the latency of name recall and ensure that the most frequently encountered names are available locally, enhancing the speed and reliability of subtitle generation. It also helps maintain battery efficiency by offloading less critical tasks to the cloud.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.
