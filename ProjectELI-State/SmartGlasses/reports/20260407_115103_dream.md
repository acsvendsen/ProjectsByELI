### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving subtitle placement rules under different confidence states will enhance the wearer's ability to understand the conversation in noisy environments without becoming distracting. Stable and strategically placed subtitles will maintain trust and help the wearer stay present, ensuring that the assistive behavior is both useful and unobtrusive.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement rules: one for high-confidence matches (e.g., familiar names or faces) where subtitles are shown prominently but briefly, and another for lower-confidence matches (e.g., new people or weakly recognized entities) where subtitles are shown discreetly and persistently.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing these two subtitle placement rules, the wearer will have a clearer understanding of who is speaking in real-time conversations, reducing cognitive load and increasing trust in the assistive behavior. This will make the product more useful in noisy environments and during dense interactions, where quick recognition is crucial.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the confidence display format ensures that the wearer can trust the system's output more consistently. A clear and concise confidence score, label, and reason will help the wearer understand when they should rely on the subtitles or memory support and when to seek clarification.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple but informative confidence format: use a numeric score (e.g., 0-1), a short label (e.g., "High", "Medium", "Low"), and a brief reason (e.g., "Seen recently", "Weak match").

3. **Likely payoff**

   A well-defined confidence display will enhance the wearer's trust in the system, reduce social awkwardness, and ensure that the wearer can make informed decisions about when to rely on the subtitles or memory support.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear boundary for which tasks run phone-local versus those that fall back to cloud, we ensure that V1 focuses on delivering essential, low-latency assistance without overloading the glasses or draining the battery. This helps maintain trust and real-time responsiveness, aligning with our core attractors of subtitle clarity and memory support.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific task for which phone-local processing is critical, such as live subtitle processing. For example, set a policy where all real-time subtitle rendering happens on the glasses, while cloud fallbacks are limited to tasks like face/name recall lookup or memory archive sync.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping live subtitles phone-local, we ensure they remain clear and stable, reinforcing trust in our core attractor of "real-time subtitle clarity." This also allows for faster processing, reducing battery drain and ensuring real-time responsiveness even when cloud resources are limited.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that subtitles are clearly visible without becoming distracting helps maintain the wearer's focus on the conversation while still providing necessary assistance. A well-defined visual hierarchy ensures that critical information is prioritized and displayed prominently, which enhances the overall user experience.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a subtitle-vs-one-line priority rule that gives subtitles higher precedence in the display order when they are needed for understanding the conversation but suppresses them slightly when one-line prompts are present to avoid visual overload.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By prioritizing subtitles over one-line support, we ensure that the wearer's primary need—understanding spoken words—is met without overwhelming their field of view with too much text. This balance improves the trust and reliability of the subtitle feature while maintaining a low-friction interface.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
