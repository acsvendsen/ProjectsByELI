### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Stable subtitle placement rules are crucial for maintaining a consistent and non-distracting user experience. This helps the wearer trust the subtitles more, ensuring they remain focused on the conversation rather than the display. By defining specific subtitle placement modes that prioritize readability without becoming intrusive, we can enhance the core mission of providing real-time, clear assistance.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define and implement two basic subtitle placement rules: one for high-confidence states (e.g., when the system is highly confident about the speaker) and another for lower-confidence states (where the system might need to be more cautious).

3. **Likely payoff**

   By creating distinct placement rules based on confidence levels, we can ensure that subtitles are always visible and clear but not overly distracting. This will lead to a more reliable assistive experience, potentially increasing user trust and satisfaction.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving confidence display helps establish trust in the system's memory and assistance behavior. Clear and honest confidence indicators ensure that the wearer understands when they can rely on the glasses' memory or when additional verification is needed.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a concise confidence format that combines a numeric score, label, and short reason for each piece of information (e.g., names, context). This allows the system to show varying levels of certainty without overwhelming the wearer with excessive details.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing transparent and clear confidence indicators, the glasses can build trust over time. Users will be more willing to rely on the glasses for critical memory support if they understand when the system is confident versus uncertain. This enhances the overall reliability and perceived value of the product.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define a confidence score format using a numeric scale from 0 to 100 for V1, with corresponding labels like "High," "Medium," or "Low" and concise reasons for each piece of information displayed.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

Improving memory cache policies ensures that relevant information is readily available to the wearer, enhancing trust in the system's ability to recall names, faces, and prior context without unnecessary invasiveness or delay.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

Define a local memory threshold for face and name recall, limiting how many recent entities are cached on the phone before evicting less frequently encountered ones.

3. **Likely payoff**

This approach balances memory richness with real-world usability by ensuring that commonly seen individuals are always recalled quickly while still maintaining battery efficiency and privacy. It allows the system to provide timely assistance without overwhelming the cache, thereby improving overall trust and usefulness.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving subtitle clarity by keeping subtitles stable and fast enough ensures that the wearer can trust them during live conversations. This reduces cognitive load and social awkwardness, making the assistant more effective without overloading the user.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement rules: one for high-confidence speakers and another for low-confidence or background speakers. For example:
   
   - High-confidence speaker: Place subtitles centrally and prominently.
   - Low-confidence or background speaker: Place subtitles slightly off-center or in a less dominant position.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By clearly distinguishing between high- and low-confidence speakers, the wearer can focus on the most critical speech while still catching important details from less reliable sources. This enhances overall understanding and trust in the subtitle functionality.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the subtitle-vs-one-line priority rule ensures that subtitles remain clear and stable enough to be trusted in live conversations while also delivering necessary one-line support without overwhelming the wearer. This balance enhances real-time subtitle clarity (subtitle_clarity attractor) and low-friction assistance (low_friction_assistance attractor), making it easier for the wearer to understand and respond effectively.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a visual hierarchy where subtitles have higher precedence than one-line prompts when both are present, but allow one-line support to coexist in an unobtrusive way that does not distract from subtitle reading. This can be achieved by setting the subtitle priority rule such that subtitles remain at the top of the display unless the one-line prompt is critical and needs immediate attention.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By ensuring subtitles always take precedence, the wearer will trust the subtitles more during live conversations, reducing cognitive load and improving overall understanding. The coexistence of one-line prompts in a less intrusive manner will also provide useful support without distracting from the primary task of reading subtitles.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
