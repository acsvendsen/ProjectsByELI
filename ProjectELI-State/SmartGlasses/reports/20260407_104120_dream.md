### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By establishing stable subtitle placement rules based on confidence levels, we ensure that subtitles remain readable and unobtrusive while providing reliable assistance in noisy environments. This approach enhances the wearer's ability to understand conversations without feeling overwhelmed by too much text.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define three subtitle placement modes: 
   - **High Confidence**: Subtitles appear directly above or below the speaker.
   - **Medium Confidence**: Subtitles appear off-center, but still within the field of view.
   - **Low Confidence**: Subtitles are cached in a sidebar for quick reference.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will improve subtitle clarity and reduce cognitive load by placing subtitles where they are most likely to be noticed and understood. It also allows for more flexible placement without overwhelming the wearer, thereby enhancing real-time understanding in noisy environments.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   - By refining the confidence display format, we can ensure that the wearer understands the reliability of the information provided by the system. This will build trust and improve the overall user experience, as the wearer will be better informed about when to take action based on the system's output.

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   - Define a consistent confidence format for all types of feedback, including subtitles, memory recall, and one-line prompts. The format should include a numeric score (e.g., 0-1), a label indicating high, medium, or low confidence, and a short reason explaining the basis for the confidence level.

3. **Likely payoff**
   - A clear and consistent confidence display will help users understand when they can rely on the system's output, thereby reducing cognitive load and increasing trust in the technology. This will enhance the overall user experience and ensure that the wearer is better informed about the reliability of the information provided by the system.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define the numeric score for the confidence display format.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

Improving local memory thresholds for face/name recall ensures that critical information is retained on the phone without overwhelming the system's resources or compromising privacy. This enhances trust by providing quick and reliable access to key context, which is crucial for maintaining real-time subtitle quality and ensuring the wearer can understand conversations effectively.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

Define a local cache size limit of 50 entries for face/name memory objects on the phone. This ensures that frequently encountered names and faces are cached locally while reducing the risk of overloading the device's memory, which could impact subtitle performance or battery life.

3. **Likely payoff**

By setting a clear cache size limit, the system will maintain fast access to common entities during live interactions without unnecessary data fetching from the cloud. This reduces latency in name recall and enhances overall trust by ensuring that key context is always available when needed.

4. **Immediate next probe**

Define one local-cache size limit of 50 entries for face/name memory objects on the phone, prioritizing frequent encounters over less common ones.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that subtitle processing is handled phone-first for critical tasks improves real-time performance and trust. By keeping live subtitle processing on the phone, we can maintain faster response times and more stable behavior, which are crucial for the wearer's understanding in noisy environments.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific task boundary where subtitle processing must remain phone-local. For example, set a rule that subtitles for live conversations with high confidence should always be processed on the phone to ensure minimal latency and stability.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping critical subtitle tasks local, we reduce the risk of dropped connections or cloud latencies affecting real-time clarity. This ensures that the wearer can trust the subtitles during important interactions, enhancing their overall understanding and engagement.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support enhances the wearer's ability to focus on critical information without overwhelming them with too much text. This improves daily-use value by ensuring that the most important information (subtitles) remains prominent, while secondary assistance (one-line prompts) is presented in a way that does not distract from the main conversation.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a visual priority rule where subtitles are always displayed prominently, and one-line support text is hidden or suppressed when the subtitle area is full, but still cached locally for quick retrieval if needed.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By clearly prioritizing subtitles over other text elements, the wearer can remain focused on the main conversation while still having access to essential prompts when necessary. This improves overall comprehension and reduces cognitive load, leading to a more natural and effective assistive experience.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
