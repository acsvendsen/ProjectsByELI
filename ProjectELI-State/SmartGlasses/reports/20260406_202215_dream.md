### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   Enhancing subtitle placement rules ensures that subtitles remain clear, readable, and stable enough to be trusted in live conversations. Stable placement prevents distractions and maintains focus on the conversation.

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   Define a fixed subtitle placement rule for high-confidence states where subtitles should appear at the bottom center of the glasses display, with minimal movement during speech.

3. **Likely payoff**
   By keeping subtitles stable in high-confidence states, users can rely more on the system without visual distractions, reducing cognitive load and increasing trust in real-time assistance.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the confidence display format helps ensure that the wearer can trust the memory and subtitle assistance provided by the glasses, thereby enhancing overall usability and trustworthiness.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple confidence format: `score 0.85 + "High" + "Seen recently"` for existing memories and `score 0.34 + "Low" + "Weak match"` for new or uncertain memories.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing clear and concise certainty indicators, the system can help the wearer better understand the reliability of the information presented. This reduces uncertainty and enhances trust in the glasses' memory support, leading to more effective use.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the memory cache policy ensures that frequently accessed information remains readily available, enhancing the wearer's confidence in the system’s reliability and accuracy. This is crucial for maintaining a high level of trust and ensuring that critical interactions are supported effectively.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple local cache eviction rule based on recency and frequency: prioritize keeping entities like names and recent conversations in memory, with a fallback to the phone when necessary but only after a defined threshold of inactivity or new evidence.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By focusing on recency and frequency, the system will more effectively support rapid lookups during conversations, reducing latency and improving overall user experience. This will also help manage battery and thermal constraints by minimizing frequent trips to the cloud.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining clear boundaries for what stays phone-local versus what falls back to cloud, we ensure that the system remains responsive and privacy-preserving. This balance is crucial for maintaining trust and ensuring that real-time subtitle assistance is fast enough and reliable.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one specific task boundary where the system decides whether to process live subtitles locally or fallback to the cloud. For example, choose a threshold for confidence levels below which the glasses should request cloud processing to ensure high-quality subtitles.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By setting this boundary, we can achieve faster subtitle processing times while maintaining privacy and reducing bandwidth usage. This will enhance user trust in the system's reliability and responsiveness during live conversations.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear visual hierarchy rule between subtitles and one-line prompts, we ensure that the wearer can focus on the most critical information while maintaining a low-friction experience. This rule helps in preventing overwhelming the wearer with too much text at once, thus enhancing overall understanding and reducing cognitive load.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a single visual priority rule stating that subtitles should be shown prominently when their confidence score is above 0.8, while one-line prompts are shown subtly or suppressed when there is high certainty in the subtitle content.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This rule will ensure that critical information (high-confidence subtitles) remains visible and legible, while less certain prompts do not distract the wearer, leading to a more focused and efficient interaction experience. It will also help in maintaining the wearer's trust by avoiding unnecessary visual clutter.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
