### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving subtitle placement rules helps ensure that subtitles are always clear and readable in noisy environments, which directly supports the primary attractor of real-time subtitle clarity. Stable and predictable subtitle placement reduces visual distractions and enhances trust in the assistive system.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement modes: one for high-confidence speech (e.g., when the recognizer is very certain) and another for low-confidence speech (e.g., when the recognizer is uncertain). This allows subtitles to remain stable and unobtrusive in familiar contexts while still appearing quickly during new or uncertain speech.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing two subtitle placement modes, the system will provide a clearer visual experience for both the wearer and bystanders. This reduces cognitive load and maintains trust by ensuring that critical information is always easily readable, even in complex social situations.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving confidence display ensures that the wearer can trust the system's output by clearly indicating the certainty level of the provided information. This enhances the overall user experience, builds trust, and supports the core mission of delivering reliable real-time subtitle assistance.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a confidence format that uses a numeric score plus a label and a short reason for each piece of displayed information. For example: "score 0.85 + 'High' + 'Seen recently in this conversation'".

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing a clear and concise confidence display, the system will provide more reliable and trustworthy subtitle support, reducing cognitive load on the wearer and increasing their trust in the product.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the local memory threshold for face/name recall helps maintain a balance between privacy and usefulness by ensuring that names and faces are remembered reliably but not stored indefinitely on-device. This aligns with the project's core mission to provide reliable, low-friction support without invading the user's personal space.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit for face/name memories that is large enough to handle typical social interactions but small enough to avoid storing indefinite memory traces. For instance, limit the number of recent face/name encounters stored locally to 10-20 entries with automatic removal after 7 days.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By setting a specific local cache size limit for face/name memories, the system can provide reliable support for recent interactions while respecting user privacy and reducing storage overhead. This balance enhances trust through consistent behavior and avoids overloading the device's memory, thus preserving battery life and performance.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one local-cache size limit of 15 entries with automatic removal after 7 days for face/name memories.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By carefully defining the boundary between what stays phone-local versus what falls back to cloud for live subtitle processing, we can ensure that critical and real-time subtitle quality remains stable and responsive on the device. This improves trust in the system by guaranteeing near-instant feedback, which is crucial for understanding during live conversations.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a single task-specific fallback boundary: for live subtitle processing, use phone-first architecture with cloud as an optional fallback for complex or ambiguous cases where local processing would otherwise fail due to latency constraints.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping critical subtitle processing on the phone, we can maintain real-time performance and ensure that the subtitles remain stable and reliable during live interactions. This reduces the risk of dropped or delayed text, which could break trust with the wearer.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

Improving the visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support helps ensure that key information is always clear and prominent while minimizing distractions. This enhances the wearer's ability to follow conversations, remember important names or faces, and respond appropriately without feeling overwhelmed by too much text.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

Define a prioritization rule that places high-confidence subtitles over one-line prompts when both are available simultaneously. This ensures that critical information is not overshadowed and remains the primary focus of the wearer's attention.

3. **Likely payoff**

By ensuring that high-confidence subtitles take precedence, the system will provide clearer, more useful information during live conversations. This reduces cognitive load for the user by maintaining a clear visual hierarchy and improves their ability to follow the conversation naturally.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
