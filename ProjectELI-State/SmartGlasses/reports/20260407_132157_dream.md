### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining specific subtitle placement rules based on confidence levels, we can ensure that subtitles remain clear and non-distracting while also providing necessary support. This helps in maintaining the wearer's focus during conversations and ensures that critical information is always visible.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple rule where subtitles are placed at the bottom center of the frame for low-confidence matches, and slightly above center for high-confidence matches. This minimal change will guide the placement based on certainty without overwhelming the wearer with too many subtitles.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing this rule, we can reduce subtitle clutter while ensuring that important information is not missed. This leads to a more reliable assistive experience that builds trust and reduces cognitive load for the wearer.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the confidence display format ensures that the wearer can trust the subtitles and memory support more effectively. A clear and consistent confidence label and short reason help the user understand the reliability of the information being presented, which is crucial for maintaining the system's utility and integrity.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple yet effective confidence format for V1: use a numeric score (e.g., 0-1) with a corresponding label ("High", "Medium", "Low") and a short reason explaining why the confidence level is as such. For example, a score of 0.84 + "High" + "Seen recently".

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing clear and concise confidence information, V1 can build trust with the wearer. This will make the system more reliable in noisy environments and during critical conversations, where accurate understanding is essential.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving local memory thresholds ensures that frequently encountered faces and names are cached more effectively on the phone, reducing reliance on cloud lookups and improving response times. This enhances trust by ensuring that critical information is readily available to the wearer without latency or privacy concerns.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit for face and name memory objects, with an eviction policy that prioritizes recently seen and frequently encountered entities over older ones. This can be set as a fixed number of top-ranked entries or based on a recency score to ensure the most relevant memories are stored locally.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By optimizing local memory thresholds, V1 will have faster recall for common names and faces, reducing latency and enhancing trust through quick and reliable assistance during interactions. This improves the wearer's experience by making the product feel more responsive and personal without overloading the cloud or draining battery resources.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring a clear visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support helps maintain low-friction assistance while preventing overload. By defining rules for when to prioritize subtitles over one-line prompts, we can ensure the wearer remains focused on essential information without being overwhelmed.

3. **Likely payoff**
