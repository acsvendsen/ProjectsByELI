### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   - By refining subtitle placement rules based on confidence states, we can ensure that subtitles are more readable and less distracting in noisy environments. This improves the wearer's ability to trust the subtitles during live conversations.

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   - Define two distinct subtitle placement modes: one for high-confidence cases (e.g., well-known speakers or common phrases) and another for low-confidence cases (e.g., new faces or unclear speech).

3. **Likely payoff**
   - This refined placement will reduce visual clutter, making subtitles more readable and less distracting. It will also help in maintaining the wearer's focus during conversations by ensuring that important information is presented clearly.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the confidence display format helps establish trust in the system's memory recall and subtitle accuracy. A clear and consistent confidence indication ensures that the wearer understands the reliability of the information provided, which is crucial for building long-term user trust.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple yet effective confidence object format that includes both a numeric score and a short reason why the system is certain or uncertain about the memory recall or subtitle content.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing clear and transparent confidence indicators, the system can reduce user confusion and build trust over time. This will make the wearer more likely to rely on the system for critical information in noisy environments or dense social situations.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that face/name memory is cached locally on the phone with a well-defined eviction policy helps maintain trust by providing quick recall without relying on cloud latency, thus supporting the core attractor of "Trustworthy memory support." This reduces dependency on network availability and ensures that crucial information is always accessible.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit for face/name memory objects, with an eviction rule based on recency and confidence scores. For example, keep the most recent 10 interactions per person, evicting less certain memories first when the cache reaches its threshold.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will allow the system to quickly recall names and faces even in low-bandwidth or offline scenarios, enhancing the wearer's ability to understand conversations without delay. It also reduces battery usage by minimizing cloud requests for commonly accessed information.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define a local cache size limit of 10 face/name interactions per person.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that live subtitle processing remains local on the phone allows V1 to leverage phone resources efficiently without overloading the battery or thermal limits. This focus on phone-first runtime helps in maintaining real-time clarity and stability of subtitles, which is critical for the wearer's trust and understanding.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a clear policy that live subtitle processing remains local on the phone unless explicit cloud fallbacks are needed due to complex processing demands or latency constraints.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping subtitle processing local, V1 can ensure subtitles remain fast and stable, reducing battery drain and thermal stress. This will help maintain real-time clarity and trust, enhancing daily-use value for the wearer.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support ensures that the wearer can focus on the most critical information without being overwhelmed. By clearly prioritizing subtitles over one-line prompts, we enhance the wearer's ability to understand spoken content in noisy environments while still providing necessary contextual cues.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a clear subtitle-vs-one-line priority rule where subtitles are always given precedence and prominence, with one-line support visible but not intrusive. This can be achieved by setting a visual hierarchy policy that routes subtitles through a higher-priority display channel than one-line prompts.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By ensuring that subtitles remain the primary focus, the system will provide clearer and more reliable real-time assistance in noisy environments. This clarity will build trust with users and improve their overall understanding during conversations. Additionally, by keeping one-line support less prominent, we reduce visual clutter, which can be distracting and overwhelm the wearer.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
