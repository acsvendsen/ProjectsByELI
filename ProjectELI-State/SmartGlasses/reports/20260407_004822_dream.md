### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving subtitle placement rules to ensure stability and clarity will enhance the wearer's trust in real-time subtitles, reducing cognitive load and improving overall understanding during live conversations.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a fixed subtitle placement rule that centers subtitles at the bottom center of the screen, ensuring they do not obstruct critical visual information while remaining visible and readable.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By maintaining a consistent, unobtrusive position for subtitles, the wearer can focus more on the conversation without being distracted by moving text or conflicting with other important visual elements, thus improving overall usability and trust in real-time subtitles.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the confidence display format will enhance the wearer's trust in the system by clearly indicating the reliability of each piece of information provided, whether it be a subtitle or memory recall. This clarity helps maintain the balance between providing enough support and avoiding overconfidence that could lead to misunderstandings.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one confidence format candidate for V1 that includes both a label and a short reason, such as "score 0.84 + 'High' + 'Seen recently'; score 0.42 + 'Low' + 'Weak match'. This format provides the wearer with immediate context about why a piece of information is being presented.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By clearly labeling the confidence of each subtitle and memory recall, the system will reduce cognitive load by ensuring that the wearer understands the reliability of the information they are receiving. This clarity can prevent misunderstandings and enhance overall trust in the system's accuracy, which is crucial for real-time communication support.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define the label style for the confidence display as "High" or "Low".

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a local cache threshold for face/name memory, the system ensures that critical information is readily available without overloading the device's limited storage and processing capabilities. This approach maintains trust by providing quick recall of important people and contexts while respecting privacy constraints.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit for face/name memories on the phone, ensuring that only recently encountered or frequently mentioned individuals are stored locally for fast lookup.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This small change will enhance the system's responsiveness to new people and contexts by allowing quick recall of recent encounters without consuming excessive resources. It also supports the core mission by maintaining a balance between useful memory support and privacy concerns.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one local-cache size limit for face/name memories, with a preference for retaining entities seen within the last week, prioritizing those encountered most frequently.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear phone/cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing, we ensure that subtitles remain stable and reliable in real-time interactions. This approach minimizes latency and ensures that critical information is always available when needed, even if cloud services are temporarily unavailable.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one task-specific phone/cloud fallback boundary rule for exactly one task such as live subtitle processing. For instance, decide to use local processing on the phone for subtitles unless explicit user input requests a cloud fallback or if there is a critical network outage.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This definition will help maintain high-quality, real-time subtitles in noisy environments where reliability and speed are crucial. It ensures that the system remains usable even when internet connectivity is poor, thereby maintaining trust with the wearer.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one phone-local subtitle processing rule for live interactions: 
   - Use local processing unless explicit user input requests a cloud fallback or if there is a critical network outage.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring a clear visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support helps maintain the wearer's focus on critical information, reducing cognitive load and enhancing overall understanding without overwhelming them with too much data.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a visual priority rule that routes one-line prompts to an overlay or corner of the display, while maintaining clear and stable subtitle placement at the center or top of the field of view.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By defining this visual hierarchy, the system can ensure that subtitles remain the primary focus, providing the wearer with reliable real-time information. One-line prompts will be seen but not distract from the core task, leading to a more intuitive and effective assistive experience.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support where subtitles always take precedence in the central or top display area, while one-line prompts are shown in an overlay at the periphery.
