### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring stable subtitle placement rules helps maintain the core attractor of real-time subtitle clarity. By defining fixed position and mode rules, we can reduce the risk of subtitles jumping or disappearing, which would break trust in live conversations.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two fixed subtitle placement modes: one for high-confidence scenarios (e.g., clear hearing conditions) where subtitles should be displayed prominently but not distractively, and another for low-confidence scenarios (e.g., noisy environments) where more stable and easily readable positions are critical.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing these fixed placement rules, the system will provide a consistent and reliable visual experience, reducing distraction and maintaining trust during conversations in various environmental conditions.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining how confidence is displayed in real-time subtitles, we can ensure that the wearer understands the reliability of the information presented. This improves trust and the overall assistive value by making sure the wearer knows when to rely on the subtitles and when additional verification might be necessary.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple confidence format for V1: `score 0.85 + "High" + "Seen recently"`.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By clearly indicating high-confidence information, wearers will have more trust in the subtitles during critical moments of conversation, reducing cognitive load and increasing their ability to focus on the interaction.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a clear local cache threshold for face/name memory on the phone, we ensure that frequently encountered names and faces are quickly accessible, reducing latency and improving the user's trust in the system. This helps maintain real-time subtitle clarity and memory support without overburdening the cloud.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache threshold for face/name memory on the phone that retains recently seen names and faces for fast lookup. This involves setting a recency and frequency-based eviction policy to keep the most relevant memories in local storage.

3. **Likely payoff**

   The likely payoff is improved real-time subtitle clarity and faster recall of frequently encountered names, which enhances the user's confidence in the system’s memory support. This reduces the need for frequent cloud requests, conserving battery and improving overall usability.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define a local cache threshold that retains recently seen faces and names on the phone for fast lookup, with a recency limit of 24 hours and a frequency limit of 5 encounters in the last week.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear boundary between what runs on the phone locally and what falls back to the cloud for specific tasks, we can ensure that V1 remains focused on delivering high-value, low-latency subtitle assistance and memory support while respecting battery, thermal, and privacy constraints.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one task-specific cloud fallback boundary, such as live subtitle processing or face/name recall lookup, to be handled primarily locally where possible but falling back to the cloud for critical heavy lifting only when necessary.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This will help maintain a low-friction user experience by ensuring that real-time subtitle rendering and basic memory support can happen almost instantly on the phone. It also allows us to leverage the cloud for more complex tasks without sacrificing responsiveness or draining battery, thereby strengthening trust in the system's reliability.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining the visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support, we can ensure that critical information is presented in a clear and unambiguous way, reducing cognitive load and social friction for the wearer. This will help maintain the wearer's focus on the conversation without overwhelming them with too much information.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a subtitle-vs-one-line priority rule where subtitles always take precedence over one-line support unless the one-line content is crucially important, such as an urgent reminder or a highly contextual prompt that directly relates to the current topic of discussion.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will result in a more focused and less distracting user experience. The wearer can trust the subtitles while still receiving necessary one-line prompts when needed, leading to better understanding and engagement without feeling overloaded by information.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
