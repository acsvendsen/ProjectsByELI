### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   - By defining specific subtitle placement rules based on confidence states, we can ensure that subtitles remain clear and stable while minimizing distraction. This will help maintain trust in the system by providing reliable information when needed most.

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   - Define two subtitle placement rules: one for high-confidence scenarios where subtitles should be visible but non-obtrusive, and another for low-confidence scenarios where subtitles can remain hidden to avoid potential distraction.

3. **Likely payoff**
   - This will enhance the system's ability to provide timely and reliable subtitle support without overwhelming the wearer. It will improve overall user trust by ensuring that critical information is always visible when needed most, while minimizing visual clutter in less critical situations.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

3. **Likely payoff**

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a local cache size limit for face/name memory, we ensure that the system can quickly recall and show names of people encountered multiple times without relying on cloud lookups, thereby reducing latency and improving the real-time responsiveness and trustworthiness of name recognition.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size threshold where names seen more than \(X\) times are stored in a fast-access index on the phone, with a maximum capacity limit to avoid overloading local memory.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This will enable faster and more reliable face/name recall during conversations, reducing the need for cloud lookups that could introduce latency or privacy concerns. It also ensures that only frequently encountered names are cached locally, keeping the cache size manageable and preserving battery life.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one local-cache size limit for face/name memory, with a maximum capacity of 50 entries to balance between fast recall and limited storage.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that memory handling is stored locally on the phone rather than falling back to the cloud can improve privacy and reduce latency, which are critical for a product focused on discreet real-time assistance.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache eviction policy where new-person memories are cached only on the phone until they have been reinforced multiple times by repeated encounters.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach will enhance privacy by minimizing data sent to the cloud, and it will also reduce latency since memory lookups can be performed locally without network delay, improving the responsiveness of name recall during conversations.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring a clear visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line prompts enhances the wearer's ability to focus on essential information without overwhelming them with too much text at once. This clarity supports both subtitle clarity (real-time, readable, stable) and low-friction assistance by providing just enough context for effective communication.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a visual priority rule that shows subtitles prominently when they are needed but suppresses one-line prompts during active listening or speaking phases to avoid distraction.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This rule will reduce cognitive load and increase the chances of the wearer trusting the subtitles, thereby improving overall understanding in noisy environments. It also minimizes social awkwardness by preventing one-line prompts from interrupting the flow of conversation.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support to prioritize subtitle clarity during active listening phases.
