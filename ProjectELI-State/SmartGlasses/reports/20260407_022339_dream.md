### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving subtitle placement rules can enhance the wearer's ability to read subtitles clearly without becoming distracted. Stable and strategic subtitle placement ensures that critical information is easily visible, reducing cognitive load during conversations.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple fixed-position rule for subtitles: place them at the bottom center of the field of view (FOV), with a slight vertical offset to avoid overlapping with face recognition frames or other UI elements.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping subtitles in a consistent, unobtrusive location, the wearer can focus more on the conversation and less on trying to find and read subtitles. This improves overall understanding and trust in the system's ability to provide real-time assistance.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define the default subtitle placement rule to be fixed at the bottom center of the field of view with a slight vertical offset.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By optimizing the confidence display format to clearly communicate certainty and source, the system can enhance trust in both subtitle accuracy and memory recall. Clear confidence indicators ensure that the wearer knows when they are relying on highly certain information versus less reliable data, which is crucial for maintaining the core mission of helping the wearer understand real-world interactions without deceiving them.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Introduce a confidence format that combines a numeric score with a label and a short reason, e.g., "score 0.84 + 'High' + 'Seen recently'; score 0.42 + 'Low' + 'Weak match'; score 0.63 + 'Medium' + 'Name heard once in introduction'."

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will provide the wearer with a clear, non-narrative way to understand the reliability of the system's output. It will enhance trust by making it transparent when the system is certain about its information and when there is more uncertainty. This clarity can reduce cognitive load for the wearer, allowing them to focus on understanding rather than questioning the accuracy of each piece of information.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the local cache threshold for face/name memory ensures that frequently encountered individuals are remembered with high confidence and recalled quickly, enhancing the wearer's trust in the system's ability to support them effectively.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache eviction policy where recently seen faces/names are kept in the phone's local memory for at least 7 days before being evicted based on recency.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By ensuring that commonly encountered individuals remain in the wearer’s local memory, the system can provide faster and more accurate recall, reducing the need for cloud interactions and improving overall trust and reliability.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Set a local cache eviction rule where faces/names seen within the last 7 days are retained locally for at least 30 days before being eligible for eviction.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear boundary for what stays phone-local versus what falls back to cloud, we can ensure that V1 remains focused on providing real-time, low-latency subtitle assistance and memory support without overloading the glasses hardware. This improves the overall user experience by maintaining high responsiveness and reducing thermal and battery strain.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a single task-specific cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing. Specifically, set a rule that subtitles should be processed locally on the phone unless the audio input is too noisy or the glasses are in an environment with limited connectivity.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This setup will allow V1 to maintain high performance and real-time responsiveness for critical tasks like subtitles, while still leveraging cloud resources when necessary. It reduces the risk of overheating and battery drain, ensuring a smoother user experience across different environments.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Enhancing the visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support ensures that critical information is prioritized for easy reading while minimizing distraction. This focus on usability and clarity directly supports the core attractor of real-time subtitle clarity and low-friction assistance.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a clear precedence rule where subtitles take priority over one-line support in fast-moving contexts, but allow one-line prompts to override subtitles in scenarios where brief context is needed quickly (e.g., name recall during an introduction).

3. **Likely payoff**

   By optimizing the visual hierarchy, users can focus on essential information more effectively without being overwhelmed by too much text. This improvement will enhance overall user trust and satisfaction, aligning with the core attractors of real-time subtitle clarity and low-friction assistance.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define a visual hierarchy rule where subtitles take priority over one-line support in fast-moving contexts.
