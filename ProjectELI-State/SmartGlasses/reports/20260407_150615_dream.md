### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving subtitle placement rules based on confidence levels ensures that subtitles remain clear and unobtrusive while providing critical assistance when needed. This approach helps in maintaining the wearer's focus during conversations, thereby enhancing their understanding without creating visual distractions.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement rules: one for high-confidence matches (e.g., names or faces seen multiple times) where subtitles can be displayed slightly above the speaker’s mouth to avoid overlap with speaking lips but remain visible, and another for low-confidence matches (new people or less frequent encounters) where subtitles appear at a fixed position on the wearer's left side.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By adapting subtitle placement based on confidence levels, the system can provide more relevant assistance while reducing visual clutter. This enhances the overall user experience by making the subtitles useful without overwhelming the wearer with too much information.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining the confidence display to use a clear, concise format that combines numeric scores with labels and short reasons, we can enhance the wearer's trust in the system's memory and recall accuracy. This will help the wearer understand when the system is certain about something versus when there is doubt or uncertainty.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   The small change involves updating the confidence format to include a numeric score, label, and short reason for each piece of memory. For example: `score 0.84 + "High" + "Seen recently"; score 0.42 + "Low" + "Weak match"; score 0.63 + "Medium" + "Name heard once in introduction"`.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing a clear and concise confidence format, the wearer will be better able to understand the reliability of the system's memory support. This can lead to increased trust and more effective use of the memory feature, as the wearer knows when to rely on the system's recollections and when additional verification may be needed.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the local memory threshold for face/name recall ensures that frequently encountered individuals are remembered more reliably and faster, enhancing trust in the system’s ability to assist with names and faces. This will help maintain a consistent level of confidence without overloading the phone's memory, thereby preserving battery life and thermal management.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit for face/name memories that balances frequent encounters with less common ones. For example, set a threshold to keep up to 50 recent faces in the cache based on their frequency of appearance.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By setting this local cache size limit, the system can maintain a high level of trust through fast and accurate face/name recall while ensuring that memory usage remains efficient. This will enhance the overall user experience by providing quick access to commonly encountered individuals without unnecessary data storage overhead.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define the local memory threshold for face/name recall to keep up to 50 recent faces in the cache based on their frequency of appearance.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear boundary for what stays phone-local versus falling back to cloud for live subtitle processing, we ensure that subtitles remain real-time and stable. This helps in maintaining high trust among wearers by ensuring that their experience is not delayed or impacted by network conditions.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one specific task-based fallback boundary: "Live subtitle processing should stay phone-local if the transcription delay is less than 500ms, and fall back to cloud only if the delay exceeds this threshold."

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping live subtitles as a phone-first task, we reduce latency and ensure that the wearer has a smooth, reliable experience without interruptions from network conditions or cloud processing delays. This aligns with the core attractor of real-time subtitle clarity.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear visual hierarchy rule between subtitles and one-line support, we ensure that the wearer can focus on critical information without being overwhelmed by too much text. This enhances the overall clarity and usability of the display, which is crucial for maintaining trust and reducing cognitive load in real-world interactions.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a priority rule that gives subtitles higher precedence over one-line support when both are active. For example, prioritize subtitles with a confidence score above 0.75 while allowing one-line prompts to appear at the bottom of the display but not overlap or obscure subtitles.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will improve the wearer's ability to follow live conversations by ensuring that key information (subtitles) remains prominent and unobstructed, even when less critical text is present. It will also help in managing visual complexity without sacrificing real-time subtitle clarity, which is a core attractor for the project.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
