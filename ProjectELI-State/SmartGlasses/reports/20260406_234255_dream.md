### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving subtitle placement rules under different confidence states ensures that subtitles remain clear, stable, and readable in live conversation. This enhances trust by making sure the wearer can rely on the subtitles for understanding and context.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define 3 to 5 subtitle placement rules based on confidence levels: high (always visible), medium (adjustable visibility), low (temporarily hidden until confirmed). 

3. **Likely payoff**

   Better subtitle placement will reduce cognitive load by ensuring that subtitles are always in a readable position, thus improving the wearer's understanding and trust in the system.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the confidence display format in V1 ensures that the wearer can trust the system's output more consistently. By clearly showing certainty levels, the glasses help the wearer understand when to rely on the subtitles and when they might need to focus more on the conversation or visual context.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a confidence object format that includes both a numeric score and a short reason for the accuracy of the subtitle placement. For example: "score 0.85 + 'Recent match'".

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing clear, actionable confidence information, the glasses can reduce cognitive load on the wearer by making them aware when the system is most certain about its output. This improves overall trust and usability in noisy environments.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define the format for presenting confidence scores: high (≥ 0.8) as "Very Confident", medium (0.6 to < 0.8) as "Somewhat Confident", and low (< 0.6) as "Less Confident".

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the local memory cache size helps ensure that critical face and name memories are consistently available to the wearer without undue battery drain or thermal stress. By optimizing this threshold, we can maintain a balance between useful memory recall and efficient use of resources.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit for frequently encountered faces and names, ensuring that these entities are cached on the phone while minimizing overall memory usage.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By setting an optimal local cache size, we can enhance the wearer's experience by providing more reliable recall of important people without significantly impacting battery life or thermal management. This improvement will strengthen trust through consistent and timely memory support.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Set a local cache size limit for frequently encountered faces and names to 50 entities, ensuring fast lookup and reinforcement over time.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that subtitles are processed locally on the phone rather than relying on cloud latency for live subtitle processing enhances real-time clarity and trust. This local processing reduces the risk of delays or dropped connections, making the subtitles more reliable and less distracting to the wearer.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a rule where all live subtitle processing is done locally on the phone, with minimal fallback to cloud if necessary for heavy computation but ensuring that the live transcript always remains on-device.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping subtitles local, we ensure faster and more reliable real-time transcription, reducing latency and enhancing the wearer's trust in the assistive system. This will directly improve the core attractor of subtitle clarity and reduce the risk of dropped or delayed text, which could otherwise break the user experience.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support, we ensure that the most critical information—such as live subtitles—is always prominent and readable. This reduces cognitive load on the wearer by minimizing distractions from less important elements like contextual prompts.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple visual priority rule that keeps subtitles at the top of the display hierarchy, with one-line support below but still visible without obstruction.

3. **Likely payoff**

   Enhancing subtitle prominence will lead to higher trust in real-time captioning during conversations, reducing misunderstandings and improving overall understanding for hearing-challenged or noisy environments.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define a visual hierarchy rule that places subtitles at the top of the display, with one-line support below but still prioritized for prominence.
