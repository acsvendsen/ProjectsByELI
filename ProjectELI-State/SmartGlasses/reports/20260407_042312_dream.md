### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving subtitle placement rules ensures that subtitles remain stable, readable, and unobtrusive during live conversations. Stable placement enhances trust in the system by reducing distractions and ensuring that the wearer can focus on the conversation without glancing at the glasses too frequently.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement modes: one for high-confidence speech and another for lower confidence or non-speech audio. For high-confidence speech, place subtitles slightly above the speaker's face; for lower confidence or non-speech audio, place subtitles in a less prominent area to avoid distraction.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By defining specific subtitle placement modes based on confidence levels, the system can reduce visual distractions and maintain trust during live interactions. This improves overall user experience by ensuring that important information is clearly visible while minimizing unnecessary glances at the glasses.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving confidence display helps ensure that the wearer can trust the system's assistance, which is crucial for maintaining a low-friction, non-intrusive user experience. Clear and accurate confidence indicators prevent misunderstandings and build long-term trust in the product.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple numeric score format with labels (e.g., High, Medium, Low) and short reasons that can be shown next to subtitles or memory entries.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing clear and actionable confidence indicators, users will better understand the reliability of the system's assistance. This will lead to more consistent use and higher trust in the product, ultimately enhancing its daily-use value.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a local cache size limit for face/name memory objects, we ensure that the system retains relevant information without overwhelming the phone's storage or battery resources. This balance helps maintain trust and usefulness while staying mindful of real-world constraints.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific maximum number of face/name memories to be cached locally on the phone. For example, limit the local cache to 50 unique faces/names at any given time.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This caching rule will help reduce memory bloat and ensure that the system remains responsive and low-friction for the wearer. It also respects privacy by limiting how much data is stored locally without sacrificing the ability to recall important names and faces efficiently.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define a local cache size limit of 50 unique face/name memories for V1.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By carefully defining which subtitle processing tasks run locally on the phone versus falling back to cloud, we ensure that V1 remains lightweight and reliable while still providing essential real-time assistance. This balance is critical for maintaining trust and usability in noisy environments.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a clear rule that live subtitle processing runs entirely on the phone with minimal latency, while transcript cleanup and face/name recall lookup can fall back to cloud when needed but are prioritized based on confidence scores.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach ensures that subtitles remain stable and fast in real-time scenarios, building trust with users. It also allows for more robust memory support without compromising the core subtitle functionality, enhancing overall user experience and daily-use value.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Enhancing the visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support ensures that the wearer can focus on critical information while maintaining a low-friction, unobtrusive experience. This improves daily-use value by balancing the need for clear, real-time assistance with social acceptability.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a subtitle-vs-one-line priority rule that prefers subtitles over one-liners in high-noise scenarios and coexists peacefully in low-noise environments.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By clearly prioritizing subtitles in noisy or critical contexts, the system can maintain trust by providing reliable real-time information while reducing visual clutter, thus enhancing the overall user experience without overwhelming the wearer with unnecessary prompts.

4. **Immediate next probe**
