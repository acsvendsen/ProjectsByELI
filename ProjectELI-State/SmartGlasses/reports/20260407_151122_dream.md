### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining specific subtitle placement rules based on confidence states, we can ensure that subtitles remain stable and unobtrusive while still providing timely assistance. This will help in maintaining a balance between readability and distraction, enhancing the wearer's trust in the system.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define three subtitle placement modes: default (always on top), high confidence (slightly lower for better visibility), and low confidence (near the bottom or hidden). These rules can be applied based on the confidence score of the detected speaker.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach will reduce the likelihood of distracting the wearer with subtitles that are not highly confident, thereby maintaining focus during critical parts of the conversation. It also ensures that important information is clearly visible when needed, enhancing overall usability and trust.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

Improving the confidence display format ensures that the wearer can trust the system's output more consistently. By refining how certainty is communicated through a clear and concise label, short reason, and numeric score, the system can build stronger trust in its memory and subtitle assistance.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

Define three distinct confidence labels: "High," "Medium," and "Low." Each label will come with a short-reason format that explains why the confidence is as such. For example, "High" might be accompanied by "Seen recently," while "Low" could be "Weak match."

3. **Likely payoff**

This change will enhance the wearer's trust in the system’s memory and subtitle assistance. Clear labels and reasons for each level of confidence will make it easier for the user to understand when they can rely on the system and when additional attention is needed.

4. **Immediate next probe**

Define three distinct confidence labels: "High," "Medium," and "Low," along with their corresponding short-reasons, for use in V1.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a clear local memory threshold for name recall, we ensure that the system does not overstore information on the phone while still providing robust support for recognizing and recalling familiar names in real-time interactions.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit of 10 recent face/name encounters to be stored on the phone with confidence objects and reinforcement history.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach will balance memory storage efficiency with effective name recall, reducing battery usage and thermal strain while maintaining high trust in name recognition during interactions.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear boundary for what runs on the phone versus what falls back to the cloud, we ensure that V1 focuses on delivering real-time subtitle clarity and memory trust without overloading the glasses hardware. This helps maintain the simplicity of early hardware while ensuring the core mission is well-supported.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a single task boundary for live subtitle processing that runs entirely on the phone, with any required cloud fallbacks handled only during transcript cleanup or conversation snapshot uploads.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping live subtitle processing local, we ensure real-time response and minimal battery/thermal impact. This improves trust in the system by delivering timely and accurate subtitles, which are critical for the core mission of enhancing understanding in noisy environments.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By clearly defining how subtitles coexist with one-line prompts in terms of visual hierarchy, we ensure that the wearer can focus on critical information without feeling overwhelmed by too many elements. This enhances the overall clarity and usability of the system, aligning with the core attractor of low-friction assistance.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific subtitle-vs-one-line priority rule where subtitles have higher precedence when they are associated with high confidence levels (e.g., 0.9 or above), but one-line prompts should be shown for lower-confidence interactions to provide additional context.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This decision will help in managing the visual clutter on the glasses display, ensuring that critical information is prioritized and easier to read, which in turn improves the wearer's ability to focus and understand during conversations. It also respects the core tension of discreet UX vs visual clarity by providing a clear hierarchy.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
