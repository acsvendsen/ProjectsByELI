### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring stable subtitle placement rules helps maintain consistent and reliable subtitle visibility, which is crucial for real-time understanding and trust in live conversations. Stable placement allows the wearer to focus on the conversation without being distracted by subtitles that move erratically or block important visual cues.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement modes: one for high-confidence contexts where accuracy is paramount, and another for lower-confidence situations where flexibility can help avoid occlusion but may tolerate some jitter. For example:
   - High-confidence mode: Fixed position near the bottom of the frame with minimal movement.
   - Low-confidence mode: Dynamic position that avoids blocking critical visual elements but allows minor repositioning to maintain readability.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing consistent and predictable subtitle placement, the system can enhance trust in the subtitles, reduce cognitive load for the wearer by minimizing distractions, and ensure that important information is always accessible during high-stakes interactions such as meetings or social gatherings.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining the confidence display to use a numeric score with a label and short reason, the system can provide a more nuanced and transparent way for the wearer to understand the reliability of the displayed information. This will enhance trust by showing not just whether something is true or false but also why the system believes it.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Modify the confidence object format in the app to include a numeric score, label, and short reason. For example, display "Score 0.84 + 'High' + 'Seen recently'" for well-confirmed information or "Score 0.42 + 'Low' + 'Weak match'" for less reliable data.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will help the wearer better understand the reliability of the displayed subtitles and memory support, which in turn can reduce cognitive friction and increase trust in the system. Users will be more confident in the accuracy of their understanding during conversations.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the local memory cache threshold ensures that frequently encountered names and faces are stored in a way that maximizes fast lookup while respecting battery constraints. This helps maintain high trust in the system by ensuring quick recall of important people without unnecessary energy consumption.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit for frequently encountered names and faces, set to a value that balances rapid memory retrieval with acceptable battery usage.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By setting an optimal local cache size, the system can quickly recall important people during conversations without significant latency, enhancing user trust and satisfaction. This will also help in managing battery usage effectively, ensuring longer runtime for daily use scenarios.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define a local cache size limit of 50 entries for frequently encountered names and faces to balance fast lookup with battery conservation.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that live subtitle processing remains phone-local optimizes battery usage and latency, maintaining high-quality real-time subtitles without overloading the glasses hardware. This focus keeps the system lightweight and socially acceptable while ensuring critical functionality is robust.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a rule for live subtitle processing to stay local as long as possible, with explicit fallbacks only under high-latency conditions or when transcript cleanup requires cloud-based model enhancements that can't be cached locally.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping subtitle processing phone-local, the system reduces battery drain and maintains low latency, ensuring clear and stable subtitles for the wearer during live conversations. This improves trust and daily-use value without compromising on real-time quality.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define rules for live subtitle processing to remain phone-local unless high latency conditions exceed 50ms, forcing a fallback to cloud-based processing for real-time adjustments.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   
   Ensuring that subtitles coexist with one-line support in a way that minimizes visual overload while maintaining clarity and readability is crucial for the wearer's understanding and comfort during interactions. This balance helps in creating a low-friction, assistive experience without overwhelming the user.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a subtitle-vs-one-line priority rule that places subtitles above one-liners when both are needed simultaneously, but allows one-liners to override subtitles if they provide critical context or prompt actions more urgently.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By carefully managing the visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support, the product can maintain high clarity in critical moments while ensuring that essential prompts do not get lost amidst dense subtitle text. This improves overall usability and user trust by providing a clear focus during important interactions.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
