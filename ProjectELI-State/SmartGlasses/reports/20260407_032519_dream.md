### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring stable subtitle placement in a fixed position minimizes distraction, which is crucial for maintaining the wearer's trust and focus during live conversations. This stability enhances the wearer’s ability to follow the conversation without interruptions.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define 2-3 specific subtitle placement rules based on confidence levels: high-confidence text always appears at the bottom center of the field of view, medium-confidence text can appear in a slightly lower position but still visible, and low-confidence text should be hidden or shown as needed without blinking.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By maintaining stable and predictable subtitle placement, users will experience less distraction, leading to improved comprehension and trust in the system’s assistance. This enhances the wearer's ability to focus on the conversation, reducing cognitive load and social awkwardness.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Enhancing the confidence display format by using a numeric score + label + short reason helps build trust and clarity in the wearer's understanding of the system's certainty. This improves the overall assistive value without adding unnecessary complexity.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a consistent confidence object format: `score 0.84 + "High" + "Seen recently"; score 0.42 + "Low" + "Weak match"; score 0.63 + "Medium" + "Name heard once in introduction"`.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing a clear and concise representation of confidence, the wearer can better understand when to trust the system's memory and when it might be uncertain. This reduces social friction by making the system appear more reliable and thoughtful.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the local cache size threshold for face/name memory ensures that frequently encountered names and faces are quickly accessible, reducing latency and enhancing trust by providing timely support without burdening cloud resources.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit of 10 recent entities (names/faces) to be stored on the phone with high confidence scores.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By caching up to 10 frequently encountered names and faces locally, the system can provide fast recall during interactions, reducing latency and improving trust through quicker and more reliable memory support.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that live subtitle processing remains phone-local can enhance real-time performance, reduce latency, and maintain the wearer's trust in the system. This focus on local processing for subtitle clarity directly supports the core attractor of "Real-time subtitle clarity" by ensuring subtitles are delivered with minimal delay and high quality.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a clear boundary where live subtitle processing always runs locally, without any fallback to the cloud. For example, set a rule that all real-time speech-to-text conversions happen on-device within 10 seconds of audio capture.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping subtitle processing local, we can ensure subtitles are crisp and timely, reducing the risk of delays or errors. This will strengthen the core mission by providing reliable real-time assistance in noisy environments.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line prompts, we ensure that the wearer can focus on the most critical information without distraction. This improves overall understanding and trust in the system by making the subtitle display more reliable and less prone to overload.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a subtitle-vs-one-line priority rule where subtitles always take precedence over one-line prompts, with both displayed in distinct styles that avoid overlap. Ensure that one-line prompts are shown only when necessary and do not interfere with the readability of the subtitles.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach will enhance the wearer's ability to follow conversations by reducing visual clutter, thereby increasing trust in the subtitle display. It also aligns with the project's core mission of providing discreet, context-aware support without overwhelming the user.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one visual priority rule where subtitles always take precedence over one-line prompts, showing them in distinct styles to avoid overlap and ensure clear readability.
