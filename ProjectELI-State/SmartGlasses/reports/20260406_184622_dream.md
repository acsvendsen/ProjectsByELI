### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving subtitle placement rules for different confidence states directly enhances the core mission of providing real-time, readable subtitles that help the wearer follow conversations in noisy environments. Stable and non-distracting subtitle placement ensures that critical information is easily accessible without overwhelming or distracting the user.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define 2 to 3 subtitle placement rules for different confidence states: high confidence (likely active speaker), medium confidence (potential speakers), low confidence (background noise).

3. **Likely payoff**

   By establishing clear and stable subtitle placement rules, the system can maintain a high level of trust with users who rely on real-time subtitles. This improves overall usability and reduces cognitive load, allowing wearers to focus more on the conversation itself rather than trying to decipher ambiguous or poorly placed subtitles.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By clearly defining and standardizing confidence labels, short reasons, and numeric scores, we enhance the trustworthiness of the system's memory support. This ensures that when a new person is introduced, their name and face are remembered with appropriate uncertainty, preventing overconfidence in unknown identities.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one specific format for confidence objects: use a score ranging from 0.0 to 1.0, paired with a label (High, Medium, Low) and a short reason that captures the main evidence or context.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This format will provide a clear, consistent way to display memory trust levels. It allows the wearer to understand when new information is being stored tentatively versus confidently, reducing potential confusion and improving overall system reliability.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By optimizing the memory object cache and lookup policy, we can ensure that the system remains responsive and accurate in providing relevant context to the wearer. This directly supports the core mission of helping the wearer understand better by ensuring that relevant names, faces, and prior contexts are readily accessible.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache eviction rule based on recency for face/name memory objects, ensuring that recently encountered individuals are prioritized over older ones.

3. **Likely payoff**

   Implementing this eviction policy will reduce the likelihood of showing outdated or irrelevant information to the wearer, thereby maintaining trust and relevance in the context provided by the system.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one local-cache size limit for face/name memory that balances storage efficiency with frequent updates.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By ensuring that subtitles and face/name memory are stored locally on the phone only when necessary, we can reduce battery drain and thermal load while maintaining high trust in critical moments. This balance helps preserve the wearer's experience by keeping the glasses lightweight and socially acceptable.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache eviction rule that keeps key face/name memory objects but evicts less frequently used or lower-confidence items to maintain performance without overwhelming the phone's resources.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach will help manage the balance between providing useful contextual support and maintaining battery efficiency. It ensures that critical information is always available on-device, reducing the need for cloud-based fallbacks that could introduce latency or privacy risks.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support, we ensure that the wearer can focus on essential information without feeling overwhelmed. This helps maintain the product's goal of providing low-friction assistance while still delivering critical context.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement rules: one for high-confidence subtitles (e.g., active speaker) and another for lower confidence or less urgent information (e.g., contextual prompts). Use a distinct visual style for one-line support that ensures it does not distract from the primary subtitle content.

3. **Likely payoff**

   A well-defined visual hierarchy will reduce cognitive load, making the product more usable in noisy environments and multi-person conversations. It will also align with the core mission of providing clear, unobtrusive assistance, thereby enhancing user trust and satisfaction.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
