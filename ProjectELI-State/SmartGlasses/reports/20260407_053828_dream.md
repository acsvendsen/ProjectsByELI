### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving subtitle placement rules can enhance the wearer's ability to trust and understand the subtitles in real-time conversations. By ensuring that subtitles are consistently placed, they become a reliable aid rather than a distraction.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement modes: one for low-confidence states (e.g., when speech is hard to distinguish) and another for high-confidence states (e.g., clear speaking conditions). This will provide more stable and readable subtitles without overwhelming the wearer.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing stable subtitle placement, the system can maintain higher levels of trust and comprehension during conversations. The wearer will find it easier to follow along in noisy environments or when dealing with multiple speakers, enhancing their overall experience.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By optimizing the confidence display format to use a numeric score, label, and short reason, we can provide a clear, unambiguous indication of the system's certainty about its memory or subtitle content. This improves trust by showing the wearer exactly how certain the system is, and why that level of certainty exists.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Introduce a simple confidence format: `score 0.84 + "High" + "Seen recently"` for high-confidence cases, and `score 0.35 + "Low" + "Weak match"` for low-confidence cases. This format is concise yet informative.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing a clear confidence display, the wearer can better judge the reliability of the system’s output. High-confidence indicators will build trust when correct, while low-confidence indicators can prompt the wearer to seek clarification or ignore the information temporarily.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   - Improving the local cache size for face/name memory helps in ensuring that frequently encountered individuals are remembered more reliably with higher confidence over time, reducing the need to rely on cloud fallbacks and thus improving overall trust.

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   - Define a policy where the local cache retains 10 most recently seen faces and names with their associated confidence levels for at least 24 hours after last interaction.

3. **Likely payoff**
   - This will lead to more accurate and faster recall of common individuals, reducing the latency in providing assistance during conversations, thereby enhancing the core mission of trust and reliable memory support without overloading the cloud or causing unnecessary privacy concerns.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define a local cache retention policy where the 10 most recently seen faces and names are retained for at least 24 hours after last interaction.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear boundary for what stays phone-local versus what falls back to cloud, we can ensure that the glasses remain lightweight and socially acceptable while still delivering essential real-time subtitle assistance. This balance is crucial for maintaining trust and utility without unnecessary data transmission or processing overhead.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one task-specific fallback boundary for exactly one task such as live subtitle processing. For example, set a rule that subtitles should be processed locally on the phone whenever possible but fall back to cloud only if the local processing fails due to latency constraints.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By setting this boundary, we can ensure that the glasses remain fast and responsive in real-time scenarios, which is critical for maintaining trust and providing useful assistance without delay. This also helps manage battery and thermal constraints effectively, ensuring a smoother user experience.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one phone/cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing such that subtitles are processed locally on the phone unless latency exceeds 100ms, at which point they fall back to cloud processing.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that subtitles are coexistent with one-line support without overwhelming the wearer is crucial for maintaining trust and usability. The system should allow subtitles to provide continuous, reliable information while one-line prompts can offer timely, relevant assistance when needed but not interfere with subtitle readability.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a visual hierarchy rule that prioritizes subtitle clarity over one-line support prominence in high-noise or low-light scenarios where subtitles are most critical for comprehension.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By maintaining clear and stable subtitles, the system can ensure the wearer trusts the information provided, leading to better understanding and reduced cognitive load during conversations. Simultaneously, well-placed one-line prompts can enhance situational awareness without detracting from subtitle clarity.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
