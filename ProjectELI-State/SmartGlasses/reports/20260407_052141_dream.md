### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving subtitle placement rules helps ensure that subtitles are clear, readable, and do not distract from the conversation. Stable, well-placed subtitles enhance the wearer's ability to follow live interactions without constant eye movements or distractions, thereby maintaining focus and trust in the assistant.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a fixed subtitle placement rule for different confidence states. For example, when the confidence is high (e.g., 0.9+), place subtitles at the bottom of the screen; when the confidence is medium (e.g., 0.6-0.8), position them slightly lower but still visible; and for low-confidence scenarios (e.g., <0.6), show a brief, hidden prompt that reappears if the wearer interacts with it.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By setting clear placement rules, the subtitles will be more predictable and less distracting. This improves the overall user experience by ensuring that critical information is always visible when needed but not overwhelming during casual interactions. The wearer can trust the subtitles more, leading to a better understanding of conversations in noisy or challenging environments.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring a clear and stable subtitle display that remains readable in motion and noise is crucial for maintaining trust and real-time understanding during live conversations. By defining specific rules for subtitle placement, we can ensure that subtitles are always visible and not distracting, which supports the wearer's ability to follow the conversation without interruptions.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a set of 3 to 5 subtitle placement rules based on confidence levels (high, medium, low) and environmental factors (motion, noise). For example:
   - High confidence: Subtitles appear directly below the speaker's face with minimal motion sensitivity.
   - Medium confidence: Subtitles appear near the speaker but allow for some movement, reducing flicker.
   - Low confidence: Subtitles are displayed on a fixed line or screen edge with high stability.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By defining these rules, we can ensure that subtitles remain clear and stable, even in challenging environments. This will lead to higher trust in the system's accuracy and reliability during live conversations, directly supporting the core attractors of real-time subtitle clarity and low-friction assistance.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
