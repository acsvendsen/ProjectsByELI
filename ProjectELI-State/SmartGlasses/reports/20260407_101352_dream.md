### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving subtitle placement rules will enhance the stability and readability of subtitles, ensuring that the wearer can trust them in live conversation. This improvement aligns with the core attractor of real-time subtitle clarity, which emphasizes keeping subtitles clear, fast, and stable.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define three distinct subtitle placement rules: one for low-confidence states (e.g., when initial recognition is uncertain), another for medium-confidence states (when there's a reasonable match but less certainty), and a third for high-confidence states (when the speaker or name is well known). This will provide a structured approach to handling different levels of confidence.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing these placement rules, subtitles will be more readable and less distracting in various contexts. This improves the overall user experience by reducing cognitive load and ensuring that critical information is easily accessible during conversations. The increased clarity and stability of subtitles will contribute significantly to maintaining trust in the system's accuracy.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the confidence display format enhances trust by clearly indicating to the wearer the reliability of the subtitle text and memory support. A well-designed confidence display can prevent misunderstandings and ensure that the wearer relies on accurate information.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Introduce a simple numeric score (e.g., 0-1) alongside a label (e.g., High, Medium, Low) with a short reason (e.g., "Seen recently," "Weak match"). For example: `score 0.84 + "High" + "Seen recently"`.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing a clear and concise confidence display, the system can prevent misunderstandings and ensure that the wearer relies on accurate information. This will enhance trust in the system’s ability to provide real-time subtitles and memory support.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear local memory threshold for face/name recall, we can ensure that the system retains key information without overloading the phone's memory or battery. This helps maintain real-time subtitle clarity and low-friction assistance by providing quick access to names and faces that are likely to be relevant in the current conversation.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit for face/name recall, ensuring that only recently seen or frequently encountered individuals are cached on the phone. This can be set to 10-15 entries based on observed interaction patterns and battery constraints.

3. **Likely payoff**

   Implementing this cache policy will reduce latency in name recall, improve memory trust by providing quick access to relevant names, and prevent unnecessary data transmission to the cloud, thus saving bandwidth and battery life. This will enhance the wearer's ability to understand conversations more effectively without being interrupted by delays.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Set a local cache size limit of 15 entries for face/name recall on the phone.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

By clearly defining what stays phone-local versus what falls back to cloud for live subtitle processing, we ensure that subtitles remain real-time and stable enough to be trusted in noisy environments. This focus on real-time performance is crucial for maintaining user trust and ensuring that the wearer can rely on the subtitles during critical moments of conversation.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

Define a clear policy where all subtitle rendering and initial processing happen locally on the phone, with any heavy lifting or fallback to cloud limited to specific tasks like face/name recall lookup or memory archive sync.

3. **Likely payoff**

By keeping subtitle processing entirely local, we can achieve faster response times, lower latency, and more stable subtitle display. This will enhance user trust in the system and ensure that subtitles remain readable and helpful during live interactions.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support ensures that the wearer can focus on critical information without being overwhelmed by too much text or visual noise. This helps maintain trust in the system's ability to assist effectively while reducing cognitive load.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a clear precedence rule where subtitles have higher priority over one-line prompts when both are available simultaneously. For example, prioritize subtitles for critical information and show one-line prompts only when they add significant value without clashing with the subtitles.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By clearly defining subtitle vs one-line priority, the system can avoid visual clutter that might distract or overwhelm the wearer, leading to a more seamless and effective assistive experience. This will help in maintaining the trust and usability of the product.
