### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   - By defining specific subtitle placement rules for different confidence states, V1 can ensure that subtitles remain stable and readable while reducing visual distraction.

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   - Define three subtitle placement rules: 
     - Fixed position at 70% of field-of-view (FOV) for high-confidence subtitles.
     - Lowered to 50% FOV for medium-confidence subtitles.
     - Further lowered to 30% FOV for low-confidence subtitles.

3. **Likely payoff**
   - This approach will help the wearer trust the subtitles more, as they remain visible and stable without overwhelming their peripheral vision. It also reduces visual distraction, maintaining the core mission of discreet assistance.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving confidence display ensures that the wearer can trust the system's output more effectively. By providing clear and accurate confidence indicators, the system enhances the wearer’s understanding of when they should rely on the subtitles or when additional context might be necessary.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a consistent format for confidence labels, ensuring they are neutral and concise. For example, use a numeric score (0-1) accompanied by a label such as "High," "Medium," or "Low" with a short reason why the confidence is at that level.

3. **Likely payoff**

   Clear and reliable confidence indicators will build trust in the system’s output. This trust is crucial for the wearer to rely on the subtitles during critical interactions, reducing anxiety and ensuring they are not misled by uncertain information.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   - Improving memory cache policy ensures that likely new-person memories are reinforced over time while maintaining privacy-aware behavior, enhancing overall trust and reliability in face/name recall.

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   - Implement a local cache threshold for person memories on the phone, defining a recency rule to prioritize recent encounters and ensure that frequently seen but infrequently remembered individuals are still reliably recalled.

3. **Likely payoff**
   - This will help maintain trust by ensuring that new faces and names are quickly and accurately recalled, while also respecting privacy constraints by not persistently storing unnecessary information locally.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   Define a local cache threshold for person memories on the phone, setting a recency rule to prioritize recent encounters over older ones.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   - By defining clear boundaries for what stays phone-local versus what falls back to cloud, we ensure that V1 focuses on providing real-time, reliable subtitle and memory support without unnecessary complexity or latency.

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   - Define a specific task boundary such as "live subtitle processing" and establish explicit rules for what data must be processed locally and when fallbacks to the cloud are necessary.

3. **Likely payoff**
   - Clear phone/cloud boundaries will enhance V1's performance by reducing latency, conserving battery, and maintaining privacy. This will lead to a more reliable and usable product in early iterations.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one task-specific phone/cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing, such as "process live subtitles on the phone if confident face/name detection is possible; otherwise, fall back to cloud."

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring a clear visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support helps maintain low-friction assistance without overwhelming the wearer. By defining how these elements coexist, we can prevent information overload while still providing necessary context.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple subtitle-vs-one-line priority rule that ensures subtitles remain visible even when one-line prompts are active, but the prompts are clearly secondary and do not interfere with reading.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach will enhance the wearer's ability to focus on conversations by reducing visual clutter while still providing essential contextual support. It improves trust in the system’s reliability and usability.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support where subtitles have higher precedence over prompts.
