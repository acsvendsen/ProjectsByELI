### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining specific subtitle placement rules based on confidence levels, we ensure that subtitles are always visible but do not become overly intrusive. This balance helps maintain the wearer's ability to follow conversations without feeling overwhelmed by too much text.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Introduce a simple rule where subtitles are displayed more prominently when the confidence level is high (e.g., 0.9 or above) and slightly less prominently when the confidence level is moderate (e.g., 0.7 to 0.8). For low-confidence text, subtitles can be hidden entirely or shown very briefly.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This rule will help in maintaining a clear and unobtrusive UI while ensuring that important information is always visible. It reduces the risk of distracting the wearer with unnecessary text and enhances their ability to focus on the conversation.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining the confidence format, we ensure that the system's trustworthiness and reliability are clearly communicated to the user. This clarity helps build and maintain trust in the assistant’s abilities, which is crucial for the wearer to feel comfortable using the glasses in real-world situations.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a clear and concise confidence format that combines a numeric score with a label and a short reason. For example: "score 0.95 + 'High' + 'Seen recently'".

3. **Likely payoff**

   A well-defined confidence format will help users understand the reliability of the system’s predictions, making them more likely to trust the glasses in noisy environments or when following unfamiliar speakers. This clarity can reduce cognitive load and social friction for the user.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a local memory threshold for person-memory objects, we can ensure that only the most relevant and frequently encountered names and faces are cached on the phone. This reduces the risk of storing unnecessary information while maintaining useful recall abilities. It also helps in managing the battery and thermal constraints by limiting the amount of data stored.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   To unlock this improvement, we need to set a specific threshold for how many recent or frequently encountered faces and names should be cached locally on the phone. This can be defined as a recency limit (e.g., last 5 interactions) or an encounter frequency limit (e.g., more than 3 encounters in the past week).

3. **Likely payoff**

   Setting a local memory threshold will enhance the system's performance by reducing the load on the battery and improving response times for common names and faces. It will also ensure that the most relevant information is always available, thus increasing the trust users have in the system’s memory capabilities.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one recency-based local cache threshold of 5 recent interactions for person-memory objects to enhance memory recall efficiency while managing battery usage.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   - By defining clear boundaries for what runs on the phone and what falls back to the cloud, we can ensure that V1 focuses on providing the most critical real-time assistance without overloading the device. This helps in maintaining a balance between usability and battery efficiency.

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   - Define one task-specific fallback boundary for live subtitle processing where content is too complex or uncertain to be processed locally.

3. **Likely payoff**
   - This decision will help in creating a more robust V1 that can handle edge cases without compromising on the core functionality of real-time subtitles, thereby enhancing user trust and satisfaction.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   Define one phone/cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing where content is too complex or uncertain to be processed locally.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring a clear visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support helps prevent overloading the wearer's attention without compromising on the quality of information provided. By defining how these elements coexist, we can enhance the overall user experience, making sure that crucial information is prioritized while minimizing distraction.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a visual priority rule that ensures subtitles are more prominent than one-line support when both are present. This can be achieved by setting a higher z-index for subtitles in the UI stack and ensuring they appear first in any visual layout.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By prioritizing subtitles over one-line support, we reduce cognitive load on the wearer, making it easier to follow conversations without feeling overwhelmed by additional text. This improves the overall assistive value of the glasses, aligning with the core mission of helping the wearer stay present and informed.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define a visual priority rule where subtitles have higher precedence than one-line support in all display contexts, ensuring subtitles are always visible first and foremost.
