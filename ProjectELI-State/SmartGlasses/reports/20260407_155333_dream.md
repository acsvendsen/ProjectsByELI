### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining specific subtitle placement rules based on confidence levels, we can ensure that subtitles are always clear and readable while minimizing distraction. This will improve trust in the real-time subtitles, aligning with the primary attractor of "Real-time subtitle clarity."

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Introduce a simple rule where subtitles are placed at the bottom center for high confidence matches and slightly to one side (left or right) for medium confidence matches. Low confidence matches can be shown briefly in a corner to avoid overwhelming the wearer.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach will ensure that critical information is always visible, reducing the likelihood of misinterpretation due to poor placement. It will also help in managing the visual hierarchy effectively without causing distraction during high-concentration tasks like listening or conversation.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

A clear and concise confidence format will reduce social and cognitive friction by making the system’s certainty levels transparent.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

Define one specific confidence format candidate for V1. This could be a numeric score with label and short reason, such as "score 0.84 + 'High' + 'Seen recently'; score 0.42 + 'Low' + 'Weak match'; etc."

3. **Likely payoff**

By clearly displaying the confidence level of each piece of information, the system will gain trust from the wearer. This clarity will help in scenarios where the wearer might be unsure about a name or face, knowing that they can rely on the system's certainty without needing to ask for confirmation.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving memory cache thresholds ensures that likely new people and names are remembered with high confidence over time, enhancing trust in the system's ability to assist the wearer accurately and consistently.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache threshold for face/name recognition such that new entities are stored for at least 30 days before being evicted from the cache, ensuring they remain accessible and reinforced over multiple interactions.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By setting a clear retention period for new memories, the system will build stronger, more reliable associations between faces/names and their contexts, improving overall memory trust and the wearer's confidence in the system’s recall capabilities.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one local cache eviction rule where new face/name entries are retained for at least 30 days before being evicted from the cache to support reinforcement over time.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining clear boundaries for what runs locally on the phone versus what falls back to the cloud, we ensure that V1 remains focused and efficient. This clarity helps in managing battery and thermal constraints while maintaining real-time responsiveness critical for subtitle assistance.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific task boundary where live subtitle processing is handled by the phone, with fallbacks only triggered under certain conditions. For example, use local processing for initial subtitle rendering but fall back to cloud processing if there's significant latency or ambiguity in face/name recall.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping subtitle processing local and smartly using cloud resources for memory and lookup tasks, we can achieve a balance between real-time responsiveness and efficient resource usage. This will help maintain the core mission of providing clear, stable subtitles without overloading the phone's limited battery or thermal capacity.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that subtitles are displayed in a visually balanced way with one-line support (e.g., prompts or reminders) helps maintain the wearer's focus on the conversation without overwhelming them with too much information. This balance is crucial for both real-time subtitle clarity and low-friction assistance, as it prevents distraction while ensuring the user remains informed.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a visual hierarchy rule that routes subtitles to appear above one-line support in most contexts but allows one-line prompts to be visible when they are critical, such as in high-distraction environments or during complex conversations.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By prioritizing the visibility of subtitles over one-line prompts when necessary, this rule will enhance the wearer's ability to follow the conversation without being disrupted by too many overlays. This improvement should increase trust and the perceived value of the system in noisy or densely populated environments where clarity is essential.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define a visual priority rule that shows subtitles above one-line support except when the one-line prompt is critical, such as during complex conversations with multiple speakers.
