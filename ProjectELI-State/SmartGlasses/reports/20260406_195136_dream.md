### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving subtitle placement rules ensures that subtitles remain readable, stable, and unobtrusive, thereby enhancing the wearer's ability to follow conversations in noisy environments without feeling overwhelmed or distracted.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define 3 fixed subtitle placement modes: 
   - Top center
   - Bottom right
   - Dynamic (based on confidence level but never covering critical parts of speech)

3. **Likely payoff**

   By defining clear, consistent placement rules, the system will be more reliable and less likely to cause distraction or misinterpretation during conversations, leading to a smoother user experience.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining the confidence display to use a clear numeric score, label, and short reason format, we can enhance the wearer's trust in the system's accuracy and reliability. This improves the overall quality of the subtitle experience and memory support, ensuring that the wearer is always aware of the certainty level behind each piece of information.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   The small change is to define a specific confidence format: `score + label + short reason`. For example, "score 0.84 + 'High' + 'Seen recently'; score 0.42 + 'Low' + 'Weak match'; score 0.63 + 'Medium' + 'Name heard once in introduction'."

3. **Likely payoff**

   Implementing this format will provide a clear and concise way to communicate the certainty of each piece of information, helping the wearer make better decisions during conversations. This can reduce misunderstandings and increase the overall trust in the system.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving memory cache size and eviction policies ensures that relevant names, faces, and context are always available to the wearer, enhancing the reliability of face/name recall and conversation memory. This directly addresses the "Trustworthy memory support" attractor, ensuring the system remains useful without becoming intrusive.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit for frequently seen names and faces, with an eviction policy that favors keeping recently encountered memories active while removing older ones to make space for new evidence.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By setting a clear threshold for memory caching, the system can balance between storing enough relevant information to be useful and avoiding memory bloat that could degrade performance or battery life. This will help in maintaining trust by providing accurate and timely recall of names and faces without overwhelming the wearer with unnecessary data.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define a local cache size limit for frequently seen names and faces.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that live subtitle processing remains phone-local until necessary tasks are offloaded to cloud enhances battery life and keeps the system lightweight, which is crucial for maintaining a discreet and socially acceptable product.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one local fallback boundary for transcript cleanup where low-confidence segments can be automatically cached locally before being sent to the cloud for final processing.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By caching low-confidence transcripts locally, we reduce the frequency of high-latency requests to the cloud and preserve battery life, leading to more stable and responsive subtitle delivery in noisy environments. This improves overall user trust and satisfaction without compromising on the core functionality of real-time subtitles.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support ensures that critical information is always readable, while minimizing visual clutter to maintain social acceptability and reduce cognitive load.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a single visual priority rule that routes higher-priority content (subtitles) over lower-priority content (one-line prompts) in the display layer.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will enhance overall usability by ensuring that subtitles remain readable and not obscured by less critical one-line support text, thereby maintaining trust and reducing distraction during conversations.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support such that subtitles always override lower-priority content in the display layer.
