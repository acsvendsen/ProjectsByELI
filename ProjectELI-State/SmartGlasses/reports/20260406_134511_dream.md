### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining specific subtitle placement rules based on confidence levels, we ensure that critical information is always easily readable while minimizing distraction during less crucial parts of a conversation.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define three subtitle placement modes: fixed bottom, dynamic center, and hidden until touch. The mode should automatically switch based on the speaker's confidence score.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach will reduce eye strain and cognitive overload by keeping critical information always visible while maintaining a clean, unobtrusive interface for less important parts of the conversation. It will also ensure that important details are not missed during high-confidence interactions.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define fixed position rules for subtitle placement based on confidence states: fixed bottom for high confidence, dynamic center for medium confidence, and hidden until touch for low confidence.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining the confidence display, we can better manage the balance between providing clear assistance without overwhelming the wearer or compromising their ability to respond authentically. This helps in maintaining trust and ensuring that the system's support is always useful but not intrusive.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Introduce a simple label-based confidence format with an accompanying short reason for each memory object, such as names and terms. For example, "High: Seen recently" or "Low: Weak match."

3. **Likely payoff**

   This improvement will help the wearer understand the reliability of the system's suggestions, thereby increasing trust in the technology. It also ensures that the subtitles remain clear and uncluttered while still providing necessary context.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one confidence format candidate for V1: score 0.84 + "High" + "Seen recently"; score 0.42 + "Low" + "Weak match".

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a local cache size limit for name recall, we ensure that memory is managed efficiently without overwhelming the wearer's attention. This helps in maintaining a balance between providing relevant information and avoiding unnecessary distractions.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific threshold for how many names should be cached locally on the phone before new entries start evicting older ones. For example, caching up to 50 unique names with recent encounters being prioritized over less frequent ones.

3. **Likely payoff**

   Implementing this cache size limit ensures that the system remains lightweight and avoids cluttering the wearer's memory with too many names. This improves the overall user experience by keeping the UI uncluttered while still providing fast recall for frequently encountered individuals.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define a local cache size limit of 50 unique names, prioritizing recent encounters over less frequent ones for V1 implementation.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

By defining clear boundaries between what runs on the phone and what falls back to the cloud, we ensure that V1 remains focused, low-friction, and reliable. This clarity helps maintain user trust by avoiding latency issues and ensuring real-time performance.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

Define a simple rule for when face/name memory should be stored locally versus falling back to the cloud. For instance, store names seen within the last 30 minutes on the phone, but sync them to the cloud after an hour to maintain history and avoid privacy concerns.

3. **Likely payoff**

This policy will reduce latency during critical conversations by keeping frequently encountered names local, while still ensuring that important memories are not lost over time through cloud storage. It also helps manage battery and thermal constraints by minimizing data sent over the network.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define a rule for when face/name recognition results should be stored locally on the phone versus falling back to the cloud, setting a threshold of 30 minutes for local storage followed by periodic synchronization to the cloud.
