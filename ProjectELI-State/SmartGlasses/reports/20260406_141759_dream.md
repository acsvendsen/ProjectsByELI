### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring stable subtitle placement helps maintain a consistent user experience, reducing distractions and improving readability. This stability is crucial for the wearer's understanding and trust in the system.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement rules: one fixed position at the bottom of the screen for high-confidence text and another slightly lower but less intrusive position for low-confidence text.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By defining clear, stable subtitle placement rules, users will have a predictable experience with minimal distractions, leading to increased trust in the system's reliability and effectiveness.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring clear and reliable confidence display helps build trust with the wearer by providing honest feedback on the certainty of the system's recognition. This transparency can lead to more accurate and useful interactions, as the wearer knows when to rely on the smart glasses' assistance.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a consistent confidence format that includes both a numeric score and a label with a short reason for the confidence level. For example: "Score 0.85 + 'High' + 'Seen recently in this conversation'".

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing a clear and concise confidence display, users will have better understanding of when to trust the smart glasses' information. This can lead to more productive interactions and reduced cognitive load, as users know which parts of the system's output are most reliable.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a clear threshold for local caching of face/name memory, the system can balance the need for fast lookup with privacy concerns. This ensures that frequently encountered individuals are quickly recognized without storing unnecessary information indefinitely.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a recency-based eviction policy where recently seen faces and names are cached locally but older entries are evicted after a set number of days or interactions.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach enhances the system's ability to quickly recall known individuals while respecting user privacy by limiting the storage of personal data. It also reduces the load on the cloud, making the system more efficient and responsive in real-time interactions.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one recency-based eviction rule for face/name memory, where entries are retained for up to 30 days with a daily interaction refresh.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By carefully defining which tasks run phone-local versus those that fall back to the cloud, we can ensure real-time responsiveness and reduce battery drain while maintaining privacy and usability.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

3. **Likely payoff**

   This will improve the overall user experience by reducing latency and ensuring that subtitles remain near-real-time, while also conserving battery life and maintaining privacy.

4. **Immediate next probe**
