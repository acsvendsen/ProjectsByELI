### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining specific subtitle placement rules that are stable yet adaptable to confidence levels, the system can maintain a high level of clarity without overwhelming the wearer. This ensures that subtitles remain readable and useful in noisy environments, enhancing both real-time understanding and long-term memory support.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement rules: one for low-confidence states (where subtitles are displayed at the bottom to avoid distraction) and another for high-confidence states (where subtitles can be placed more prominently on the top or side of the field of view).

3. **Likely payoff**

   This simple yet effective placement strategy will help maintain a balance between visual clarity and cognitive load, ensuring that the wearer remains focused on the conversation while still receiving necessary support. It will also provide a foundation for more complex subtitle behavior in future updates.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring a clear and consistent confidence format helps maintain user trust in the system's reliability. By providing a simple yet informative way to display certainty, the wearer can better understand when they should rely on the subtitles or memory support.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a concise confidence label with a short reason for each confidence state. For example:
   - High (seen recently)
   - Medium (name heard once in introduction)
   - Low (weak match)

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing clear and specific confidence labels, the system can better convey its reliability to the wearer, reducing uncertainty and increasing trust. This clarity will help the wearer make more informed decisions about when to rely on the system's assistance.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a local cache threshold for name recall, we can ensure that frequently encountered names remain available locally without overloading the phone's memory or draining battery life. This improves daily-use value by reducing latency and ensuring that important names are always accessible in real-time.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one local cache size limit for names that balances frequent use with battery and storage constraints. For example, limiting the number of frequently seen names to 50 or 100 can ensure that the most relevant names remain cached locally while minimizing resource usage.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will reduce latency in name recall and improve the accuracy and speed of name-related memory support, enhancing the wearer's trust in the system’s ability to assist with names during conversations.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define a local cache size limit of 50 frequently seen names for V1.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear boundary for what runs phone-local versus what falls back to cloud, we ensure that V1 remains focused on real-time subtitle clarity and memory support without overloading the glasses hardware. This helps maintain low-latency responses and preserves battery life, which are critical for trust and usability.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific task boundary where live subtitles processing will primarily run phone-local and only fallback to cloud when necessary, such as during transcript cleanup or face/name recall lookup. This involves setting clear thresholds for what runs on-device versus offloading tasks to the cloud.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping subtitle processing local, we can achieve faster and more stable subtitle delivery, reducing battery drain and ensuring that subtitles remain readable in real-time conversations. This enhances trust and the overall user experience by providing immediate and reliable assistance.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one phone-local vs cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing where transcripts are cleaned up after initial processing to improve readability and coherence.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   
   By defining a clear visual hierarchy rule between subtitles and one-line support, we ensure that the most critical information is always prominently displayed without overwhelming the wearer. This helps in maintaining focus on live conversation while still providing necessary contextual cues.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Introduce a simple display precedence rule: Subtitles are shown first and foremost with high visual prominence, followed by one-line support when space allows. If subtitles cannot be fully displayed due to screen limitations, the most recent or critical ones should remain visible, while older or less important content is suppressed.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will enhance the wearer's ability to follow live conversations by ensuring that essential information (subtitles) remains easily readable and prominent. It also prevents cluttering the display with too much text, which could reduce usability and distract from the primary task of understanding real-time communication.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
