### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring stable subtitle placement improves the wearer's trust in the system, making them more confident during conversations. Stable placement prevents distractions and ensures that subtitles remain visible and readable throughout the interaction.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a fixed subtitle placement rule based on the lower third of the screen, with a slight buffer above and below to account for head movements and eye tracking. This rule will help maintain consistent visibility without being overly intrusive.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing a stable subtitle placement rule, the system can ensure that subtitles remain easily readable and do not distract the wearer during conversations. This improves trust and overall usability, making the product more valuable to users in noisy environments.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

Improving confidence display by defining a clear, concise format enhances trust in the system's accuracy and reliability. A well-defined confidence label with a short reason will help users understand when they can rely on the subtitles or memory support, reinforcing the core attractors of real-time subtitle clarity and trustworthy memory.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

Define a confidence display format that includes a numeric score, a confidence label (e.g., High, Medium, Low), and a short reason for the score (e.g., "Seen recently," "Weak match").

3. **Likely payoff**

Implementing this format will provide users with clear indicators of subtitle and memory reliability, enhancing their trust in the system's assistance. This clarity will reduce cognitive load by ensuring that users can quickly assess the quality of the information provided, thereby improving overall user experience and daily-use value.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that face/name memory is stored and reinforced with a local cache threshold helps maintain trust by allowing the system to provide fast, accurate recall without overloading the phone's resources or compromising battery life.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit for storing recently encountered faces and names on the phone, ensuring that only the most relevant memories are retained for quick lookup.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By limiting the amount of memory stored locally, we can reduce the risk of battery drain and ensure faster recall times during interactions. This will enhance the overall user experience by providing timely support without excessive data usage or processing overhead.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear boundary for what runs phone-local versus cloud, we ensure that critical live subtitle processing and memory recall happen on the phone in real-time, maintaining trust and reducing latency. This focus keeps the assistant discreet and effective without overloading the wearer's cognitive load.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one task-specific cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup. Specifically, set a rule that any face or name recognition request with less than 80% confidence should automatically fall back to the cloud for verification before showing any result on the glasses.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This decision will help maintain real-time responsiveness and reduce false positives in memory recall, ensuring that the wearer gets accurate information without unnecessary delays or incorrect displays. It also aligns with the core goal of providing low-friction assistance while maintaining privacy and trust.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that subtitles coexist effectively with one-line prompts in a way that neither overwhelms nor distracts the wearer is crucial for maintaining trust and usability. By defining clear rules on when to show subtitles versus one-liners, we can enhance the wearer's ability to follow conversations without feeling like they are receiving too much information at once.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a single subtitle-vs-one-line priority rule that prioritizes subtitles over one-liners in noisy environments and lessens this preference as the environment becomes more quiet or the conversation slows down.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This rule will help in reducing visual clutter, ensuring that critical information (subtitles) is always visible while still providing helpful context when necessary. It will also reduce the likelihood of the wearer feeling overwhelmed by too much on-screen text, thereby enhancing overall usability and trust in the system.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support that prioritizes subtitles over one-liners in noisy environments but allows for one-liners when the environment is quieter or during slower conversations.
