### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   Improving subtitle placement rules enhances the wearer's ability to read subtitles without becoming distracted. Stable, well-placed subtitles can help maintain focus on conversation flow, thus improving overall understanding and engagement in social situations.

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   Define two subtitle placement modes: one for high-confidence speech (e.g., clear, familiar speakers) and another for low-confidence speech (e.g., noisy environments or new speakers). Use the existing `More Prominent` setting as a starting point but introduce an option to be less intrusive.

3. **Likely payoff**
   By providing distinct placement modes based on confidence levels, the system can better balance between ensuring clear visibility and minimizing distraction, leading to more consistent and useful subtitle experiences.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining the confidence format for V1, we can ensure that the system's certainty is clearly communicated to the wearer, thereby enhancing trust and understanding. This will help the wearer interpret the reliability of the displayed information accurately.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple confidence format that uses a numeric score with a label and a short reason. For example: "Score 0.85 + 'High' + 'Seen recently'; Score 0.32 + 'Low' + 'Weak match'; Score 0.64 + 'Medium' + 'Name heard once in introduction'."

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach will provide a clear and concise way for the wearer to understand the reliability of each piece of information, whether it's a name, face, or conversation memory. It will help build trust by being transparent about the certainty of the displayed data.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By setting a clear local cache size limit for face/name memory objects, the system can ensure that only frequently encountered or recently seen individuals are stored locally, reducing memory overhead while maintaining high-confidence recall.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a policy where the phone caches up to 10 recently seen faces and names on the glasses, with a recency threshold of within the last hour for highest confidence objects.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach reduces the overall memory load while ensuring that the most relevant information is always available locally, thus improving response time and trust in the system's ability to recall important individuals quickly during conversations.

4. **Immediate next probe**

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By clearly defining what runs on the phone versus what falls back to the cloud for live subtitle processing, we can ensure real-time performance while keeping battery use manageable. This balance is crucial for maintaining trust and usability in noisy environments.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple rule: critical subtitle processing (e.g., speech-to-text and real-time rendering) runs locally on the phone, with fallbacks to cloud only when the phone's resources are stretched or the local models need updates.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping subtitle processing local, we can ensure near-real-time performance, which is essential for maintaining trust and usability. This approach also reduces battery drain and ensures that the glasses remain reliable in low-signal areas where cloud connectivity might be weak.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one real-time subtitle processing rule: critical speech-to-text and rendering runs locally on the phone with a fallback to cloud only when local resources are exhausted or model updates are needed.
