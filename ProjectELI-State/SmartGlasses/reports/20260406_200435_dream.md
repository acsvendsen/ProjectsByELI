### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Subtitle placement rules that are stable and predictable enhance trust by reducing visual distraction during conversations. By avoiding frequent, reactive changes to subtitle positions, the system can maintain a consistent reading experience that aligns with natural conversation flow, thereby keeping the wearer focused on the interaction.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define 2 to 3 fixed subtitle placement rules for different confidence states: high-confidence (always visible), medium-confidence (slightly below eye level but always readable), and low-confidence (only shown when explicitly touched or in critical contexts).

3. **Likely payoff**

   By setting stable subtitle placement rules, the system can minimize visual clutter and ensure that subtitles remain easily readable without interrupting natural conversation flow. This will enhance trust and usability for the wearer by making the smart glasses feel more discreet and effective.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining the confidence display format, we can ensure that the wearer understands the reliability of the subtitles and memory support in real-time. This clarity will help build trust in the system's accuracy and reduce cognitive load by providing immediate feedback on how certain the system is about its information.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple confidence object format for V1 that includes a numeric score, label, and short reason. For example: "score 0.84 + 'High' + 'Seen recently'; score 0.42 + 'Low' + 'Weak match'; score 0.63 + 'Medium' + 'Name heard once in introduction'".

3. **Likely payoff**

   Implementing a clear and concise confidence display will enhance the wearer's trust in the system, making them more likely to rely on the subtitles and memory support during critical interactions. This increased trust can lead to better user adoption and higher daily-use value.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Memory support is crucial for building trust in the system's ability to recall names, faces, and prior context accurately. By ensuring that new people are remembered with confidence objects and reinforced over time, the system can provide reliable and consistent assistance during conversations. This enhances the wearer's confidence in the smart glasses as a useful tool.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one reinforcement rule for repeated face/name encounters to ensure that new entities are cached locally on the phone with increasing certainty. This involves setting a threshold of interactions (e.g., three mentions) after which a person is marked as known and stored in memory, reducing the likelihood of false negatives.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing a clear reinforcement rule, the system will more accurately recall new people over time, enhancing overall trust and usability. This reduces the chance of the wearer feeling frustrated by missed name recognitions or incorrect face identifications, thereby improving daily use value.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that live subtitle processing remains phone-local helps maintain real-time performance, which is crucial for trust and clarity in noisy environments. By keeping this critical task on the phone, we avoid latency introduced by cloud delays, ensuring subtitles are always timely and reliable.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific confidence threshold to trigger local subtitle processing instead of relying solely on global network conditions. For example, if the confidence score for speech recognition falls below 0.9, process the transcript locally without sending it to the cloud.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By maintaining low-latency subtitle processing on the phone, we ensure that subtitles are always clear and readable in real-time conversations. This improves the wearer's trust in the system and enhances their ability to follow along during noisy interactions, making the glasses a more reliable tool.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support, we ensure that the wearer can focus on essential information without being overwhelmed by additional text. This balance enhances the overall user experience, making the glasses more useful and less intrusive.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a single rule for subtitle-vs-one-line priority that prioritizes subtitles over one-line prompts when both are present in the visual field. This can be a simple Boolean check where subtitles always have higher precedence unless explicitly overridden by explicit user interaction.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By establishing a clear hierarchy, we reduce cognitive load on the wearer, making the glasses more effective and less likely to distract from the conversation or task at hand. This can significantly improve trust in the system's ability to provide relevant support without overwhelming the user.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support where subtitles always have higher precedence unless explicitly overridden by a frame touch for one-line prompts.
