### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring stable subtitle placement rules in V1 will improve the wearer's trust and understanding, making the subtitles clear and readable even during rapid or noisy conversations. This stability is crucial for maintaining the attractor of "Real-time subtitle clarity" and contributing to the overall "Low-friction assistance" by reducing visual distractions.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define 2 to 3 fixed subtitle placement rules based on confidence levels: 
   - High-confidence subtitles appear above the gaze line but do not move.
   - Medium-confidence subtitles hover below the gaze line and follow eye movements within a certain threshold.
   - Low-confidence subtitles are cached in a fixed position near the bottom of the field of view, ensuring they remain visible without blocking the user's view.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing stable placement for high-confidence subtitles while allowing medium and low-confidence ones to be more dynamic but still visible, this approach ensures that the wearer can trust the information provided by the glasses. This stability reduces cognitive load and enhances the overall readability of the subtitles, contributing positively to both "Real-time subtitle clarity" and "Low-friction assistance."

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the confidence display format will help build trust in the system's memory and assistive capabilities, ensuring that the wearer can rely on the information provided. A well-designed confidence format will make the uncertainty clear to the user without causing confusion or overloading them with too much detail.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple, neutral confidence object format that combines a numeric score, a label, and a short reason. For example: "score 0.84 + 'High' + 'Seen recently'; score 0.42 + 'Low' + 'Weak match'; score 0.63 + 'Medium' + 'Name heard once in introduction'.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing clear and concise confidence information, the system will be able to convey its level of certainty about a piece of memory or spoken word. This will enhance user trust and help the wearer understand when they can rely on the system's assistance. Clear confidence indicators will also support better decision-making during interactions, making the system more effective in real-world scenarios.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define the numeric score presentation for the confidence display format.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   Memory policies that ensure fast and accurate recall of people and names are crucial for maintaining trust in the wearer's memory support. By setting a local cache threshold, we can balance the need for quick name recall with the constraints of battery and thermal management.

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   Define a local cache size limit for storing recently encountered faces and names, ensuring that the system retains confidence objects for these entities but also frees up memory to handle new encounters without overwhelming the phone's resources.

3. **Likely payoff**
   This change will enhance the system’s ability to quickly recall familiar individuals in real-time conversations, reducing latency and improving trust by showing consistent and reliable recognition of names and faces.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.
