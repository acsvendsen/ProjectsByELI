### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving subtitle placement rules ensures that subtitles remain clear, stable, and unobtrusive, enhancing real-time clarity and trust. This is crucial for the wearer's understanding in noisy environments.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two fixed subtitle placement modes: one at the bottom of the field of view (FOV) and another slightly above center. The bottom placement mode ensures subtitles are always visible, while the center mode minimizes distraction during motion.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By defining clear and consistent subtitle placement rules, V1 will deliver a more stable and usable experience, reducing visual distractions and enhancing the wearer's ability to trust the subtitles in dynamic scenarios.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the confidence display format helps ensure that the wearer can trust the system's assistance in real-time conversations. By making the confidence score, label, and short reason more clear and consistent, we can reinforce the reliability of the subtitles and memory support.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a standard confidence object format: `score 0.84 + "High" + "Seen recently"` for high confidence, with similar formats for medium and low confidence levels.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By standardizing the confidence display, we can ensure that the wearer understands when to trust the subtitles and memory support more easily. This clarity will build user trust and reduce cognitive load during conversations.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving memory cache policies ensures that likely new-person memories are reinforced over time, enhancing trust in name recall without invading privacy. This improves daily-use value by ensuring the wearer can rely on face/name recognition even when not explicitly touched.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache eviction rule based on reinforcement history, where memories with multiple explicit touches or frequent recent encounters are kept longer than those encountered less frequently.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By prioritizing memory based on interaction and recency, the system will more accurately recall names and faces in real-world interactions, reducing the need for frequent manual touches and enhancing overall trust and reliability.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one reinforcement rule for repeated face/name encounters that keeps memories with multiple explicit touches or recent interactions cached longer than those encountered less frequently.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining clear boundaries for local processing versus cloud fallback, we ensure that critical real-time functionality like live subtitle processing remains fast and reliable while non-critical tasks fall back to the phone or cloud when necessary. This approach maximizes the reliability of real-time subtitles and ensures that memory support and confidence display remain responsive even under resource constraints.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a single task-specific boundary for live subtitle processing where the glasses only send raw audio input directly to the phone for transcription and subtitle generation, but do not store or process any audio locally. This simplifies the on-glasses architecture and ensures real-time performance without overloading the glasses.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By focusing solely on sending raw audio to the phone for live subtitles, we can reduce battery drain and thermal load on the glasses. This allows the glasses to remain lightweight and socially acceptable while ensuring that the most critical functionality remains fast and reliable. Additionally, this approach will enable more efficient cloud fallbacks when needed without compromising real-time performance.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one live subtitle processing task boundary where raw audio input is sent directly to the phone for transcription and subtitle generation.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By ensuring that subtitles are clear, fast, and stable enough to be trusted in live conversation, we enhance the wearer's ability to understand and participate effectively in real-world interactions. This improves trust and reliability of the assistive feature, making the glasses more valuable.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define 3 subtitle placement rules for different confidence levels: 
   - High confidence (seen multiple times): Stable subtitle at eye level.
   - Medium confidence (hears once or twice): Subtitle slightly lower in field of view but still visible.
   - Low confidence (first encounter or weak match): Subtle, quickly displayed and hidden.

3. **Likely payoff**

   These rules will help ensure that subtitles are always placed where they can be read without distraction, maintaining the wearer's focus on the conversation while ensuring critical information is conveyed. This improves overall user experience and trust in the system.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
