### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   Improving subtitle placement rules can enhance readability and minimize distraction, ensuring that subtitles are always in a clear, unobtrusive position. This improves the wearer's understanding without overwhelming their focus.

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   Define two distinct subtitle placement rules: one for high-confidence speakers (e.g., those who have been seen multiple times) and another for low-confidence speakers (new people or those encountered infrequently).

3. **Likely payoff**
   By customizing subtitle placement based on confidence, the system can ensure that familiar voices receive clearer subtitles, while less certain ones get a simpler display to avoid distraction. This improves overall user experience and trust in the system.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a local cache size limit for face/name memory objects, we ensure that critical information is readily available even when network conditions are poor or battery life is low. This enhances the reliability of name recall and ensures that frequently encountered individuals are always quickly accessible.

   Define a fixed-size local cache on the phone to store recently seen face/name memory objects, with a limit of 10-20 entries based on frequency of interaction.

   This approach will significantly reduce latency in name recall during conversations, especially in noisy environments where cloud access may be unreliable. It also aligns well with the V1 focus on phone-first processing and low-friction interactions, ensuring that critical memory is always within reach without overloading the device.

4. **Immediate next probe**
