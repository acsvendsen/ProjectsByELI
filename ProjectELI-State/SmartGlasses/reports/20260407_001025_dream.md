### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining specific subtitle placement rules for different confidence states, we can ensure that subtitles are always readable and stable enough to be trusted in live conversation. This will enhance the wearer's ability to follow along in noisy environments and maintain a natural flow of interaction without distractions.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define two subtitle placement rules: one for high-confidence text (e.g., names, faces) and another for lower-confidence text (e.g., contextual support, weak matches). For high confidence, the subtitles should be displayed at the bottom center or slightly above eye level to maintain focus. For lower confidence, they can be hidden or displayed in a smaller font size.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing clear and consistent subtitle placement rules, we will reduce visual distractions, improve readability, and ensure that important information (like names and faces) is always visible and trustworthy. This will enhance the wearer's trust in the system and their ability to follow conversations more effectively.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the confidence display format ensures that the wearer can easily understand the certainty level of the system's outputs without being overwhelmed by technical details or confusion. This clarity is crucial for maintaining trust and ensuring the wearer feels supported rather than manipulated.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple, two-part confidence object: a numeric score plus a short reason label. For example, "score 0.84 + 'High'". This format provides enough detail to convey certainty without being overly complex or noisy.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing this clear and concise confidence display, the system will be more trustworthy and less prone to creating false expectations. The wearer can make better decisions based on the actual reliability of the information provided by the glasses, leading to a stronger overall user experience.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving local memory thresholds ensures that frequently encountered names and faces are readily available to the wearer without overwhelming the cache. This enhances the reliability of face/name recall, which is crucial for maintaining trust in the memory support feature.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a threshold policy where entities (names, faces) are cached locally if they have been seen multiple times within a recent timeframe, but not indefinitely. For example, cache entities that appear more than three times over the last 10 minutes.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing this threshold policy, the system will be able to quickly recall frequently encountered individuals without excessive memory overhead, reducing latency and improving the overall user experience. This approach balances the need for persistent memory with the practical constraints of battery and thermal management.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one local-cache size limit for names and faces that are seen more than three times over the last 10 minutes.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear boundary for what stays phone-local versus what falls back to cloud, we can ensure that critical real-time processing and memory support remain on-device, preserving low latency and trustworthiness. This focus helps in delivering a consistent and reliable experience that aligns with the core mission of enhancing understanding without relying on potentially delayed or privacy-invasive cloud services.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one phone-local vs cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing. Specifically, decide what aspects of subtitle generation, placement, and confidence display can be handled locally on the phone to ensure real-time performance and minimal latency.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping subtitle processing local, we can achieve faster response times and more stable subtitle delivery, which is crucial for maintaining the wearer's trust in the system during live conversations. This decision will also reduce battery drain associated with cloud requests and improve overall user experience by ensuring that subtitles are always available without lag.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By optimizing the visual hierarchy between subtitles and one-line support, we can ensure that critical information is prioritized for quick reading while maintaining a low-friction UX. This improves subtitle clarity and memory support, as the wearer can focus on essential details without being overwhelmed by additional prompts.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a visual priority rule where subtitles are always shown prominently when they have high confidence (e.g., >0.8), and one-line support is suppressed unless explicitly requested or needed for context.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach will enhance the wearer's trust in the system by ensuring that important information is clearly visible, reducing cognitive load, and maintaining a clear visual focus on critical subtitles. It also supports the core mission of providing low-friction assistance without overloading the user with too much information.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
