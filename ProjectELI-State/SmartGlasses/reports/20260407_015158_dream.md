### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   - Improving subtitle placement rules ensures that subtitles are always clear and stable enough to be trusted in live conversation. This helps maintain high subtitle clarity, which is a primary attractor for the project.

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   - Define two fixed subtitle placement rules: one for low confidence states (e.g., when recognition is uncertain) where subtitles should appear at the bottom of the screen to avoid distraction, and another for high confidence states (e.g., clear speaker identity) where subtitles can be placed slightly higher in the center.

3. **Likely payoff**
   - By defining these placement rules, we can ensure that subtitles are always readable and unobtrusive, thereby maintaining the trust of the wearer without causing distraction during critical moments when recognition is uncertain.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By refining the confidence display format to use a simple label with a short reason for uncertainty, the system can maintain a clear and consistent level of trust without overwhelming the user with too much detail. This ensures that the wearer understands the reliability of the information provided, which is crucial for maintaining their trust in the system.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Modify the confidence display to show a label such as "Likely" or "Possible" along with a short reason like "Seen once recently" or "Weak match". This change ensures that the confidence object remains simple and easy to understand while providing enough context for the wearer.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By using a clear and concise confidence display, the system can maintain high trust levels among users. The wearable assistant will be perceived as reliable and helpful without appearing overly confident or uncertain inappropriately, which could lead to mistrust.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving local cache thresholds for name recall enhances the core mission by ensuring that frequently encountered names are available quickly without unnecessary cloud requests, thus reducing latency and maintaining trust through fast and reliable responses.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache size limit of 50 entries for recently seen faces and names, with an additional 10 entries reserved for high-confidence matches. This will ensure that the most relevant memory is prioritized locally.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By limiting the local cache to 60 total entries (50 recent + 10 high-confidence), this change will significantly reduce latency during name recall, improving the wearer's trust in the system and ensuring faster responses without overwhelming on-device storage.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set one local cache eviction rule for face/name memory in V1.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By defining a clear boundary for what stays phone-local versus what falls back to cloud, we can ensure that critical real-time subtitle processing and memory recall operations remain on-device. This reduces latency, maintains privacy, and enhances the reliability of the system in noisy environments.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define one task-specific fallback rule where live subtitle processing must stay phone-local to avoid latency issues and maintain high confidence in real-time display.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By keeping live subtitle processing on-device, we can provide near-real-time subtitles that are more reliable and easier for the wearer to trust during conversations. This will enhance the core mission of helping the wearer understand better without introducing significant lag or uncertainty.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one task-specific cloud fallback boundary for exactly one task such as live subtitle processing, ensuring it stays phone-local to avoid latency issues.

### Idea 5: Visual Hierarchy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring that subtitles are displayed in a way that coexists seamlessly with one-line support without overloading the wearer helps maintain the balance between clarity and utility. This improves the overall assistive experience, ensuring that the wearer can trust both forms of assistance without feeling overwhelmed.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a visual hierarchy rule that prioritizes subtitles over one-line prompts when confidence in the subtitle content is high but not critical for immediate action. For example, if the confidence level of a subtitle is above 0.85, suppress or downplay one-line prompts to avoid distraction.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By optimizing the visual hierarchy, V1 can provide clearer and more reliable subtitles without compromising on the utility of one-line support. This ensures that the wearer trusts the core functionality while still receiving necessary context from one-line prompts when needed but not overwhelming them with too much information.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one visual priority rule between subtitles and one-line support in V1.
