### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Stable subtitle placement rules enhance user trust by ensuring that subtitles are consistently visible and unobtrusive, reducing cognitive load during interactions.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define fixed subtitle placement modes based on confidence levels: high-confidence speaker always visible; medium-confidence speaker partially visible or visible only under touch; low-confidence speaker hidden until confirmed by touch.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By providing a clear and consistent placement strategy, the wearer can rely on subtitles to be in an optimal position for reading without constantly adjusting the glasses. This reduces cognitive load and enhances overall trust in the system's reliability.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Improving the confidence display format enhances user trust by providing clear, concise information about the reliability of the system's responses. This directly supports the mission of helping the wearer understand better without pretending knowledge they do not have.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a simple numeric score and label with a short reason for each confidence object. For example: "score 0.84 + 'High' + 'Seen recently'; score 0.42 + 'Low' + 'Weak match'; score 0.63 + 'Medium' + 'Name heard once in introduction'.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By clearly indicating the certainty and source of information, the confidence display will help users better understand when to trust system outputs and when to seek additional confirmation. This can significantly reduce cognitive load and increase overall user trust.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.

### Idea 3: Memory/Cache Policy

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring a robust local cache policy for face/name recall helps maintain high trust and reliability by reducing reliance on cloud resources, especially during transient network issues or when offline.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a local cache eviction rule that evicts old memory objects after a certain number of touch interactions to keep the cache size manageable without losing important contextual information.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This change will help manage memory use efficiently, ensuring that frequently recalled names and faces are always available quickly, while less recent encounters are refreshed over time, maintaining both performance and trust in the system.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Set the cache size to store the 20 most recently interacted with people for fast face/name recall.

### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   - Ensuring that critical subtitles are processed locally on the phone can maintain real-time delivery, which is crucial for trust and usability. This local processing reduces latency and ensures that subtitles remain as synchronous as possible with speech, enhancing the wearer's understanding in noisy environments.

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   - Define a local cache eviction policy where critical subtitles are kept on-device while less critical ones may fall back to the cloud for processing.

3. **Likely payoff**
   - By keeping critical subtitles locally, we can reduce latency and ensure that the wearer always receives timely and accurate subtitles, even in low-bandwidth or disconnected scenarios. This improves overall trust and reliability of the system.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one phone-local versus cloud fallback boundary for face/name recall lookup in V1.
