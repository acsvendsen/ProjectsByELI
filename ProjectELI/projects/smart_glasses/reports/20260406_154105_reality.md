# Reality Check for SmartGlasses V1 Direction

## Core Mission and Non-negotiables

- **Mission**: Discreet assistive glasses focused on subtitles, face/name recall, conversation memory, and one-line support.
- **Non-negotiables**:
  - Frame-touch-only interaction.
  - Phone-first architecture; no heavy on-glasses compute unless clearly justified.
  - Minimal V1 complexity to focus on core features.

## Core Attractors

- Real-time subtitle assistance in noisy environments.
- Personal memory support for names and prior conversations.
- Low-friction human experience, assistive but not deceptive.
- High daily-use value with low complexity.

## Core Tensions

- Privacy vs. usefulness.
- Always-on sensing vs. battery and thermals.
- Local processing vs. cloud intelligence.
- Rich memory vs. fast lookup.
- Discreet UX vs. enough visual clarity.

## Current V1 Direction

### Interaction Constraints
- **Frame Touches Only**: No voice activation or spoken command control in V1.
- **Phone-First Architecture**: Heavy on-glasses processing is avoided; cloud support is optional where clearly justified.

### Real-time Subtitle Behavior
- **Subtitle Placement and Visual Hierarchy**:
  - **More Prominent Selection**: Ensure subtitles are visible but not overly distracting.
  - **Visual Clarity**: Balance readability with the need to avoid distractions.
  
- **Confidence Display**:
  - **Label + Reason**: Use a clear label with a short reason for confidence levels.

### Memory/Cache Policy
- **Recency First**: Cache memory based on recency, prioritizing most recent interactions and contexts.

### Phone/Cloud Boundary
- **Phone First**: Rely primarily on the phone for processing; use cloud where it adds clear value and latency is acceptable.

## Reality Check

### Subtitle Placement: More Prominent
- **Feasible Now**: Placing subtitles more prominently can enhance readability without being overly distracting. This balances the need to provide timely information with maintaining a natural conversation flow.
  - *Reason*: Ensures wearers do not miss critical information and reduces cognitive load by making subtitles easily visible.

### Confidence Display: Label + Reason
- **Feasible Now**: Using both a label (e.g., "high", "low") and a short reason can provide clear feedback on the reliability of the information.
  - *Reason*: This approach offers a balance between simplicity and clarity, allowing wearers to quickly understand the confidence level.

### Memory/Cache Policy: Recency First
- **Feasible Now**: Caching memory based on recency makes sense as it prioritizes relevant and recent interactions while reducing storage requirements.
  - *Reason*: Most recent conversations are likely to be the most useful, making this a practical approach for initial V1.

### Phone/Cloud Boundary: Phone First
- **Feasible Now**: Relying heavily on the phone for processing is appropriate given current hardware constraints. Cloud support can be added later as needed.
  - *Reason*: This approach minimizes thermal and battery impact, ensuring reliable performance in the early stages.

## Drift Risks

- **Feature Sprawl**: Ensure that any new features align with core mission goals to avoid unnecessary complexity.
- **Interaction Complexity**: Stick to frame touches for V1 interaction; avoid adding voice commands or other heavy interactions until proven necessary.
- **Latency Issues**: High-latency responses can break trust, so continuous monitoring of subtitle latency is crucial.

## Reality Guardrails

### Subtitle Latency
- **Real-time Subtitles**: Ensure subtitles are near-real-time to maintain trust and utility. Latencies over 50ms should be avoided as they may impair understanding.
  - *Reason*: Users expect immediate feedback, and delays can lead to cognitive dissonance.

### Battery and Thermal Management
- **Simple Hardware**: Early versions should use minimal hardware to avoid overheating or excessive power draw.
  - *Reason*: Complex sensors or advanced processing units could drain the battery quickly and cause thermal issues, especially in always-on scenarios.

### Confidence Display
- **Clear Feedback**: Provide clear confidence indicators to build trust. Subtle differences in label color or style can help convey different levels of certainty.
  - *Reason*: Clear feedback helps users understand the reliability of the information provided by the glasses.

### Privacy and Social Acceptability
- **Discreet UX**: Ensure that the design is discreet and non-intrusive to maintain social acceptability. Avoid overly conspicuous features.
  - *Reason*: A discreet approach reduces potential privacy concerns and makes the glasses more socially acceptable for everyday use.

## Conclusion

The current direction focuses on core mission goals while respecting real-world constraints such as battery, latency, and social acceptability. By prioritizing simple interactions and minimal hardware usage, the project can achieve its initial objectives effectively. Future iterations can build upon this foundation to address broader functionality and feature sets.
