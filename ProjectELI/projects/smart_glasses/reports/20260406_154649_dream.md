### Idea 4: Phone/Cloud Boundary

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By carefully defining what stays phone-local versus what falls back to cloud for live subtitle processing, we ensure that the glasses remain lightweight and socially acceptable while still providing high-quality real-time subtitles. This balance helps in maintaining a discreet and useful product without overburdening the on-glasses hardware.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific threshold for when the system should fall back to cloud processing for live subtitle generation. For instance, if the local environment's noise level exceeds a certain decibel (dB) value or if there is significant lag in real-time transcription, trigger a fallback to cloud processing.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This approach ensures that subtitles remain near-real-time and accurate even in challenging environments. It also reduces battery drain by offloading complex tasks like speech-to-text conversion when the local resources are strained. The wearer will benefit from clear and timely subtitles without compromising on their social acceptance or privacy concerns.

4. **Immediate next probe**

   Define one phone/cloud fallback boundary for live subtitle processing based on noise level, with a threshold of 75 dB triggering cloud-based transcription.
