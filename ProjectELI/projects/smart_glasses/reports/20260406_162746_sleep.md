# Consolidation of Key Signals for SmartGlasses V1

## Strengthening Signals
- **Subtitle Quality**: Emphasize real-time, accurate subtitles to reduce social friction and enhance understanding in noisy environments.
- **Memory Trust**: Ensure face/name recall is reliable with clear confidence indicators (e.g., labels + short reasons) to build trust.
- **Fast Lookup**: Prioritize quick memory access for new faces and names through recency-first caching policies on the phone.

## Weakening Signals
- **Complex Interactions**: Avoid adding voice commands or advanced multimodal UI in V1, focusing instead on simple frame-touch interactions.
- **Heavy On-Glasses Processing**: Reduce reliance on heavy local processing to minimize battery drain and thermal issues.

## Contradictions That Must Stay Alive
- **Social Acceptance vs. Utility**: Strive for a balance where the glasses are socially acceptable while providing real utility in noisy environments and memory-challenged situations.
- **Privacy vs. Functionality**: Ensure that memory policies respect privacy without compromising the functionality of face/name recall.

## Dormant Ideas Worth Reactivating
- **Subtitle Placement and Visual Hierarchy**: Re-examine subtitle placement to ensure they are prominent enough for easy reading but not overly distracting, especially in one-line support scenarios.
- **Memory Reinforcement**: Consider more frequent reinforcement techniques for new faces and names to build a strong memory foundation.

## Concrete Tensions Around Key Areas
### Subtitle Quality, Confidence Trust, Automatic New-Person Memory, Memory Reinforcement, Fast Lookup, Privacy, Latency, Battery, Visual UX

1. **Subtitle Quality vs. Real-Time Latency**:
   - The system must balance the need for real-time subtitles with minimal latency to ensure they are useful and not distracting.

2. **Confidence Trust vs. Memory Privacy**:
   - Develop a confidence display strategy that builds trust without compromising user privacy, ensuring that memory policies do not leak sensitive information.

3. **Automatic New-Person Memory vs. Fast Lookup**:
   - Implement a recency-first caching policy on the phone to balance fast memory access with limited local storage and processing power.

4. **Memory Reinforcement vs. User Acceptance**:
   - Reinforce new faces and names through frequent interactions, but ensure these do not become too intrusive or annoying.

5. **Fast Lookup vs. Battery Drain**:
   - Optimize the phone's caching and processing to provide fast memory access without draining the battery excessively.

6. **Battery and Thermals vs. Always-On Sensing**:
   - Minimize always-on sensing to reduce thermal and battery strain, ensuring that continuous operation is feasible within V1 constraints.

7. **Privacy vs. Rich Memory**:
   - Balance the need for rich memory with privacy concerns by implementing clear policies on how and when memories are stored and accessed.

8. **Visual UX vs. Low Social Friction**:
   - Design subtitles and one-line prompts in a way that minimizes social friction while still providing necessary assistance, ensuring they do not become too intrusive or distracting.

### Core Decision Areas

- **Subtitle Placement and Visual Hierarchy**: Optimize for clarity and minimal distraction.
- **Confidence Display**: Use labels + short reasons to build trust without overwhelming the user.
- **Memory Policy**: Implement recency-first caching on the phone for fast lookup of new faces/names.
- **Phone/Cloud Boundary**: Prioritize phone processing with cloud support only where clearly justified.
