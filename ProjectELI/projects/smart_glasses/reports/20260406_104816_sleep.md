# Consolidation of SmartGlasses Project Signals

## Strengthening Signals
- **Focus on Real-Time Subtitles:** Prioritize real-time subtitle assistance in noisy environments to ensure wearers can understand and respond better.
- **Memory Support for Names and Conversations:** Enhance personal memory support with a focus on remembering new faces and names effectively, providing one-line contextual prompts rather than overwhelming paragraphs.
- **Low-Friction UX:** Ensure the system remains assistive but non-intrusive, with interactions limited to frame touches in V1.

## Weakening Signals
- **Complexity of Interactions:** Avoid adding voice commands or complex interaction modes until after the touch-first V1 is validated.
- **Broad Feature Sprawl:** Focus on deepening core functionalities rather than expanding features that may distract from the primary mission.

## Contradictions That Must Stay Alive
- **Privacy vs. Usefulness:** Balance between providing useful assistance and respecting user privacy, ensuring transparency in data handling and usage.
- **Always-On Sensing vs. Battery Constraints:** Address trade-offs between continuous sensing for context awareness and battery management to ensure sustainable use.
- **Discreet UX vs. Visual Clarity:** Strive for a visual design that is both discreet enough not to be distracting but clear enough to provide effective assistance.

## Dormant Ideas Worth Reactivating
- **Confidence Representation:** Investigate how to best represent confidence in memory and responses, possibly through numeric scores or labels.
- **Subtitle Placement and Visual Hierarchy:** Explore optimal subtitle placement and visual hierarchy for fast reading while minimizing distraction.

## No Fluff
- **Mission Clarity:** Ensure all development aligns with the mission of creating a discreet, context-aware personal assistant focused on live subtitles, face/name recall, conversation memory, and one-line support.
- **Real-World Constraints:** Continuously surface hardware, battery, thermal, latency, privacy, and usability constraints to avoid wasting time on impractical optimizations.

## Core Mission
Create smart glasses that act as a discreet, context-aware personal assistant focused on live subtitles, face/name recall, conversation memory, and one-line support to help the wearer understand and respond better in real-world interactions.

## Non-negotiables
- The glasses should genuinely assist; do not pretend knowledge the user does not have.
- Early hardware remains simple: display text, record audio/visual input; phone/cloud handles heavy processing.
- V1 interaction is frame-touch only; avoid voice activation or spoken command control in this version.

## Core Attractors
- Real-time subtitle assistance in noisy environments.
- Personal memory support for names and prior conversations.
- One-line contextual prompts rather than overwhelming paragraphs.
- Low-friction human experience, being assistive but not deceptive.

## Core Tensions
- Privacy vs. usefulness.
- Always-on sensing vs. battery and thermal management.
- Local processing vs. cloud intelligence.
- Rich memory vs. fast lookup.
- Discreet UX vs. enough visual clarity.

## Drift Risks
- Avoid becoming a gimmick rather than a daily-use assistant.
- Refrain from broad feature sprawl before core subtitle/memory loop is validated.
- Prevent interaction sprawl that adds command modes or hardware complexity early on.
- Ensure low-latency responses to maintain trust.
- Store only relevant and valuable information.

## Why it Matters
The system should reduce social and cognitive friction, especially in noisy environments, hearing-challenged situations, memory-challenged situations, and dense multi-person contexts. It should help the wearer stay present, informed, and confident without pretending understanding.

## Evolution Rule
The project can evolve if such evolution strengthens the core mission, improves real-world usefulness, or increases trust, clarity, and assistive value.

## Reality-Check Rule
Surface early all hardware, battery, thermal, latency, privacy, and usability constraints so that time is not wasted on impractical optimizations.

## Open Questions
- What is the best V1 split between glasses, phone, and cloud?
- How should face/name memory be stored and reinforced for fast lookup and trust?
- How should confidence be represented: numeric score, label, or short reason?
- How should subtitles and one-line prompts coexist without overloading the wearer?
- What is the best subtitle placement and visual hierarchy for fast reading with low distraction?
- Which V1 features produce the highest daily-use value with the lowest complexity?
