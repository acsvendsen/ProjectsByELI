# Consolidation for SmartGlasses V1 Direction

## Strengthening Signals
- **Subtitle Quality**: Subtitles must feel near-real-time to maintain trust, and their placement should maximize readability without becoming distracting.
- **Face/Name Memory**: Face and name recognition should be reinforced with confidence objects over time, ensuring that memory is both fast and trusted.

## Weakening Signals
- **Complexity of Interaction**: Frame-touch interaction remains the primary method. Avoid adding voice activation or spoken command control until explicitly justified by future inputs.
- **On-Glasses Processing**: Early versions should rely heavily on phone processing for speech, context, and memory lookup to manage battery and thermal constraints.

## Contradictions That Must Stay Alive
- **Discreet UX**: The system must remain discreet and non-intrusive. Avoid features that might compromise the glasses' social acceptability.
- **User Understanding vs. Faked Understanding**: The system should help the wearer understand better, not pretend to have knowledge the wearer does not possess.

## Dormant Ideas Worth Reactivating
- **Confidence Display**: Explore different formats for confidence display—label only, label + short reason, or score + label—to find the best balance between trust and clarity.
- **Subtitle Placement**: Revisit subtitle placement options to ensure they are both useful and unobtrusive. Consider multiple scenarios where subtitles might be needed.

## Concrete Tensions Around Core Features
1. **Subtitle Quality vs. Battery Drain**:
   - **Core Value**: Near-real-time subtitles.
   - **Tension**: Achieving this without excessive battery drain.
   - **Current Approach**: Optimize subtitle rendering and processing to minimize energy use.

2. **Memory Trust vs. Privacy Perception**:
   - **Core Value**: Fast, accurate face/name memory.
   - **Tension**: Balancing the need for robust memory with privacy concerns.
   - **Current Approach**: Use confidence objects and reinforce memory over time while being transparent about data usage.

3. **Assistance Clarity vs. Social Friction**:
   - **Core Value**: Low-friction human experience.
   - **Tension**: Ensuring that the system is helpful without appearing intrusive or gimmicky.
   - **Current Approach**: Focus on subtle, assistive features and avoid overwhelming the user.

4. **Rich Context vs. Low-Latency Response**:
   - **Core Value**: Accurate contextual support.
   - **Tension**: Delivering fast responses while maintaining accuracy.
   - **Current Approach**: Prioritize real-time processing where feasible but allow for occasional cloud-based enhancements to ensure accuracy.

## No Fluff
- The system should continuously surface real-world limitations early, including hardware, battery, thermal, latency, privacy, and usability constraints. Ensure that any proposed changes do not waste time optimizing impossible or low-value directions.
- Avoid broad feature sprawl and unnecessary complexity in V1. Focus on deepening the core mission rather than distracting from it.

## Reality Rules
- **Mission Clarity**: Reiterate that the system should discreetly assist the wearer through live subtitles, face/name recall, conversation memory, and one-line support.
- **Interaction Constraints**: Maintain frame-touch interaction as the primary method for V1. Do not assume voice activation or spoken command control unless explicitly justified.

## Open Questions
- **Subtitle Placement and Visual Hierarchy**: Define the best subtitle placement options to ensure readability without distraction.
- **Confidence Format**: Determine the most effective way to display confidence levels—label only, label + short reason, or score + label—to build trust without overwhelming the user.

By addressing these signals, tensions, and open questions, we can ensure that V1 remains focused on delivering high-value, low-friction assistance while maintaining a strong commitment to real-world usability and social acceptability.
