# Reality Check for SmartGlasses Core Field

## Feasibility Assessment

### V1 Split Between Glasses, Phone, and Cloud
- **Feasible Now**: Given current hardware capabilities, it is feasible to have the glasses handle basic text display while offloading heavy processing such as audio/visual input analysis to a paired phone or cloud service. This split reduces the computational load on the glasses.
  
### Face/Name Memory Storage & Reinforcement
- **Feasible Later**: Implementing an effective memory system for names and faces will require significant research and development, especially in terms of fast lookup and user trust. Early prototypes could focus on basic face recognition with limited data storage.

### Confidence Representation
- **Likely Waste of Time**: Current hardware limitations may not support complex visual feedback systems. A simple numerical score or label (e.g., "high confidence," "low confidence") might be more feasible initially, with a short reason only as an optional enhancement for later versions.

### Subtitles & Prompts Coexistence
- **Feasible Now**: The coexistence of subtitles and one-line prompts can be managed by prioritizing critical information. Designing a clear visual hierarchy that prevents overloading the wearer is essential but can be tackled in V1 with simpler implementations.

### Highest Daily-Use Value Features
- **Feasible Now**: Focusing on real-time subtitle assistance, name recall, and brief context-based prompts are feasible given current technology. These features provide immediate value to users and align well with the core mission of reducing social and cognitive friction.

## Assumptions Needing Evidence

1. **Hardware & Processing Limits**:
   - **Assumption**: Current glasses can handle basic text display and interaction.
   - **Evidence Needed**: Prototyping with existing glasses to understand their limitations in terms of processing, power consumption, and thermal management.

2. **User Acceptance & Privacy Concerns**:
   - **Assumption**: Users will accept the glasses for daily use despite privacy concerns.
   - **Evidence Needed**: Conduct user surveys or focus groups to gauge initial acceptance levels and identify key privacy concerns.

3. **Battery Management**:
   - **Assumption**: The glasses can operate on a battery that allows for at least 8 hours of continuous use.
   - **Evidence Needed**: Develop a prototype with real-world usage scenarios to test battery life under various conditions.

4. **Thermal Management**:
   - **Assumption**: Glasses will remain within safe thermal limits during extended use.
   - **Evidence Needed**: Simulate prolonged usage in controlled environments to monitor temperature and ensure cooling mechanisms are adequate.

5. **Latency Constraints**:
   - **Assumption**: Latency from local processing vs. cloud-based solutions can be managed without significantly impacting user experience.
   - **Evidence Needed**: Compare latency of both approaches using mock data and real-world scenarios.

6. **Social Acceptability**:
   - **Assumption**: The glasses will not be viewed as intrusive or overly gimmicky by potential users.
   - **Evidence Needed**: Conduct social acceptability studies to assess how different demographics perceive the glasses in various social settings.

## Reality-Check Surfaces Early Constraints

### Hardware
- **Display Quality & Resolution**: Glasses with current displays may struggle with high-resolution text and video overlay, impacting usability.
- **Processing Capabilities**: Limited processing power on smartglasses means offloading most heavy lifting to a paired device or cloud service.

### Battery
- **Power Consumption**: Continuous operation requires careful management of battery life. Early versions might have shorter operational times due to limited power efficiency.

### Latency
- **Local vs Cloud Processing**: Balancing local and cloud processing involves understanding the trade-offs in terms of latency, which can significantly affect user experience.

### Privacy
- **Data Collection & Storage**: Implementing a system that collects personal data must be done transparently and with robust privacy controls to build trust.
- **Biometric Data Handling**: Storing biometric information (e.g., facial recognition data) raises significant ethical and legal questions. Initial prototypes should use only anonymized data.

### Usability
- **User Interface Design**: Ensuring the UI is clear, unobtrusive, and easy to navigate is crucial for daily adoption.
- **Real-Time Integration**: The system must integrate smoothly with real-world interactions to be effective without causing distraction or discomfort.

## Conclusion

The core mission of providing assistive technology in noisy environments and memory support appears feasible now but will require continuous refinement. Key areas like battery management, thermal design, and privacy concerns need more detailed investigation before full-scale development. Early prototypes and user feedback loops are critical for validating assumptions and addressing potential issues early.
