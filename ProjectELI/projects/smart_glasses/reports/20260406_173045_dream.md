1. **Why it benefits the core**

   Ensuring stable subtitle placement rules helps maintain a consistent and reliable user experience, reducing visual distraction and enhancing trust in the system's performance.    Define two subtitle placement rules: one for high-confidence content and another for lower-confidence content. This simple change ensures that critical information is always placed in a prominent, non-distracting area while less certain data can be relegated to a more peripheral location.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By implementing stable placement rules based on confidence levels, the system will reduce visual clutter during high-concentration moments and maintain clarity during lower-confidence situations, leading to improved overall user satisfaction and trust in the assistive functionality.

   Improving the local cache size threshold for face/name recall can enhance the system's ability to provide quick, accurate responses without relying heavily on cloud resources. This ensures that common interactions are handled efficiently, reducing latency and preserving battery life.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Define a specific recency rule that limits the amount of time before an entity is evicted from the local cache. For example, if a person's name or face was seen more than 10 minutes ago, they should be automatically removed from the local cache to make space for more recent interactions.

3. **Likely payoff**

   By setting a clear recency threshold, the system can maintain a smaller but more relevant cache of recently encountered individuals, leading to faster and more accurate face/name recall responses. This improves the overall user experience by reducing latency and ensuring that the wearer receives timely assistance with minimal delays.

4. **Immediate next probe**

3. **Likely payoff**
