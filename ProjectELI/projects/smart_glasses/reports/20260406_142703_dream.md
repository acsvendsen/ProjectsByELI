### Idea 1: Subtitle Placement

1. **Why it benefits the core**
   Ensuring stable subtitle placement rules helps maintain a consistent user experience, reducing cognitive load and making the system more reliable. This is crucial for real-time interaction support, especially in noisy environments where clarity is paramount.

2. **What small change unlocks it**
   Define two fixed subtitle placement modes: one for high-confidence scenarios (e.g., familiar faces or topics) and another for low-confidence scenarios (e.g., new people or less certain contexts).

3. **Likely payoff**
   By having predefined placement rules, the system can quickly determine where subtitles should appear without needing to reactively adjust their position. This leads to faster subtitle delivery during conversations, enhancing the wearer's understanding of the dialogue.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Define one fixed subtitle position rule for V1: keep subtitles at the bottom center of the display.

### Idea 2: Confidence Display

1. **Why it benefits the core**

   By clearly defining confidence labels and short reasons for face and name recall, we can build trust through transparent signaling of certainty levels. This improves the wearer's confidence in the system's accuracy and reliability.

2. **What small change unlocks it**

   Introduce a simple confidence format that combines a numeric score with a label and a short reason. For example: "score 0.84 + 'High' + 'Seen recently'; score 0.42 + 'Low' + 'Weak match'; score 0.63 + 'Medium' + 'Name heard once in introduction'.

3. **Likely payoff**

   This format will help the wearer understand the reliability of the system's memory and face/name recall, leading to higher trust and better use of the assistive features. It also simplifies communication between glasses and phone for confidence updates.

4. **Immediate next probe**
   - Choose one confidence display format for V1: label only or label plus short reason.
